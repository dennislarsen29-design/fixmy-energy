Add a Tesla Powerwall to Existing Solar

If you already have solar, adding a battery can give you backup power and shift more of your solar use into the evening. Whether it pays off depends on your rate plan and how you use power.

Solar Review · Updated October 2026

How it works with existing panels

The Tesla Powerwall 3 stores 13.5 kWh and has a built-in solar inverter, so on many systems your existing panels connect to it directly. Older systems with their own inverter can also be paired with a battery. We check your equipment before recommending which approach fits.

Your net metering version matters

Homes on older net metering (NEM 1.0 or 2.0) generally keep that status when a battery is added, which is a big reason to look at storage rather than a new solar contract. Adding more panels can change that, so we check the rules before any expansion. Newer net billing plans value evening power more, which is where a battery helps most.

Possible incentives

Some utilities and community choice programs offer battery incentives. In San Diego, the community choice program offers storage incentives for eligible homes. Eligibility depends on your address, usage and program rules, and we confirm it before you decide.

What we check first

  • Your utility bill and rate plan.
  • Your solar production history and inverter.
  • Your electrical panel and where a battery can go.
  • Your net metering status and what it allows.

Not everyone needs one

If your bill is already very low and you rarely lose power, a battery may not be worth it, and we will say so. The free evaluation tells you where you stand.

Common questions

Can I add a Powerwall to solar panels I already have?

In most cases yes. We check your inverter, panel and rate plan to choose the right setup.

Will adding a battery change my net metering?

Adding a battery generally does not change it, but adding panels can. We check the rules before any expansion.

How big is a Powerwall 3?

It stores 13.5 kWh. Some homes use more than one.
