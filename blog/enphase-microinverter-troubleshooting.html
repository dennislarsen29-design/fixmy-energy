Enphase Microinverter Problems Explained

Enphase systems report production per-panel through the Envoy gateway and Enlighten app, which makes small problems easy to spot — if you know what you're looking at. Here's what the most common warnings mean.

Solar Review · Serving San Diego & Southern California · Updated July 2026

Unlike a single central inverter, an Enphase system uses a small microinverter behind every panel, all reporting back to a home gateway called the Envoy. That per-panel visibility is one of Enphase's biggest advantages — a single failing panel shows up clearly instead of hiding inside a whole-system production number. Here's how to read the warnings.

Safety first: if the Enlighten app or Envoy shows a DC Resistance Low warning, that's flagging a real electrical safety condition, not a cosmetic glitch. Have it inspected rather than dismissing it.

Common Enphase issues

DC Resistance Low

This warning means a microinverter measured the insulation resistance between the DC wiring and ground below its safety threshold (roughly 7 kilohms). Typical causes: moisture getting into a connector after rain, a cracked panel junction box, or cable insulation wearing through over time. It's the Enphase equivalent of an isolation fault and should be inspected — it often gets worse, not better, on its own.

Panel shows "offline" in Enlighten

A single panel going offline usually means that specific microinverter lost communication or stopped reporting — it doesn't necessarily mean the panel glass itself is damaged. Causes range from a loose connector at that panel to a failed microinverter. Because Enphase reports per-panel, this is one of the easier issues to catch early, before it costs you much production.

Envoy offline

The Envoy is the gateway collecting data from every microinverter and relaying it to the Enlighten app. If the Envoy itself goes offline, you lose visibility into your production data — even though the microinverters may keep making power in the background. The most common cause is a home WiFi or network outage; a brief blip after a router reboot or power outage is normal. A persistent offline Envoy after your network is confirmed working may need attention.

Sudden drop in total production

If your overall kWh output drops noticeably without a single panel showing offline, check for shading changes (new tree growth, a neighbor's addition), a dirty array, or a partial string issue. Comparing this month's production to the same month last year in the Enlighten app is the fastest way to spot a real decline versus normal seasonal variation.

What to check before you call

  1. Confirm your home WiFi/network is working — this rules out the most common Envoy-offline cause.
  2. Note which specific panel(s) show offline in the Enlighten app, and for how long.
  3. Screenshot any DC Resistance Low warning — the details (which microinverter, the measured value) speed up diagnosis.
  4. Compare production year-over-year in the app to separate a real problem from normal seasonal change.

Frequently asked questions

What does "DC Resistance Low" mean on Enphase?

The insulation resistance between the DC circuit and ground measured below the safety threshold (~7 kΩ) — usually moisture, a damaged junction box, or worn cable insulation. Worth inspecting rather than ignoring.

Why does my Enlighten app show a panel as offline?

Usually a communication dropout or wiring issue at that specific microinverter, not necessarily physical damage to the panel. Enphase's per-panel reporting catches this early.

What does it mean when my Envoy is offline?

You've lost visibility into production data, typically from a home network or power interruption. Microinverters may keep producing even while the Envoy itself is offline.

Do I need a technician for an Enphase issue?

A brief Envoy blip after an outage is normal. A persistent DC Resistance Low warning, a panel offline for days, or a real production drop are worth a professional diagnosis.
