Is Vitl Power the Same as Titan Solar? Here's What It Actually Is

Short answer: no, they're not the same company, and we found nothing connecting the two. But if you're searching for this, you're probably trying to figure out who's actually responsible for your solar system — so here's what Vitl Power really is, and what to check.

Solar Review · Serving San Diego & Southern California · Updated September 2026

Is Vitl Power the same as Titan Solar Power?

No. Vitl Power and Titan Solar Power are two different companies. Titan Solar Power filed Chapter 7 bankruptcy and shut down entirely in June 2024 — it no longer exists in any operating form (see our full Titan Solar breakdown here). Vitl Power, by contrast, is founded in 2019, headquartered in Lehi, Utah, and — as far as we could verify — still operating today. We found no public record of a merger, rebrand, or successor relationship between the two. If you searched "Vitl Power" hoping to find out what happened to Titan, the honest answer is: these are unrelated companies, and you're likely being served both names because they come up in similar searches from homeowners trying to track down who's responsible for their system.

So what is Vitl Power?

Vitl Power isn't a traditional local solar installer with a permanent office in your area. It runs on a traveling sales model: the company dispatches an organized sales team to a region for roughly ten days a month, signs up customers, and then moves on to the next area. It operates this way across dozens of states rather than staying rooted in one market.

That model matters for one practical reason: Vitl Power's own sales team typically isn't local, and isn't the crew that installs your system. Installation is handled by a separate contractor. Freedom Forever is one installer known to have carried out installs sold through Vitl Power — but which company actually built your specific system depends on your contract, and is worth confirming directly from your paperwork.

Worth knowing: Freedom Forever — one of the installers that has worked with Vitl Power — filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy in April 2026. If your system was sold through Vitl Power and installed by Freedom Forever, the installer of record may no longer be reliably reachable, even though Vitl Power itself is a separate matter.

Why do people search "Vitl Power lawsuit"?

We looked for a confirmed lawsuit against Vitl Power and did not find one at the time of writing. What we did find, on the Better Business Bureau, is a real pattern of customer complaints — mostly about slow or unresponsive communication after the contract is signed, and about being pointed back and forth between Vitl Power's sales side and the installer handling the physical work. That's a genuine service-gap problem, but it's a different thing from an actual filed lawsuit, and we don't want to overstate what's out there.

What to check if Vitl Power sold your system

  1. Pull your original contract and identify the actual installing contractor — the name on your permit paperwork is the company that did the physical work, not necessarily Vitl Power.
  2. If that installer is Freedom Forever, or another company that has since closed, treat your installer as unreachable and plan accordingly.
  3. Confirm your panel and inverter manufacturer warranties directly with the manufacturer — those typically survive regardless of what happens to the sales company or installer.
