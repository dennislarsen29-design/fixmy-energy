Sunworks and Solcius Shut Down: What Owners Should Do

Sunworks, including its Solcius subsidiary, filed Chapter 7 liquidation in February 2024 and stopped operating. Here is what that means for the system on your roof.

Solar Review · Published November 2026

Is Sunworks still in business?

No. Sunworks filed Chapter 7 liquidation in February 2024. Chapter 7 means the company stopped operating rather than reorganizing, so there is no Sunworks support line to call. Solcius, which Sunworks bought in 2021 and which installed systems from its Southern California offices, went down with it. Your permit or contract may show either name depending on when the system went in.

Will my panels stop working?

No. The liquidation did not touch the hardware on your roof. Your panels, inverter and any battery keep running exactly as before. What disappeared is the company that would have answered service calls, watched your monitoring and handled warranty claims.

Is my warranty still good?

  • Manufacturer warranties on the panels, inverter and battery come from the equipment brands, not Sunworks or Solcius, so they usually still apply.
  • The installer's workmanship warranty, which covered the labor and roof work, ended with the company.

The catch with a manufacturer warranty is that someone has to diagnose the problem, document it and file the claim. That used to be the installer's job. Now it needs a new service company.

How to confirm what is on your roof

Before anything else, find out exactly what you own. Take clear photos of four things: the label on the inverter (brand, model and serial number), a label on the back of one panel if you can see it, the sticker on your main service panel, and a screenshot of your monitoring app. Those photos tell a technician which manufacturer warranty applies and which fault codes to look up. Keep them in one folder with your original contract and any permit paperwork.

Signs your system may not be producing

  • Your electric bill has gone up without a change in how you use power.
  • The monitoring app shows zero, a flat line, or has gone offline.
  • This month's production is well below the same month last year.
  • The inverter shows a red or flashing light or an error code. Our guides to SolarEdge error codes and Enphase microinverter problems explain the common ones.

Any one of these is worth a closer look. A system can quietly stop producing for months before anyone notices, and every month offline is power you paid for and did not get.
