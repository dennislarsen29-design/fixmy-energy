Titan Solar Power Liquidated — What Happens to My System?

Titan Solar Power filed Chapter 7 bankruptcy and shut down completely in June 2024. Unlike some other solar bankruptcies, there's no successor company running the business today. Here's what that means for your panels, warranty, and monitoring.

Solar Review · Serving San Diego & Southern California · Updated July 2026

Titan Solar Power was one of the largest privately-held solar installers in the country, operating in 22 states with over 150,000 customers. Its Chapter 7 liquidation in June 2024 was more abrupt than most — Chapter 7 means the company stopped operating entirely, rather than reorganizing and continuing under a new structure (which is what happened with some other solar bankruptcies). If Titan installed your system, there is genuinely no one left at that company to call.

Will my panels stop working?

No. Nothing about Titan's liquidation affects the physical hardware on your roof. Your panels, inverter, and any battery continue producing electricity exactly as before. The liquidation only removed the company that would have handled service calls, monitoring, and warranty claims.

Is my Titan Solar warranty still good?

It depends which part of the warranty you mean:

  • Panel and inverter manufacturer warranties (from brands like Trina, Canadian Solar, SMA, or SolarEdge) are backed by the manufacturer, not Titan, so they typically remain valid.
  • Titan's own workmanship warranty — covering the installation labor, roof penetrations, and any Titan-specific service promises — is gone. There is no entity left to honor it, since Chapter 7 liquidation means the company no longer exists in any operating form.

What about monitoring?

Whatever monitoring platform your inverter uses (often tied to the inverter manufacturer rather than Titan directly) may still function, but there's no company checking alerts on your behalf anymore. If you haven't looked at your production data in a while, or your electric bill has crept up, it's worth confirming the system is still performing as expected.

What to do now

  1. Check your system's production via the inverter manufacturer's app or your utility bills.
  2. Locate your original paperwork — note the panel and inverter brands specifically.
  3. If you financed the system, keep paying the loan — that servicer operates independently of Titan.
  4. Book a free independent evaluation to confirm system health and protect your NEM rate.
