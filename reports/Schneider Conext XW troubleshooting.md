# Read the Conext XW+ before you touch it

You are looking at a battery-based hybrid system: the **Conext XW+ is an inverter/charger tied to a battery bank, not a normal grid-tie string inverter**, and the **MPPT 80 600** is the solar charge controller that feeds the bank. The **PDP** is the breaker enclosure around them, and the **Battery Monitor** is the meter that estimates battery state of charge. Schneider has discontinued the XW+ in favor of the XW Pro, and the MPPT 80 600 is on its US discontinued list, so expect an aging, probably out-of-warranty system with limited support ([XW+ page](https://solar.se.com/us/?p=17624), [US discontinued products](https://solar.se.com/us/en/discontinued-products)). On a first evaluation the job is to **read, photograph and ask, not to open or repair anything**: the most useful evidence is the fault history on the display, the battery chemistry and age, and what the homeowner says happened. The faults that matter most are overload (F63-65), over-temperature (F57/F58/F45/F68), DC voltage (F47-49), welded relays (F17-22), and the grid events F23-40, which are not equipment defects. The realistic upgrade options are a like-for-like XW Pro swap, or replacing the architecture with a Powerwall 3. Whether existing panel strings can land on a Powerwall 3 is unverified, and the effect on the customer's NEM status of removing the XW+ and MPPT is unconfirmed, so ask SDG&E before anyone decommissions equipment.

## One-page checklist for tomorrow morning

**Source confidence.** Nearly every fault code below comes from one primary source, the XW+ Owner's Guide Rev H (Nov 2017), which was downloaded and read in full ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)). The Schneider site was unreachable from the research environment, so the newer Rev I, the Service Guide, and the MPPT, Battery Monitor and PDP manuals were not read. Items marked (snippet) come from search-result summaries and (forum) from anecdotal threads. Treat those as leads to confirm.

**Before you touch anything**
- Do not open the inverter, PDP, DC breakers or the battery bank. The guide says there are no user-serviceable parts, internal capacitors stay charged after power is removed, and Standby does not make the unit safe to open ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)).
- Do not open the battery DC disconnect while the inverter is running. Disconnecting batteries during operation can trigger an F49 DC over-voltage fault ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)).
- If the bank is flooded lead-acid, expect hydrogen and acid. Check for ventilation and keep ignition sources away ([Portlandia guide](https://www.portlandiaelectric.supply/pages/battery-installation-guide)). A partly discharged bank can still sustain a damaging DC arc that fuses may not clear ([Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2019/12/battery-energy-storage-systems-are-at-increasing-risk-for-arc-flash-hazards/)).
- Read first, shut down only if there is a reason. If you must stop the unit, the manual's method is to hold STARTUP/SHUTDOWN for 5 seconds; it cannot be cancelled, and you restart by pressing it again once the display is blank. Hold STARTUP/SHUTDOWN and Equalize together for about 5 seconds for Standby, where AC passthrough stops but the unit stays on the network ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)). Full de-energizing (AC off, DC off, lockout, wait for capacitors) is a planned-service step, not a first-visit one. The manual gives no capacitor wait time in the text read.

**Photograph (in this order)**
- Every nameplate: XW+, MPPT 80 600, PDP, Battery Monitor, and each battery.
- The inverter's red Event LED, the AC1/AC2 LEDs and the 3-character display as found. The red LED is solid for a fault and flashing for a warning ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)).
- If a Conext System Control Panel (SCP) is installed: the current screen, the Fault Log and the Warning Log (the last 20 of each are stored), the Meters screens, and the battery-type, charge and grid-support settings pages.
- The MPPT's own display, the Battery Monitor screen, and the PDP breaker labels showing which are on and off.
- The battery bank: terminals, case swelling, corrosion, electrolyte level, date codes, ventilation. Also the cable runs and lugs, looking for heat discoloration, and the inverter's bottom air filter and vents.

**Ask the homeowner**
- When did it stop working, and what happened first (outage, loud noise, breaker trip, fault code)?
- Battery type, brand and age. Has it ever been watered or replaced?
- Is the house grid-tied with backup, or off-grid? Does it still export or produce?
- Who installed it, in what year, and what is the interconnection/PTO date and NEM status?
- Is there a generator? Has anything been added recently (loads, panels)?

**Codes that matter** (Owner's Guide Rev H, verified)

| Code | Meaning | First thing to check |
|---|---|---|
| F63 / F64 / F65 | AC overload; becomes manual if it happens 3 times in 5 minutes | Excess load; clearing needs a 5-second power-button hold, then 20 seconds disconnected from the batteries |
| F57 / F58 / F45 / F68 | Over-temperature (FETs, capacitor, transformer) | Fan, blocked foam filter and vents, ambient heat, sustained load |
| F47 / F48 | DC under voltage (below 32 V, or below the low-cutout setting) | Battery voltage at the inverter terminals, external DC loads |
| F49 | DC over voltage | Other charge sources, cutout setting, batteries disconnected while running |
| F17-F22 | Welded transfer relays | Service required, unless an AC source is wired to the output |
| F23-F40 | Grid anti-islanding events | A utility or wiring problem, not a unit defect; the unit reconnects after a 5-minute timer |
| W97 | Battery temperature sensor failure | Replace the sensor |
| W500 | Lost network connection | Xanbus cables and terminators |
| F46, F52-F56, F67, F500 | Controller, memory, watchdog, silicon ID | Service required |

For the MPPT, only a few codes could be tied to a source (snippet): F74 is PV input over 600 VDC and needs a manual clear, F75 is fan over-voltage, and F2/F5 are over-temperature ([Owner's Guide 990-6214B](https://sunwatts.com/content/manual/Schneider_User_Manual_Conext_600V_Charge_Controller.pdf), [MPPT manual](https://www.solaris-shop.com/content/MMPT80%20Manual.pdf)). The full MPPT list and LED meanings were not retrieved.

## What the XW+ is telling you: reading faults without guessing

The manual sorts events into warnings and faults. Warnings clear themselves or ask a yes/no question. Faults are automatic, manual (cleared from the SCP menu or by briefly pressing STARTUP/SHUTDOWN), or **escalating**, meaning an automatic fault becomes manual if it repeats ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)). That matters for your visit: a unit that is sitting in a manual fault has stopped and is waiting for a person, so a fault light on arrival may be a stale event rather than a live failure. Record the code and the conditions before pressing anything, because a brief press of STARTUP/SHUTDOWN clears active events and removes your evidence from the front panel. The SCP logs still keep history, which is why the SCP photos come first.

Two code families are easy to misread. **F23-F40 are anti-islanding trips**, where the grid went out of voltage or frequency range; the manual says the Fault light shows with no code on the 3-character display, the SCP shows the message, they cannot be cleared by hand, and the unit waits at least 5 minutes after the grid returns. For repeated trips at high sell current, the guide points to a high-impedance connection or undersized wiring between the XW+ and the meter and suggests sizing wiring for 1 to 1.5% voltage drop ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)). Second, the **over-temperature codes** most often come down to airflow and load: the guide lists a failed fan, blocked intake, high ambient and an oversized inverting load as causes, and says to replace the foam air filter on the unit's underside when it clogs. Forum owners report bad thermistors and failed fans in some cases, but one of those threads concerns the Conext SW, an adjacent product (forum, [thread](https://forum.solar-electric.com/discussion/comment/420948)).

Some failures never show a code. Per the manual, a unit with no LEDs is usually shut off by the button or lacks proper DC at its terminals. A flashing INVERT light with no output means the load is too small for Search mode. A charger that will not charge is often blocked by a setting (charger block window, load shaving) or by AC the unit refuses to qualify. Forum reports add hardware patterns from the related XW4548: a failing current sensor causing false F63, welded relays in early units, and power-board failures that are not repairable in the field (forum, snippet-level, [thread](https://forum.solar-electric.com/discussion/comment/427947)). Treat those as reasons to expect "replace the unit" rather than a quick fix.

The rest of the system fails in quieter ways. If the MPPT shows no production, the first checks are general practice rather than a Schneider table: measure PV volts at the controller input and battery volts at its output, check the PV and battery breakers in the PDP, and confirm the controller appears on the network. The PDP ships with a single 250 A battery breaker, and the MPPT needs extra breakers in its expansion positions, so an off or tripped breaker there produces zero production with no fault at all (snippet, [PDP guide](https://solar.se.com/eu/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2021/10/XW-PDP-Installation-Guide_975-0709-01-01_ENG.pdf)); the breaker ratings conflict between Schneider documents, so do not rely on a rating from memory ([Mini PDP guide](https://solar.se.com/us/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2021/10/XW-Mini-PDP-Installation-Guide_975-0735-01-01_ENG.pdf) vs [datasheet](https://backwoodssolar.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/ds20171117_conext-xw-pdp-datasheet_2.pdf)). A forum report of MPPTs showing -273 degrees battery temperature suggests the temperature data is not arriving rather than a real reading (forum, [thread](https://forum.solar-electric.com/discussion/comment/413400); the reading is the forum's, the interpretation is an inference). Communication trouble, such as a control panel that powers up but shows no devices, points to the Xanbus network, which needs a terminator at each end and known-good Cat5e cable ([Schneider FAQ](https://www.se.com/ca/en/faqs/FA337931/)). On the Battery Monitor, only its existence, a SYNCHRONIZE control and a reported lithium incompatibility could be found, so wrong state-of-charge readings should be treated as a settings and calibration question to resolve from its manual.

## Battery, inverter or wiring: how to decide on site

The research found **no sourced voltage or state-of-charge thresholds for a dead bank**, so the call comes from comparing evidence, not from a lookup table. Read the SCP fault history before anything else. The battery is the likely culprit when you see low-voltage faults under load, voltage collapsing when loads start, a bank at rest well below nominal, swollen, leaking or corroded cases, or an erratic Battery Monitor reading. The inverter or MPPT is the likelier culprit when the bank looks healthy but the unit throws hardware or temperature faults, will not sync to the grid, or the MPPT faults with healthy PV voltage. Wiring is the third suspect: look for heat discoloration at lugs and loose or corroded connections, which the manual itself lists among causes of low charge current ([Owner's Guide Rev H](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ecodirect_docs/SCHNEIDER/Conext-XW/Schneider-Electric-Conext-XW-NA-Owners-Guide-975-0240-01-01_Rev-H_ENG-ML201711.pdf)). These are installer-practice inferences, not Schneider guidance.

The most useful single measurement is a calibrated meter reading at the bank compared with what the Battery Monitor and SCP report, taken without opening anything. That requires DC-rated tools and a second person, and if the reading requires reaching into the bank it should wait for a planned service visit. Note also that a Battery Monitor that is wrong can make a healthy bank look bad, which is why the bank voltage at the terminals is the tiebreaker.

## Upgrade paths: like-for-like swap or move to Powerwall 3

**Support picture.** Schneider's December 2020 notice (for the EU 230 V XW8548) says the XW+ is being replaced by the XW Pro and that existing warranties will still be honored; the US 120/240 V page also marks the XW+ discontinued ([discontinuation notice](https://solar.se.com/eu/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2021/09/201216_XW8548_-Product-Discontinuation-Notice_IEC.pdf), [XW+ page](https://solar.se.com/us/?p=17624)). The XW+ base warranty term was not verified, and one customer review claims the warranty department does not take calls (anecdotal, [review](https://www.solarreviews.com/view-review/?reviewid=46810)). The reasonable working assumption is an out-of-warranty unit whose repair depends on distributor stock and Schneider service. Some owners on a forum believe the whole XW family is being sunset (forum opinion, [thread](https://forum.solar-electric.com/discussion/comment/428671)); whether the XW Pro itself is being wound down is unconfirmed, so a like-for-like swap carries support risk too.

**Three options.** The like-for-like path is an XW Pro with the existing battery and controller architecture, which keeps the PV working but buys into a family with an uncertain future. The second path is a Powerwall 3 with the old PV strings landing on it. Tesla's public docs prefer DC-coupled solar on Powerwall 3, but one installer page's claim that existing strings can land on its six MPPT inputs is marketing and unverified, and Tesla's own statement about legacy DC strings was not retrieved ([Tesla AC-coupled sizing page](https://energylibrary.tesla.com/docs/Public/EnergyStorage/Powerwall/3/SystemDesign/en-us/GUID-23D242D1-8D65-47B9-9118-57002FFD84D2.html), snippet; [Solar SME](https://sites.google.com/view/solar-sme/home/Tesla-Powerwall-3-Installation)). String voltage and current compatibility with the MPPT 80 600's PV inputs would need a Tesla design check before quoting. The third path is a conventional grid-tie inverter for the PV with an AC-coupled Powerwall 3, which is the common retrofit pattern for existing solar ([SolarQuotes](https://support.solarquotes.com.au/hc/en-us/articles/13622321554575-Can-I-use-a-Tesla-Powerwall-3-to-add-a-battery-to-my-existing-Enphase-SolarEdge-or-other-solar)). Aurora notes that DC-coupled batteries generally require replacing the existing inverter and suit new installs better than retrofits ([Aurora Solar](https://aurorasolar.com/home-solar/blog/energy-101/add-battery-existing-solar-system)). Which of these applies to your customer depends on whether the system is a grid-tied house with backup or a true off-grid house, which is why that is the first question to ask.

**NEM and interconnection.** California NEM 1.0 and 2.0 customers can generally add a battery without losing legacy status ([CALSSA](https://calssa.org/nem-3-faq)), and SDG&E systems with an interconnection application before April 15, 2023 stay on NEM 2.0 ([SDCP program manual](https://sdcommunitypower.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Solar-Battery-Savings-Program-Manual.pdf)). The risk is panel or size expansion: it is capped at the greater of 10% of original capacity or 1 kW in the PG&E wording, with other utilities reportedly treating inverter-limited systems differently ([CALSSA](https://calssa.org/system-size-calculation)). The utilities have contested whether the storage exemption holds when a solar inverter is replaced with a larger shared one (outcome not found). Whether removing an XW+ and MPPT and installing a different architecture counts as a repair or a new interconnection application is **not confirmed**. Preserve the original PTO and application paperwork, ask SDG&E in writing before decommissioning anything, and do not promise the customer a NEM outcome. If the house is off-grid, none of the NEM material applies, and that case was not researched.

## What to carry out of this research

The most practical takeaway is that this visit should end with a complete evidence package and a clear fork, not a diagnosis. Fault history, battery age and chemistry, and the grid-tied-versus-off-grid answer decide most of what happens next: a healthy bank with hardware faults points toward replacing the inverter side, a tired bank points toward replacing the battery, and an old bank plus an aging, discontinued inverter makes the Powerwall 3 conversation natural.

The gaps to close, in priority order: (1) read the SCP Fault Log on site, because no code table can replace it; (2) confirm with Tesla whether this system's PV strings can land on a Powerwall 3 and with SDG&E whether the swap triggers a new interconnection; (3) get the XW+ Service Guide (977-0017-01-01) and the MPPT 80 600 Owner's Guide, which were not read; (4) check the nameplate's revision and warranty status with Schneider, since neither the XW+ warranty term nor the support phone number was verified. The one important anchor on the heavy side: everything labeled installer practice here, including the shutdown order and the battery-versus-inverter heuristics, is judgment rather than documented Schneider procedure, so defer to the manual and your company's energized-work rules over this report.
