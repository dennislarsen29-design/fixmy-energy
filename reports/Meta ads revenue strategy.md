# Fix follow-up first, then test Powerwalls

The proposed Freedom Forever "never got permission to operate" ad is **not the best second ad**; the evidence favors a Powerwall-retrofit / backup-power ad for existing-solar owners, with the Freedom Forever ad kept as a small, capped third test. The Freedom Forever story is real and fresh: the company filed Chapter 11 on April 15, 2026 and converted to Chapter 7 liquidation effective August 7, 2026 ([pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/09/Freedom-Forever-Bankruptcy-Converted-To-Chapter-7-Liquidation-Key-Dates-Set/)). But no researcher found a count of homes stuck without PTO in San Diego, California or the nation, no source frames the problem as PTO-specific, and the court gave lenders (GoodLeap is the one confirmed by name), not independent service companies, the explicit right to finish stranded installs. At $25 a day, Meta cannot target "Freedom Forever customers," so a niche message would burn most impressions on people it does not describe. The larger lever is operational: the business's own cold list produced about 14 appointments from roughly 9,000 leads, and inbound ad leads only pay off if one owner answers in minutes, confirms the visit and shows up to a funnel that sells beyond the $300-500 diagnostic. On the notes' own arithmetic, $750 a month of ads is roughly break-even before the owner's time. The plan below therefore spends the first week fixing response speed, consent and tracking, then tests Powerwall/backup against the existing orphaned-installer ad, and only then risks a capped Freedom Forever test. Almost every external figure comes from search-result summaries and vendor blogs, not primary documents, so treat the numbers as directional and verify the items in the final table before spending more.

## How much of this is evidence, and how much is reasoning

All five researchers lost the ability to open full pages (DNS failures on every fetch), so nothing was cross-checked against court dockets, CSLB orders, Meta policy text, SDCP documents or the original benchmark reports. Three kinds of material follow and should be read differently. Sourced facts carry a link, but even those usually rest on a search summary. Assumptions are numbers the notes chose, such as a 65% show rate, a $350 gross profit per shown evaluation and a 20% lead-to-booked rate. My own reasoning is everything described as "I infer" or "my judgment." One gap sits underneath all of it: **there is no performance data from the first live ad** (cost per lead, click-through, booked appointments) and none of the owner's own show, diagnostic-attach or close rates. Those numbers dominate the answer, so the plan is built to produce them.

## Why Freedom Forever PTO fails as the second ad, and how to run it anyway

The case for the ad is urgency and scarcity of competing messaging. Freedom Forever owed more than $500M, including $114M to Mosaic ([Law360](https://law360.com/articles/2465664)); the Chapter 7 trustee is Alfred T. Giuliano and the proof-of-claim deadline (October 16, 2026) has already passed ([pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/09/Freedom-Forever-Bankruptcy-Converted-To-Chapter-7-Liquidation-Key-Dates-Set/)). Colorado is the only place with a customer count: more than 400 homeowners losing company support ([Denver Gazette](https://daily.denvergazette.com/article/281573772511285)). Competitors such as 8MSolar and NRG Clean Power already publish Freedom Forever advice for search ([8MSolar](https://8msolar.com/?p=20648), [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/freedom-forever-files-for-bankruptcy/)), so Google is probably contested; Meta saturation is unchecked.

Four things count against it as the second ad. First, size: the company reported 150,000+ installs nationally, but the PTO-stuck San Diego subset is unmeasured, and the ad-angles researcher's "hundreds to low thousands" is a guess. Second, reach: Meta has removed detailed-targeting exclusions and consolidated interests, leaving location and minimum age as the hard constraints ([Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/metas-advantage-audience/), [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-removal-of-detailed-targeting/)), so the only way to find these homeowners is creative that self-selects. As an illustration only (my arithmetic, assumed inputs): at a lead-gen CPM of about $25-40 ([Affect Group via search summary, per the benchmarks note]), $25 a day buys roughly 600-1,000 impressions daily, around 20-30k a month spread across a county of millions; if stuck Freedom Forever owners were even a few hundredths of a percent of viewers, the month would reach a few dozen of them at most. Third, remedy control: the stay relief went to financing partners, and the ad-angles note lists Credit Human, EnFin, EverBright, GoodLeap, Participate, Project Solar and Sunrun, while the market note could confirm only GoodLeap, so who controls completion is unsettled. I infer a licensed partner would need homeowner authorization and probably lender consent to take over a permit, inspection and interconnection, and whether SDG&E accepts a new contractor of record is unverified. Fourth, ticket: it is unclear who pays a completion contractor, the homeowner or the lender.

Run it anyway, but as a gated test, because the downside is small and the competition for the message is thin. Before spending, confirm three things: that SDG&E and the lender will let Cosmic act as completer, that Cosmic holds the right license, and that one or two real stuck homeowners exist (inbound calls, the existing `/freedom-forever` page traffic, Facebook groups, which no researcher could search). Then give it a fixed $100 and require at least one genuine San Diego PTO-stuck inquiry to continue. Copy should describe the situation ("Solar installed but never turned on?") and promise a free look at what is missing, never legal advice, claims-process help or a promise that PTO will be granted.

## Powerwall retrofit has the best ticket, but the arithmetic is thin

The ad-angles researcher ranks the Powerwall retrofit for existing-solar owners first on expected revenue per ad dollar because of margin per job and a stackable incentive, then the free solar health check, then retargeting and reviews, SunPower-specific copy, and Freedom Forever PTO, with lookalikes last. That is reasoned from vendor benchmarks, not measured, and I agree with the order for a specific reason: the funnel note's break-even math shows the diagnostic alone cannot pay for Meta leads. A diagnostic with a $100 margin on a $400 fee supports only about $20-33 per booked appointment, a $2,000-margin battery at a 15% close supports about $195, and the blended example (30% buy a diagnostic, 8% a battery, 2% new solar) yields **$350 gross profit per shown evaluation**, or about $227 per booked appointment at a 65% show rate. Every input there is an assumption, and the owner's real rates could move the answer several-fold.

One caution on the Powerwall ad. Its strongest hook, the SDCP rebate, is also its riskiest claim, and the rebate stack is murky (see the conflicts table). Lead with backup power, the 4-9 pm peak window and "what a battery would do on your system," and let the rebate come out in the human conversation.

## A 30-day plan at $25 a day

The binding constraints are owner time and learning volume, not creative ideas. At 2-4 results a week, Meta's roughly 50-events-per-ad-set-per-week learning threshold ([SaveMyLeads](https://www.savemyleads.com/blog/other/meta-ads-learning-phase)) is unreachable on Schedule, which the benchmarks note estimates would need $350-700 a day. So use one campaign, one ad set and two to three genuinely different ads, optimized on the cheapest event that still means a person submitted a phone number (likely Lead at booking-form submit; confirm which event the pixel actually fires there), and judge every ad on CRM-confirmed booked and shown appointments, not Meta's counts. Keep website bookings rather than Instant Forms: agencies report form leads close 20-30% worse ([One PPC Agency](https://oneppcagency.co.uk/facebook-ads/facebook-metas-leads-ads-optimisation-lead-quantity-vs-quality/)) while Jon Loomer saw no quality drop ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-ads-lead-generation/)), so the evidence is split and a solo follower cannot afford volume junk; revisit a qualifying-question form in month two.

| Window | Action | Spend |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 21-27 | Operational fixes below; ad 1 keeps running alone to set a baseline; check Meta Ad Library for competitors by hand | ~$175 |
| Oct 28-Nov 10 | Add Ad 2 (Powerwall/backup, real founder or technician on camera, 15-30 second Reels cut) to the same ad set; ad 1 keeps its SunPower/orphaned hook; both point to a compliant booking page | ~$350 |
| Nov 11-17 | Day-14 review; pause the loser; if checks pass, swap in the Freedom Forever ad for exactly $100 total | ~$175 |
| Nov 18-19 | Month review; decide scale, hold or kill from the measures below | ~$50 |

Decision thresholds are mine, not sourced. Set a target of **about $150 per booked appointment**, with a hard ceiling near $227 (the blended break-even above). Pause any ad that spends 2-3 times the target (about $300-450) with zero bookings, the rule suggested in the benchmarks note. With only perhaps 4-8 total bookings in the month, no result will be statistically significant, so treat the month as a test of whether the funnel works at all. Do not raise budget until cost per shown evaluation sits below gross profit per shown evaluation with margin. Measure, per ad: cost per lead, click-through (cross-industry lead-gen median 2.59% per [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2025)), video hook rate (25-30% solid, a vendor figure per [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/anatomy-top-performing-video-ad-frame-by-frame)), minutes to first response, lead-to-booked rate, show rate, diagnostic attach rate, and proposals and closes by product. Expected volume from the notes' own math: about 12 leads, 2-3 booked and 1-2 shown from $750.

What to fix first, in order of expected effect (my ranking; the speed-to-lead evidence is directional, resting on a 2007 study that compared contact within 5 versus 30 minutes and did not measure closed deals ([Ainora roundup](https://ainora.lt/blog/lead-response-time-statistics-every-study-2026))):

## Compliance changes before the next dollar

None of the legal items below could be confirmed against primary text; they are risks to put in front of a California attorney, not legal conclusions.

**CSLB license advertising.** California licensing law (B&P 7027.1) requires licensed contractors to show their license number in ads, and unlicensed persons advertising contractor work to say they are not licensed ([CSLB](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Media_Room/Press_Releases/2018/March_26.aspx)). Whether it reaches Solar Review, an unlicensed marketer that also charges the customer directly for diagnostics, is the biggest unresolved question, and CSLB issued a January 9, 2020 advisory on lead generation and solar brokers whose text nobody could retrieve ([CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Media_Room/Industry_Bulletins/2020/January_9.aspx)). The conservative structure: name the licensed performer and its license number in the ad or on the landing page, state that Solar Review is not a licensed contractor, and use "arranges, connects, schedules" rather than "we install or repair" until counsel clears it. Performing solar work requires a C-46 license ([CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Solar_Requirements.aspx)).

**AI avatar disclosure.** Meta labels photorealistic AI-generated people in ads ([Meta Help](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024/)), and the FTC's consumer reviews and testimonials rule (16 CFR Part 465) bars fake or AI-generated testimonials from people who do not exist or lack real experience ([Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/08/20/ftc-issues-final-rule-to-fight-fake-product-reviews/)). Safe lane: an avatar may present company information, must be labeled as AI on screen, and must never say "I had a SunPower system too." For a trust-heavy offer a real founder or technician is the better creative anyway; the only evidence is a vendor claim that real creators win at higher price points ([Moburst](https://intercept.moburst.com/ai-avatar-ads-vs-real-creators-which-wins-on-meta/)).

**Installer names and trademarks.** Meta bars ads likely to confuse people about source, sponsorship or affiliation and acts on rights-holder reports ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/intellectual-property-infringement/copyright-and-trademarks)). Nominative fair use can protect plain-text naming of a company to identify the audience ([Smith v. Chanel brief](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/smith-v-chanel-inc)), but no source tested it against Meta's ad review. Use names only in plain text with an "independent, not affiliated" line, no logos, and avoid brand-style URLs such as `/sunpower` as the headline experience. Do not state that a company is out of business without checking: Complete Solaria operates under the SunPower brand, and Freedom Forever's CSLB license status conflicts between SiteVetter ("EMP/WK Bnd Susp") and Prelien ("CLEAR").

**TCPA and consent.** The FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated in January 2025 ([Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule)), but carriers still police brand-specific consent. Add an unticked consent checkbox naming Solar Review next to the phone field, store timestamp and exact text, register 10DLC, honor STOP, and treat skip-traced numbers as cold (the project's own notes say Tracerfy returns no DNC data). California courts have let wiretapping class claims against AI call-transcription tools proceed ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/recent-genai-class-actions-build-on-early-successes)), so keep the existing announce-and-consent gate on inbound ad calls.

**Financing claims.** Meta's mandatory "Financial products and services" special category covers loans ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/special-ad-categories-meta-ads/)); an ad promising low monthly payments or naming Wheelhouse CU risks it, and it restricts targeting. Whether a Participate lease counts is unconfirmed. Keep financing, rebate dollar amounts, tax-credit language and any SDG&E, SDCP or Tesla affiliation out of ads, and state what "free" covers and what costs money next. "5.0 on Google" must match the live rating (34 reviews per the project notes).

## Where the notes disagree, and what to verify first

The most consequential conflict is the SDCP rebate. SDCP's own page lists existing solar plus a new battery at **$250/kWh** market rate and **$350/kWh** for CARE/FERA/Community of Concern, with $350 and $500/kWh for new solar plus battery ([SDCP](https://sdcommunitypower.org/?p=11847)); the funnel note quotes "$350/kWh (up to $500)" for the program, which I read as the new-solar row mixed with the existing-solar one. At 13.5 kWh the arithmetic gives **$3,375** (market) and **$4,725** (CARE/FERA tier), the latter matching Tesla's cited $4,725 per Powerwall ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/sdcp)). The owner's project note of $3,375 is therefore consistent; **$4,275 looks like a digit transposition of $4,725**. No SDCP source found mentions medical baseline, only CARE, FERA and Community of Concern.

## Conclusion

The main change in understanding is that the second ad is a downstream choice, not the lever. A Freedom Forever message may be the most emotionally compelling creative on the list, yet it is the one a $25 budget is least able to deliver to the right people, while the Powerwall offer matches the business's actual margin and the existing-solar audience that the orphaned-installer ad already attracts. Even that offer is only marginally profitable on assumed numbers, so the month's real output is a measured funnel (response time, show rate, attach rate) that tells the owner whether to scale Meta, switch to purchased appointment-set leads or an outsourced setter paid only for shown visits, or spend on search instead.

A second implication is that the most valuable Freedom Forever work may not be an ad at all. If the three feasibility checks pass, a direct approach to the financing partners that now control completion, or a search-intent page, could reach stuck owners more cheaply than Meta ever will. That is my judgment; nothing in the notes tests it.
