# Quoting Utah's Wattsmart battery incentive without overpromising

Rocky Mountain Power's Utah Wattsmart Battery Program pays a homeowner an upfront incentive plus an annual bill credit in exchange for a four-year commitment to let the utility dispatch the battery. For a Powerwall 3 retrofit, the best-supported planning number is **up to $2,000 upfront and about $172.50 per year in bill credit starting in year two**, subject to RMP approval. Solar Review should quote it as "up to", never as a rebate the customer is owed, and should keep it out of the savings math. The sources disagree on the post-June-15-2026 amounts, the credit cap, the reserve floor and whether solar must charge the battery. **No official RMP, PacifiCorp or Utah PSC page could be fetched**, so every figure below comes from search-result summaries and vendor pages. The one clearly actionable process finding is sequencing: the incentive is reportedly lost if the battery is installed, or the PowerClerk application filed, before RMP approves it, so the application must start before installation. The proposal builder and wizard should therefore show the incentive as a separate, conditional line, collect the facts that decide eligibility, and promise nothing that RMP controls.

## What RMP pays, and why the headline number is only a ceiling

RMP's program page, as quoted in search results, says Utah changes take effect June 15, 2026: systems of **10 kW or larger are capped at $2,000 per household and smaller systems at $1,000** ([RMP](https://www.rockymountainpower.net/savings-energy-choices/wattsmart-battery-program.html)). The prior formula was **$400 per kW of battery output, capped at $2,000 per household** ([PacifiCorp program administration PDF](https://www.pacificorp.com/content/dam/pcorp/documents/en/pacificorp/environment/dsm/utah/UT_Program_Administration_Wattsmart_Batteries_25.pdf)), and FranklinWH dates the per-household cap to May 1, 2025 ([FranklinWH](https://www.franklinwh.com/homeowner-incentives/wattsmart-battery-program)). A Powerwall 3 has 11.5 kW output, so it lands in the $2,000 tier under either regime; that classification is my inference, not a quoted statement. Tesla's own page agrees on the ceiling: up to $2,000 upfront for Powerwall 3 owners whose net metering application was filed on or after Sept 1, 2025 ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/RockyMountainPower/wattsmart)). Because every source converges on $2,000 as the maximum, "up to $2,000" is defensible; any single dollar figure beyond it is not.

The ongoing credit is murkier. Tesla says $15 per kW of nameplate as a monthly credit starting year two, roughly **$172.50 per Powerwall 3** ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/RockyMountainPower/wattsmart)); 11.5 kW × $15 is $172.50, so I read that as per year, though Tesla's "over the four-year term" phrasing is ambiguous. A secondary tracker instead reports $15/kW in years 2–4 **capped at $100**, rising to $50/kW (cap $250) from year five ([batteryeconomics](https://batteryeconomics.com/state-programs/program-status-tracker.html)). A $100 cap would contradict Tesla's $172.50, and nothing resolves it. An older news figure of $90 per year ([Fox 13](https://www.fox13now.com/news/technology/more-utahns-getting-solar-storage-batteries-joining-the-virtual-power-grid-model)) and the pre-change $600/kW retrofit rate for solar installed before Sept 1, 2021 ([Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2024/09/rocky-mountain-power-is-paying-solar-customers-more-incentives-if-they-add-storage-later/)) look superseded; one installer blog hedges that the new structure "may include" $1,000 for some systems ([BYOP Electric](https://byopelectric.com/blog/wattsmart-tesla-powerwall-rebate-2026)). Two further items are single-source: a 70%-of-equipment-cost cap (also in the RMP customer brochure per search summary) and a 30 kW maximum battery size (sonnen FAQ, possibly dated). Wyoming's own $1,000/$2,000 tiers dated July 1, 2026 ([Tigo](https://www.tigoenergy.com/bip/rocky-mountain-power-wattsmart-battery-program-wyoming)) can be mistaken for Utah's on aggregator pages, so never reuse a Wyoming or Pacific Power document for a Utah quote.

## Eligibility, enrollment order and the clock that can forfeit the money

Eligibility is where sources split most. Utah Clean Energy's summary says residential and non-residential RMP customers are eligible and solar is not required ([Utah Clean Energy](https://utahcleanenergy.org/rocky-mountain-power-wattsmart-battery-program/)), while the same organization's coverage elsewhere, NREL, and SolarReviews say solar is required or the battery must be charged by it ([NREL](https://nrel.gov/docs/fy24osti/87134.pdf), [SolarReviews](https://www.solarreviews.com/blog/rocky-mountain-power-wattsmart-battery-program)). For existing-solar retrofits this conflict is mostly moot, since the home has solar, but the notes found no source on whether an AC-coupled Powerwall 3 on an existing string inverter qualifies, or whether the battery may charge from the grid. Equipment must be new and on RMP's approved list; Tesla lists Powerwall 3 as eligible and Powerwall 2 and Powerwall+ as not ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/RockyMountainPower/wattsmart)). Homes served by municipal utilities such as Lehi or Provo are reportedly excluded ([batteryeconomics](https://batteryeconomics.com/state-programs/program-status-tracker.html)).

The sequence, per RMP's page as summarized, is: the installer files a customer generation application, the customer files the participation and incentive application, RMP verifies the generation application was received and approved, and then a check is issued ([RMP](https://www.rockymountainpower.net/savings-energy-choices/wattsmart-battery-program.html)). Tesla adds that the offer appears in the Tesla app within about 10 days of the online application, after commissioning and RMP approval. The critical timing rule is that no incentive is paid for batteries installed, or with a PowerClerk application submitted, before the approval date ([RMP customer PDF](https://www.rockymountainpower.net/content/dam/pcorp/documents/en/rockymountainpower/savings-energy-choices/wattsmart-batteries/RMP_BatteryStorageProgram_customer_FINAL.pdf)); the exact meaning of "before the approval date" is ambiguous in the summary and must be confirmed in writing. No source gives approval time, payment time, rejection reasons, required documents, or whether an installer can be assigned the payment, and the contact cited is battery@RockyMountainPower.net.

## Obligations the homeowner takes on

The customer commits to **four years**, keeps an internet connection, and may withdraw on 30 days' notice, with a **prorated payback of the upfront incentive** if they leave early and none after completing the term ([SolarEdge](https://www.solaredge.com/us/incentives/rocky-mountain-power), [Fox 13](https://www.fox13now.com/news/technology/more-utahns-getting-solar-storage-batteries-joining-the-virtual-power-grid-model)). Tesla says the Powerwall is never dispatched below **10%** ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/RockyMountainPower/wattsmart)), but one installer blog says 20% and another summary says RMP's site shows both ([BYOP Electric](https://byopelectric.com/blog/add-battery-to-existing-solar-utah)). Dispatch frequency is unverified: roughly 10 events per year per that blog; sonnen and Utility Dive describe daily dispatch March–October and grid emergencies only November–February, not confirmed for Powerwall 3 ([sonnen FAQ](https://www.sonnenusa.com/frequently-asked-questions/faq-wattsmart-program), [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/rocky-mountain-powers-distributed-battery-grid-management-system-puts-utah/608213)). Pacific Power's "several events a day, about 5 minutes" language belongs to a different utility and must not be applied to RMP. The practical consequence for a retrofit buyer is that the battery is partly RMP's during events, which cuts into backup reserve and own-use savings, and nobody has published how much.

## How it interacts with Utah solar rate schedules

Existing-solar homes sit on one of three schedules. Schedule 135 is legacy net metering, closed to new applicants Nov 15, 2017 and expected to end Dec 31, 2035, with service passing to a later owner at the premises ([PSC docs](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/14docs/14035114/297824ExACommOCS11-08-2017.pdf)). Schedule 136 net billing opened Oct 1, 2020 with a $85 fee, and Schedule 137 is the current export-credit service ([PSC order](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/17docs/1703561/3161911703561o10-30-2020.pdf)). RMP's Jan 2026 filing, approved effective Mar 1, 2026, lists a **4.855 ¢/kWh summer (Jun–Sep) and 4.033 ¢/kWh winter (Oct–May) export credit** ([PSC filing](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/26docs/26035T03/343554Trf1-26-2026.pdf), [approval letter](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/26docs/26035T03/343975TrfAprvlLtrfromPSC2-23-2026.pdf)), matched by a third-party guide ([Gardner Energy](https://gardner-energy.com/net-metering-in-utah-2026-how-rocky-mountain-power-net-billing-works/)). DSIRE (5.704/4.199 ¢) and Permitdesign (5.636/4.745 ¢) disagree and look stale, and the credit resets annually. Schedule 1 residential energy charges on the Dec 2025 tariff were 9.32/12.01 ¢ (Jun–Sep) and 8.25/10.63 ¢ (Oct–May) below and above 400 kWh ([PSC tariff](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/26docs/26035T08/346341Trf8-5-2026.pdf)), but a later redline conflicts and basic charges were not found.

My arithmetic, not a sourced claim: shifting a surplus kWh to the evening on Schedule 137 earns roughly retail minus export credit, about 4.2–4.5 ¢ in the lower tier and 6.6–7.2 ¢ in the upper tier. At the 12.15 kWh usable figure used in this project's notes, with a full surplus cycle every day (the ceiling case), that is only about **$190–$310 a year**, excluding the Wattsmart credit. On Schedule 135, exports credit near retail, so shifting yields roughly nothing and the battery's value is backup plus Wattsmart payments. Whether Schedule 136 behaves like 135 or 137 is unverified. Several vendors say Wattsmart enrollment does not change existing net-metering terms ([Elite Electric](https://eliteelectricpro.com/rmp-wattsmart-battery-rebate-2026/), [BYOP Electric](https://byopelectric.com/blog/add-battery-to-existing-solar-utah)), but nobody read the tariff on whether adding storage or capacity to a legacy system forfeits 135/136 status. That is the highest-stakes unverified item for an installer, because losing legacy status could cost far more than the incentive pays.

## What to build into the proposal builder and wizard

The builder should treat the program as a conditional line, separate from price and savings. Show "Wattsmart incentive (estimate, up to $2,000, paid by RMP to you after approval)" as a clearly labeled possible offset, never netted from the System Price, mirroring the existing SDCP pattern where a rep must tick an explicit qualification flag before the amount appears on a customer document. Default it off. Show the annual credit as "reported about $172.50 per year starting year two, unconfirmed" or omit it until confirmed. Keep it out of the commission, redline and Participate math, since the incentive is RMP's decision and a Participate or financed price must never silently absorb it. The Utah wizard should capture, with a "verified / unverified" mark each: utility is RMP (not municipal), rate schedule (135, 136, 137, or unknown, from the bill), system interconnection date, inverter type and coupling, who holds the existing interconnection agreement, and whether the customer can maintain internet.

The script should say: this is a utility program with a four-year commitment; RMP can use part of the battery during events; the amount is "up to", set by RMP, and applied for before installation; early exit means paying back a prorated share. It should never say the incentive is guaranteed or "free money", never state a savings number that assumes the credit, never promise legacy net-metering status is unaffected, never state a reserve percentage or event frequency as fact, and never imply Solar Review or Tesla controls approval. Before quoting, verify on RMP's page and by email to battery@RockyMountainPower.net: current tiers and effective-date rule (application, approval or install), whether retrofit on existing solar qualifies and what coupling is accepted, the credit amount and cap, reserve floor and event limits, program budget status, and the effect of adding storage on Schedules 135/136. The wizard's current Utah rate handling (Schedule 137 figures, a 12¢ fallback) should be labeled as estimates until the PSC tariff is read directly.

## Conclusion

The research mainly shows how thin the verifiable record is. One number, the $2,000 ceiling, survives every source; almost every other term the buyer cares about (credit cap, reserve floor, event frequency, coupling, grandfathering) is either contradicted or silent. For a battery-retrofit seller this argues for treating the program as an upside to be confirmed in writing, not a selling point, because the arithmetic suggests rate arbitrage under Schedule 137 is modest and could be zero on legacy net metering.

The next step is concrete and cheap: allow the RMP, PacifiCorp and Utah PSC hosts to the research environment, or have a person read the program page, administration PDF and tariff sheets 135/136/137, then replace every "unverified" label above. Until then, quote "up to", apply before install, and never promise.
