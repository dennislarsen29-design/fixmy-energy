# Meta ad angles for a San Diego solar-service business: ranking by expected revenue per ad dollar

Research limits: about 14 tool calls. WebSearch worked. WebFetch failed on every host (DNS errors), so everything below comes from search-result summaries, not full pages. Primary-source pages (SDCP, Tesla, Solar Power World) were seen only as snippets. Nothing was cross-verified against full text. No Meta Ad Library data was reachable. Treat all rankings as reasoned estimates, not measurements.

## 1. Current incentive and tax-credit facts (Oct 2026) that shape every angle

### Takeaway
The federal 30% homeowner credit (25D) is gone for systems installed in 2026 or later, including cash- or loan-bought batteries. SDCP's battery rebate, the Tesla VPP/DSGS payments and legacy NEM terms remain the main value levers for existing-solar owners. SDCP's $3,375 figure appears consistent with its $250/kWh existing-solar market-rate tier, and the CARE/FERA tier is $350/kWh.

### Cited Findings
- 25D covers only property installed by year-end 2025. Anything placed in service on or after Jan 1, 2026 gets no 25D credit. — [tax47.app summary](https://tax47.app/blog/residential-clean-energy-credit-ended-2026/) (secondary vendor source); consistent with [SEIA OBBB explainer](https://seia.org/?p=12215)
- Batteries bought with cash or a loan in 2026 have no federal credit. Batteries installed through a lease or PPA can still qualify for up to 30% under the Section 48E commercial credit. One vendor source says storage is not subject to the wind/solar deadlines. Another says 48E has extra restrictions. — [search summary of installer/tax blogs](https://nuwattenergy.com/en/commercial-solar/tax-credits/battery-storage-2026). Unverified against IRS text.
- SDCP Solar Battery Savings, official page: existing solar plus a new battery pays $250/kWh at market rate and $350/kWh for non-market (CARE/FERA or Community of Concern). New solar plus battery pays $350 and $500/kWh. Everyone earns $0.10/kWh for discharging 50% of the battery in the weekday dispatch window. — [SDCP Solar Battery Savings](https://sdcommunitypower.org/?p=11847)
- The fall 2025 SDCP manual lists the same existing-system tiers ($250 / $350). — [SDCP SBS Program Manual 090925](https://sdcommunitypower.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/SDCP_SBS-Program-Manual_090925.pdf)
- Retention rule: customers must stay enrolled for five years to keep the upfront rebate. — [Enphase press release](https://investor.enphase.com/news-releases/news-release-details/enphase-energy-announces-iq-battery-systems-now-eligible-san)
- Participation rules per an installer summary: the battery must be new, charged only from solar, installed by an approved contractor, and installed after the SDCP application is submitted. A $10,000 rebate cap appears in one aggregator. — [solarinsure summary](https://solarinsure.com/san-diego-community-power-solar-battery-program) (third party, unverified)
- Tesla's SDCP VPP page cites an upfront incentive of $4,725 per Powerwall plus $0.10/kWh discharged, about $345/yr. — [Tesla SDCP VPP page](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/sdcp) (seen via search summary)
- DSGS is the current enrollment route: up to about $350/yr per Powerwall, paid after the season. ELRP is closed to new sign-ups since April 2024. Tesla reports record dispatches in Sept and Oct 2026 (reports conflict on MW totals). — [Tesla DSGS page](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/dsgs), [teslanorth](https://teslanorth.com/2026/10/08/powerwall-600mw-record/). 2026 payout amounts unconfirmed.
- NEM 1.0 and 2.0 keep their terms for 20 years from interconnection. One source says adding a battery keeps legacy status. Another says adding panels beyond a small threshold can move the system to net billing. — [exspenditure](https://exspenditure.com/adding-battery-nem-2-0-california/), [SDCP rooftop solar](https://sdcommunitypower.org/billing-and-rates/rooftop-solar/)
- SDG&E bundled residential average rate: about 41.0¢ to 45.7¢/kWh at Jan 1 2026, then 45.7¢ to 46.4¢ in April 2026 per SDG&E's notice. Summer on-peak TOU-DR1 is about 69.7¢. One tracker claims a 2% cut on June 1; this is unconfirmed. — [SDG&E April 2026 rate alert](https://www.sdge.com/sites/default/files/SDG%26E%20Electric%20Rate%20Change%20Alert%20April%202026.pdf), [nrgcleanpower](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/san-diego-electricity-rates/)

### Inferences
- $250/kWh × 13.5 kWh = $3,375, so the "standard" figure in the project notes is the market-rate existing-solar tier. The CARE/FERA tier would be $350 × 13.5 = $4,725, which matches Tesla's $4,725. The project's CLAUDE.md quotes $4,275 for the CARE tier; this looks like a digit transposition and should be checked.
- Medical baseline does not appear in any SDCP source found. CARE, FERA and Community of Concern do. The "higher for medical baseline" assumption is unsupported.
- Ending 25D removes a strong ad hook ("30% back") but not the SDCP rebate. A SDCP-qualified Powerwall retrofit is still about $3.4k to $4.7k off plus VPP income.

### Gaps
- Official SDCP rebate budget/availability for the current fiscal year and any waitlist (page could not be fetched).
- Whether 48E storage credit is usable by a company-owned/TPO battery for these customers.
- SGIP status and amounts for 2026 (no useful result retrieved).
- Whether DSGS 2026 per-kWh rate and the $350/yr figure still hold.

## 2. Evidence on what converts on Meta after 25D, and demand context

### Takeaway
Residential solar demand fell after 25D ended, and the battery share of installs is high, so storage and service are the stronger positioning than "go solar". No credible battery-specific or service-specific Meta cost-per-lead benchmark was found. Generic solar Meta benchmarks are vendor blogs and should be treated as rough.

### Cited Findings
- Wood Mackenzie's 2026 residential forecast is about 21% below 2025. Q1 2026 installs fell 15% from Q4 2025. — [leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/first-quarter-without-25d-solar-credit-cpl-reset/) (vendor blog)
- Late-2025 demand was pulled forward: EnergySage saw a 205% jump in homeowner engagement in H2 2025. — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/03/02/expiring-us-tax-credit-drives-record-solar-demand-in-h2-2025/)
- Battery attach is high but sources conflict. EnergySage: national 41% to 38% and California 79% to 71% in H2 2025. Another source: record 45% in Q1 2026. — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/03/02/expiring-us-tax-credit-drives-record-solar-demand-in-h2-2025/), [leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/first-quarter-without-25d-solar-credit-cpl-reset/)
- Aurora: only 3% of solar-engaged homeowners say they are not interested in storage. — [Aurora Solar](https://aurorasolar.com/aurora-solar-snapshot/)
- Solar-wide Meta CPL: most sources $30 to $100; one table $22 to $120 with Meta at about $71; conversion 4% to 8%; lead-to-sale 5% to 10%. Meta leads are lower intent than search. — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-facebook-ads-statistics), [Elevarus](https://elevarus.com/meta-ads-solar-lead-generation/) (vendor marketing, conflicting)
- Lead-to-appointment 25% to 40% and appointment-to-sale 20% to 35% pre-25D; payback stretched from about 6.7 to 9.6 years. — [leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/first-quarter-without-25d-solar-credit-cpl-reset/)

### Inferences
- Rough cost per booked appointment, assuming a Meta lead costs $30 to $100 and 25% to 40% of leads become appointments: about $75 to $400 per appointment. This business's own funnel data would be better than these benchmarks.
- With one person handling all follow-up, capacity (not budget) caps volume. Angles with higher ticket per appointment deserve priority.

### Gaps
- No battery-retrofit or service-business Meta CPL data; no Meta Ad Library inspection (not reachable); no San Diego audience-size figures from Meta.

## 3. Angle-by-angle assessment

### Takeaway
Ticket and margin favor Powerwall retrofit and new-solar partner deals; audience size favors SunPower and generic "solar owner" targeting; Freedom Forever PTO is a small, urgent, very fresh niche.

### Cited Findings
- **Angle 1, Freedom Forever PTO.** Chapter 11 filed Apr 15 2026; converted to Chapter 7 effective Aug 7 2026. Claims deadline Oct 16 2026 already passed. Court let financing partners (Credit Human, EnFin, EverBright, GoodLeap, Participate, Project Solar, Sunrun) complete unfinished installs to PTO. The company reported 150,000+ installs nationally. — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/09/Freedom-Forever-Bankruptcy-Converted-To-Chapter-7-Liquidation-Key-Dates-Set/), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/freedom-forever-to-liquidate-after-insider-sale-effort-collapse). Homeowner reports of systems with PTO that did not work or stalled repairs. — [SolarReviews customer reviews](https://www.solarreviews.com/installers/freedom-solar-co-reviews?page=2)
- **Angle 2, SunPower.** Complete Solaria (d/b/a SunPower) bought assets Sep 30 2024. Cash/loan-owner systems energized on or before that date can use other licensed servicers. Lease/PPA customers go to the financier or SunStrong. SunPower's site says warranties for systems energized after that date remain valid. — [SunPower warranty page](https://us.sunpower.com/home-solar-system-warranty), [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/sunpower-is-bankrupt-what-now/?rc=p-pvmag)
- **Angle 4/5, rates and outages.** Rates in section 1. Peak window 4 to 9 pm. No 2026 PSPS or outage data for SDG&E found. Tesla's record VPP dispatch events in Sept to Oct 2026 show grid stress. — [teslanorth](https://teslanorth.com/2026/10/08/powerwall-600mw-record/)
- **Angle 3, battery for NEM 1.0/2.0.** Legacy NEM terms preserved for 20 years and battery additions reportedly keep them (section 1). SDCP existing-solar tier applies.

### Inferences (rough ranking inputs; not measured)
Ticket values are from the business context provided, not researched.
- Angle 3 (Powerwall retrofit, SDCP + VPP + backup): highest ticket ($1k to $4k margin per job) with a strong stackable offer; competition from Tesla-certified installers and solar companies is likely heavy (unverified). Largest addressable pool: every San Diego owner with legacy NEM solar and no battery (count not found).
- Angle 6 (free solar health check): cheapest to convert to appointment and feeds paid diagnostics ($300 to $500) and repairs; low ticket per lead but also a feeder into angle 3 upsell.
- Angle 2 (SunPower): large base, existing skip-trace list overlap, but "orphaned" is already ad 1's theme, so incremental audience is small; best used as a creative variant of the orphaned ad.
- Angle 4 (rates/true-up) and 5 (outages): good hooks for angle 3 creative rather than standalone ads; outage urgency spikes with heat waves and PSPS, which are seasonal (Oct to Nov Santa Ana season is a plausible window, unverified).
- Angle 1 (Freedom Forever PTO): very high urgency and almost no competing messaging, but the affected San Diego population is unknown and probably small (hundreds to low thousands, a guess); ticket per job unclear since financiers may pay completion contractors; claims deadline passing may reduce urgency. Good cheap test, not a scale ad.
- Angle 7 (retargeting, reviews): cheapest cost per appointment because audiences are warm, but small volume; should run alongside any prospecting ad.
- Angle 8 (lookalikes): paid-customer seed is too small (about a dozen payments per project notes; Meta recommends far more); use Schedule events or leads as seed instead.
- Angle 9 (seasonality): 25D gone removes year-end rush; Oct to Mar is rainy and shorter days, which favors indoor/backup and bill-savings messaging over install-your-roof messaging. Specific San Diego seasonal CPL data not found.

### Gaps
- Addressable counts: SDG&E legacy NEM 1.0/2.0 customers, SunPower and Freedom Forever installs in San Diego County, homes with solar but no battery.
- Meta Ad Library competitor themes, spend and creative.
- Whether Powerwall 3 retrofit installers advertise SDCP rebates on Meta.
- Real review counts or photo-review availability for social proof.

## 4. Proposed ranking (reasoned, not measured) and verdict on Freedom Forever

### Takeaway
Run the Powerwall retrofit offer (with SDCP rebate and VPP income, framed around outages and peak rates) as the second ad. Run Freedom Forever PTO as a small, cheap third test. Run retargeting and reviews continuously in the background.

### Inferences
1. Powerwall retrofit for existing-solar owners (angles 3 + 4 + 5 combined): best expected revenue per ad dollar because of margin per job and a time-limited, stackable incentive. Test one offer with one headline per hook (outage, peak rates, SDCP rebate).
2. Free solar health check / "system not producing" (angle 6): lower ticket but high appointment yield and a path into diagnostics, repairs and the battery upsell.
3. Retargeting + social proof (angle 7): highest efficiency per dollar but capped by audience size.
4. SunPower "who services it now" (angle 2): creative variant under the existing orphaned theme.
5. Freedom Forever PTO (angle 1): strong urgency, uncertain size; cap test spend and measure appointments before scaling. Verdict: not the best second ad on expected revenue; a good third test because it has little competition.
6. Lookalikes (angle 8): wait until a larger seed exists.

### Gaps
- All ordering above rests on judgment plus vendor benchmarks. A two-week test with equal budget per angle and tracking cost per booked appointment and per sold job is the way to confirm it.
- Verify before publishing any ad copy: current SDCP rebate amounts and funding availability, CARE/FERA tier figure ($4,725 vs $4,275), the NEM-retention claim for added batteries, and 48E treatment of TPO batteries. Do not state a federal credit for cash/loan batteries.
