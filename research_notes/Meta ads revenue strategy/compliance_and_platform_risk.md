## 1. Meta Advertising Standards: misleading claims, personal attributes, third-party names and trademarks (SunPower, Freedom Forever, etc.)

### Takeaway
Meta's IP policy bans ads likely to confuse people about source, sponsorship or affiliation and acts on rights-holder reports, so "SunPower customers" copy is a real rejection and complaint risk unless it is plainly descriptive and non-affiliated. Nominative fair use is a legitimate legal defense in principle, but no source found applies it to Meta's ad review, and none addresses marketing to a bankrupt company's customers.

### Cited Findings
- Meta ad standards bar ads that infringe third-party IP including trademark, and ads "likely to confuse people about the source, sponsorship or affiliation of the goods or services"; ads can be rejected after a rights-holder report or when there are signs of infringement; rights holders can use the IP Help Center or Brand Rights Protection. — [Meta Copyrights and Trademarks](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/intellectual-property-infringement/copyright-and-trademarks)
- Meta's published fair-use allowance appears on the organic Community Standards page ("authorised by the rights holder and follows established fair use principles"), not confirmed for ads. — [Meta IP Community Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/community-standards/intellectual-property/)
- Nominative fair use three-part test (mark needed to identify the product, no more of the mark than necessary, no suggestion of sponsorship or endorsement); the Ninth Circuit (*Smith v. Chanel*) reasoned that naming a competitor's mark in advertising is not barred absent misrepresentation or confusion. — [Justia summary of nominative-fair-use test case](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/delaware/dedce/1:2023cv00856/83244/106/0.pdf); [Smith v. Chanel brief](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/smith-v-chanel-inc)
- Circuits differ on whether nominative fair use is an affirmative defense (Second Circuit: no, ISC2 v. Security University) versus a confusion-focused approach (Third Circuit). — [Int'l Info. Sys. Sec. Certification Consortium v. Security Univ. (2d Cir. 2016)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/14-3456/14-3456-2016-05-18.html)
- Meta bars "personal attributes" assertions: ads may not assert or imply knowledge of protected traits (race, religion, health, criminal record), and "you/your" phrasing aimed at a trait can trigger review (secondary, agency blogs). — [Directive Consulting](https://directiveconsulting.com/ca/blog/meta-ads-manager-brand-safety/); [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/meta-ads/meta-advertising-policies/provision/CA-P-053227/prohibition-on-asserting-personal-attributes-in-ads/)
- Meta bans false, exaggerated or unsubstantiated claims; testimonials and statistics without support can cause disapproval (secondary). — [LSEO](https://lseo.com/blog/social-media-marketing/meta-ads/ad-policy-compliance-ensuring-your-meta-ads-get-approved/)

### Inferences
- "Is your solar installer still in business?" is a question, not a personal-attribute assertion, but copy like "Your SunPower system is broken/abandoned" implies knowledge of the viewer's customer relationship and equipment; keep to the question form. (Equipment ownership is not a protected class, but the "you/your" trigger is reported.)
- Highest rejection/complaint exposure: the defunct company's name or logo as headline, URLs/page titles that look like the brand's own (fixmy.energy/sunpower), logos, or anything implying SunPower/Freedom Forever endorses or is succeeded by Solar Review. Lower: plain-text name used only to identify whose customers the offer is for, with a clear "independent, not affiliated" line.
- Bankruptcy trustees or successors (e.g., "Complete Solar" holds SunPower assets per the repo's own notes) may still own the marks, so "defunct" is not a safe assumption of abandonment. Claims such as "Titan/SunPower is out of business" are factual statements that must be accurate and current per company.
- Landing-page names are part of the review: Meta policy covers destination pages too for its own marks; for third parties the confusion test applies to the whole ad experience (inference).

### Gaps
- Meta's official text on personal attributes and misleading claims was not read directly (only secondary summaries).
- No source found on Meta's actual handling of competitor names in ads, or on trademark rights after bankruptcy / soliciting a bankrupt company's customers. Ask a lawyer: trademark/Lanham Act §43(a) and Cal. unfair-competition (B&P 17200/17500) exposure of the "/sunpower" URL and name use; whether estate/successor ownership of marks changes risk.

## 2. Meta rules on AI avatars; FTC rules on AI endorsements, fake reviews, and using real Google reviews

### Takeaway
An AI spokesperson is allowed but must not pose as a real customer, real employee or real person with personal experience; Meta may label photorealistic AI people, and FTC rules ban fake or AI-fabricated reviews. Real Google reviews can be quoted but must be genuine, not cherry-picked misleadingly, and attributed.

### Cited Findings
- Meta labels ad images created/materially edited with its generative AI tools "AI info"; for photorealistic AI-generated humans the label sits next to "Sponsored"; C2PA and similar signals are used to flag third-party tool output; advertisers on political/social-issue topics must disclose digitally created content. — [Meta Help: AI-generated images in ads](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024/)
- A blog claims undisclosed AI content is now a rejection reason; unconfirmed from Meta. — [Common Thread Co](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-ai-ad-labels-mandatory-disclosure-ecommerce-2026)
- FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, announced Aug 14, 2024, effective 60 days after Federal Register publication (Oct 2024): bars fake reviews/testimonials including AI-generated ones that falsely claim to be from a real person or someone with no experience; bars buying/selling reviews; covers insiders' undisclosed reviews and review suppression; reach extends to businesses that knew or should have known. — [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/08/20/ftc-issues-final-rule-to-fight-fake-product-reviews/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/ftc-publishes-final-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [FTC final rule PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r311003consumerreviewstestimonialsfinalrulefrn.pdf)
- Review incentives: neutral prompts are deemed compliant; prompts implying reviews must be positive to earn an incentive are not. — [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/08/20/ftc-issues-final-rule-to-fight-fake-product-reviews/)
- 2023 Endorsement Guides update treats virtual influencers/avatars as endorsers; a company-controlled avatar should disclose it is not independent; an avatar cannot honestly claim personal use or experience; avatars are not inherently deceptive (secondary law-firm/blog sources). — [Mondaq on the 2023 Guides](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/social-media/1338132/ftc-finalizes-updates-to-endorsement-guides-reflecting-increased-focus-on-online-reviews-and-social-media-marketing); [Revision Legal](https://revisionlegal.com/internet-law/virtual-influencer-ftc-compliance-guide/)

### Inferences
- An avatar saying "I had a SunPower system too" or "I'm a homeowner like you" would be a fabricated testimonial; a labeled avatar presenting company information ("Solar Review is…") is the safe lane. Add visible on-video disclosure ("AI-generated presenter") even if Meta does not auto-label HeyGen output.
- Using real Google reviews in ads: use verbatim, attributed to the reviewer as shown; don't edit meaning, don't gate who is asked, don't pay for positive reviews; keep the platform's own terms in view (Google's review policies not researched). Star-rating claims ("5.0 on Google") need to match the live rating (the repo notes 34 reviews).
- Don't use the 2024 rule's penalty figures without checking; not retrieved.

### Gaps
- Meta's exact current policy on third-party AI avatars (HeyGen) and whether a label is mandatory for non-Meta tools was not found in primary text.
- Google review reuse permissions (Google ToS / review content license) not researched.
- Whether the Rule was amended or enforced since 2024 not checked.

## 3. California CSLB advertising rules, applicability to a non-licensed lead/sales company, HIS registration, solar disclosure

### Takeaway
B&P 7027.1 requires licensed contractors to show their license number in ads and requires unlicensed persons advertising contractor work to state they are not licensed; whether it reaches Solar Review (an unlicensed marketer promoting Cosmic's work) is the single biggest legal question here and could not be resolved from accessible sources. CSLB has a 2020 advisory specifically on lead generation and solar broker restrictions that must be read before running ads.

### Cited Findings
- CSLB: licensed contractors must show their license number in all ads; unlicensed contractors must disclose in ads that they are not licensed (BPC 7027.1); a San Diego CSLB sting cited illegal advertisements under 7027.1. — [CSLB press release 2018](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Media_Room/Press_Releases/2018/March_26.aspx); [CSLB Spring 2020 newsletter](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/Newsletters/ContractorNewsletterSpring_2020.pdf)
- Covered media reportedly include online ads, websites and social media; contractors can't advertise work outside their classification or reference bonds in ads (BPC 7071.13) (secondary, with conflicting sources on the $500 unlicensed threshold section 7027.1 vs 7027.2). — [Jet Surety](https://app.jetsurety.com/articles/detail/cslb-advertising-requirements); [CSLB 2013 newsletter](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2013-Winter/form.asp); [CSLB 2014 newsletter](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2014-Fall/ad%20guidelines.asp)
- Performing work under a solar lease or sales agreement requires a C-46 license; CSLB issued "Affirming California's Restrictions on Lead Generation and Solar Broker Services in the Residential Solar Industry" (Jan 9, 2020), text not retrievable here. — [CSLB advisory (title/link only)](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Media_Room/Industry_Bulletins/2020/January_9.aspx)
- Solar Energy System Disclosure Document is required under B&P 7169 (not 7159, which supplies the cancellation right) on the front page of every solar energy contract in 16-pt bold, covering costs, complaint info and cancellation right; effective Jan 1, 2019. CSLB form versions differ (5-business-day right for buyers 65+ vs 3-day version in a 2026 newsletter). — [CSLB solar requirements](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Solar_Requirements.aspx); [CSLB Right to Cancel form (Dec 2020)](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/Contractors/RightToCancel.Dec2020.Eng.pdf); [CPUC doc](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Published/G000/M558/K784/558784081.PDF)
- SB 1006 was not found by the search; AB 2699 (Gonzalez) is referenced as a disclosure-document bill (enactment unconfirmed). — [Gravel2Gavel](https://www.gravel2gavel.com/ca-solar-bill-to-watch/)

### Inferences
- Safest ad structure: ads identify the licensed contractor and show CSLB 831831 (Cosmic) where Cosmic is the contractor offering the work, and include a plain "Solar Review is not a licensed contractor; work performed by [licensed contractor]" statement. Whether the license number must appear when the advertiser itself is a marketer is unresolved.
- Claims that Solar Review "repairs/installs/fixes" solar could read as the advertiser contracting for C-46 work; use "arranges/connects/schedules" language. This is the highest-stakes legal risk; the repo's own DBA/"lead/service/sales company" framing may not match how CSLB treats a company that sells and contracts the job (the repo notes Solar Review charges the customer directly for diagnostics, which is a fact a lawyer should weigh).
- HIS registration (B&P 7152) relates to salespersons soliciting home-improvement contracts; the repo tracks it per rep, but applicability to solar sales reps was not verified by a source. (No source found; ask CSLB/attorney.)
- Home-improvement contract rules (B&P 7159) and the solar disclosure live on the contract, not the ad, but ads promising specific price/savings can still be challenged as misleading.

### Gaps
- Full text of B&P 7027.1, 7026, 7030.5 and the CSLB 2020 lead-generation advisory could not be fetched. Needed: does CSLB treat a non-licensed company that markets a licensed contractor's services, and sets appointments, as acting as a contractor/broker?
- HIS applicability to solar; whether CPUC/CSLB issued solar-sales-claim guidance beyond the consumer guide.

## 5. Meta Special Ad Categories: housing/credit/financial products; financing offers (Wheelhouse CU, Participate)

### Takeaway
Solar service ads are not housing, but any ad that promotes a loan or financing offer (Wheelhouse CU loan, Participate lease) risks the "Financial products and services" special category, which restricts targeting; keep financing out of Meta ads or declare the category.

### Cited Findings
- Meta's special categories now cover housing, employment, financial products and services, and social issues/elections/politics; "Financial products and services" became mandatory in the US on Jan 14, 2025, replacing "credit," covering cards, loans, debt recovery plus insurance, bank accounts, investment and payment services (secondary summary). — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/special-ad-categories-meta-ads/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/special-ad-category/)
- Meta developer docs: the restrictions applied to Housing and Employment also apply to the financial input; ads can be rejected if the advertiser doesn't choose the appropriate category. — [Meta Marketing API: Special Ad Categories](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category)

### Inferences
- A diagnostic/repair offer is outside these categories; an ad promoting "$0 down", "low monthly payments" or a loan from a named credit union is likely inside "Financial products and services." Recommend: ad copy stays on the free check/diagnostic; financing talk happens only on the call or proposal page. If financing is advertised, declare the category and accept limited targeting (no narrow age/gender, fewer detailed targets). Whether a lease/PPA like Participate counts was not confirmed in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Meta's own help-center text on what counts as financial products for solar leases/PPAs was not found; ask Meta support or test in Ads Manager.

## 6. TCPA, CTIA / A2P 10DLC, Do-Not-Call, FCC one-to-one rule, CIPA, and a consent flow

### Takeaway
Texting people who clicked/booked is lawful only with prior express (written, for marketing) consent that names the sender; skip-traced numbers have no consent and need DNC scrubbing, which the repo already flags as unaddressed. The FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated in Jan 2025, but carrier/CTIA rules and state laws still push for brand-specific consent. California CIPA claims against call recording and AI transcription tools are active and carry statutory damages.

### Cited Findings
- The Eleventh Circuit in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC* vacated the FCC's one-to-one consent rule (and the "logically and topically associated" requirement) on Jan 24, 2025, just before its effective date. Later FCC/Supreme Court action not checked. — [Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule)
- CTIA Messaging Principles (not law) are enforced by carriers via filtering/blocking; latest update reported Oct 2025; shared-lead consent that doesn't name the sending brand is called out as a risk; unregistered 10DLC traffic reportedly blocked since Feb 2025 (vendor claims). One vendor attributes a "January 2026 FCC one-to-one rule" which is unverified and conflicts with the vacatur above. — [Infobip 2026 TCPA guide](https://www.infobip.com/blog/tcpa-compliance-sms); [MyTCRPlus](https://mytcrplus.com/video-wall/a2p-10dlc-for-affiliate-marketers-lead-generation/)
- CIPA: courts have let class claims against AI call-analytics/transcription vendors proceed past motions to dismiss (*Valencia v. Invoca*, S.D. Cal., Nov 17, 2025; *Ambriz v. Google*; *Taylor v. ConverseNow*, N.D. Cal., "capability" theory); *Brewer v. Otter.ai* claims reportedly survived in Aug 2026 (secondary date); statutory damages cited up to $5,000 per violation. — [Holland & Knight, May 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/recent-genai-class-actions-build-on-early-successes); [BuildBetter](https://blog.buildbetter.ai/ai-notetaker-lawsuits-2026-how-to-record-meetings-legally/); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/dressing-old-laws-in-class-action-suits-applying-anti-wiretapping-laws-to-ai-transcription-services)

### Inferences
- Because the one-to-one rule is gone, a form consent doesn't legally need a per-brand rule, but name "Solar Review (FixMy.Energy)" in the consent checkbox anyway to satisfy carriers and Meta lead forms.
- Recommended web-booking consent: unticked checkbox adjacent to the phone field, text naming the company and that it may call/text (including automated) about the appointment and offers, message frequency, "consent not a condition of purchase", STOP/HELP, link to privacy and terms; store timestamp, IP, page URL and exact consent text with the lead; register 10DLC brand/campaign in GHL; honor STOP and sync to DNC; scrub skip-traced and purchased numbers against the National and California DNC lists and treat them as cold calls (no autodialed text).
- Recording and AI notes: announce at call start that the call is recorded and transcribed by an AI tool and obtain a clear "yes" before the AI tool runs (the repo already gates this; keep the gate for inbound ad calls too). Disclose AI-tool vendor in the privacy policy.
- Apple iMessage commercial-use flagging: not researched.

### Gaps
- FCC status after Jan 2025 (rehearing, new rulemaking, the unverified "Jan 2026" claim) and the FCC revocation-of-consent rules (repo notes Apr 11, 2025) not verified here.
- Apple iMessage rules, Penal Code 632.7 specifics, CTIA primary text, Cal. DNC/Cal. Bus. & Prof. 17592 not researched.

## 8. Practical: new-account restriction triggers, appeals, and an ad-copy "safe language" checklist

### Takeaway
No sourced material gathered on account-restriction triggers or the appeal process; the checklist below is derived from the findings above.

### Cited Findings
- Ads may be rejected after IP reports or signs of third-party infringement. — [Meta Copyrights and Trademarks](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/intellectual-property-infringement/copyright-and-trademarks)
- Rejection if the required special ad category is not chosen. — [Meta Marketing API docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category)

### Inferences (safe-language checklist, derived)
1. Say "solar system check/diagnostic" and "connect you with a licensed solar contractor"; avoid "we install/repair" unless Cosmic is named as the performer.
2. Show "Work performed by Cosmic Solar & Roofing, CSLB #831831" and "Solar Review is not a licensed contractor" in the ad or landing page (confirm requirement with counsel).
3. Name defunct installers only in plain text as the audience ("Had a SunPower system?"), no logos, no brand-style URLs/page titles, with "not affiliated with SunPower or any former installer".
4. No "your system is broken/abandoned"; no assertions about the viewer's finances, health, status.
5. Don't state a company is "out of business"/bankrupt unless currently accurate and sourced; avoid "warranty is void".
6. "Free" must state what is free and what comes next (e.g., diagnostic fee).
7. No savings, rebate, tax-credit or incentive numbers; no SDG&E/SDCP/Tesla affiliation; no urgency/fear countdowns.
8. AI presenter labeled as AI, speaks only as the company, no personal-experience claims; no AI-made customer testimonials.
9. Real Google reviews: verbatim, attributed, current rating.
10. Keep financing and loan terms out of the ad; if used, declare the special category.
11. Consent checkbox and privacy link on the booking form; call-recording/AI notice.

### Gaps
- Account-restriction triggers for new ad accounts, appeal procedure, business verification and the "Advantage+ creative" disclosure rules were not researched; consult Meta Business Help Center. The repo notes the ad account is active with billing set.
