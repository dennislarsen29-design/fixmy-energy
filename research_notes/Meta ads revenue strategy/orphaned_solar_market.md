## Freedom Forever: verified timeline, scale, remedies, CSLB history

### Takeaway
The Chapter 11 filing (Apr 15, 2026, Delaware) and conversion to Chapter 7 (Aug 7, 2026, trustee Alfred T. Giuliano) are consistently reported. Court relief for financing partners to finish stranded installs is reported (GoodLeap named explicitly). I found no count of stuck-without-PTO projects nationally, in California, or in San Diego, and no source that frames the problem as "PTO-stuck" in particular.

### Inferences
- Because the stay relief went to lenders (GoodLeap named) and not to third-party servicers, the first line of remedy for a stranded install is the lender/financier, not an independent service company. A servicer likely fits best for homes already energized, or as a licensed completer the lender or homeowner hires.
- The Oct 16 claim deadline passing means homeowners with deposit/warranty claims who did not file are an added source of anxiety and a possible messaging hook, but also a legal-advice risk area for us.

## Other orphaned segments (size and main pain point)

### Takeaway
Only national or industry-level figures were found; no reliable California- or San Diego-specific customer counts per installer. Pain points are well documented for SunPower (warranty/monitoring) and Sunnova (lease servicing).

### Cited Findings
- SunPower: Chapter 11 on Aug 5, 2024; Complete Solar bought assets (closed Sept 30, 2024) and now uses the SunPower brand. Panel/inverter makers (Enphase, Maxeon) say manufacturer warranties are unaffected; a Complete Solar executive reportedly said it is not responsible for fixing systems for which it did not buy warranties. — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/mortgages/sunpower-bankruptcy); [Solar Insure](https://www.solarinsure.com/sunpower-bankruptcy-warranty) (via summary)
- SunPower monitoring (mySunPower app) warned it may stop working if no third party takes it; Enphase reportedly offers alternative monitoring. Installers report SunPower stopped reimbursing warranty work, leaving some batteries unrepaired. — [ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/let-abc15-know/what-customers-need-to-know-about-sunpower-after-company-files-for-bankruptcy) (via summary)
- SunPower customer count: ~600,000 US per the project's own notes; not independently re-verified here.
- Titan Solar: ceased operations June 13, 2024, Chapter 7 June 20, 2024 (Arizona); claimed 100,000+ households served nationally; many customers still pay a financing company. — [TIME](https://time.com/6989810/titan-solar-closing/); [Arizona bankruptcy court FAQ](https://www.azb.uscourts.gov/sites/azb/files/Titan%20Solar%20Bankruptcy%20FAQs.pdf)
- Sunnova: Chapter 11 June 9, 2025 (S.D. Texas), 500,000+ customers; assets bought by bondholder group (Solaris Assets, ~$118M), servicing moved to SunStrong Management; Omnidian reported to take over customer service/system management for many customers. Customers under lease were reported left without repairs for months while still paying. — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/06/10/sunnova-files-for-bankruptcy/); [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/news/sunnova-bankruptcy/); [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2025/07/omnidian-in-the-running-to-buy-sunnovas-solar-service-platform/)
- Industry-wide: Solar Insure data claims California has 820,000+ "abandoned installations" (all installers, not Titan only; vendor figure, unverified). — as summarized from [TIME](https://time.com/6989810/titan-solar-closing/) search results
- Bloomberg Tax headline: California solar bankruptcies leave homeowners with unfinished work and stuck in equipment leases. — [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/california-brief/solar-panel-bankruptcies-leave-frustrated-homeowners-in-limbo)

### Inferences
- Pain points differ by segment: SunPower = out-of-warranty service and monitoring; Titan = purchased systems with no installer and a loan still due; Sunnova = lease/PPA customers who cannot buy service from us without contract complications; Freedom Forever = unfinished/never-energized systems plus loans.
- Lease/PPA owners (much of Sunnova) have limited ability to pay for repair because the lessor owns the system; purchased-system segments (SunPower, Titan) are better paid-service fits.

## Can a third party help a PTO-stuck Freedom Forever homeowner (SDG&E)?

### Takeaway
Not established. No source describing SDG&E's process for a changed contractor of record, and no data on conversion rates.

### Cited Findings
- Advice articles suggest contacting the utility for PTO status (installer paperwork is usually required) and contacting a licensed local installer for partial systems. — as summarized from search results on [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/04/15/residential-solar-company-freedom-forever-files-chapter-11-bankruptcy/) and [8MSolar](https://8msolar.com/?p=20648) (general guidance, unsourced to utility)
- Court relief lets lenders complete installs, implying the lender (e.g., GoodLeap) may control who finishes the job. — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/09/Freedom-Forever-Bankruptcy-Converted-To-Chapter-7-Liquidation-Key-Dates-Set/)

### Inferences
- A licensed partner would likely need homeowner authorization and, for a financed system, lender consent before taking over a permit, inspection and interconnection application. Whether SDG&E accepts a new contractor of record is unverified.
- A realistic revenue fraction cannot be estimated from found data; any figure would be a guess.

### Gaps

## Preliminary ranking (my judgment, low confidence because sizing data is missing)
1. SunPower: largest purchased-system base, documented warranty/monitoring orphan problem, verified brand continuity confusion.
2. Titan Solar (and other purchased-system orphans like Sullivan, Petersen-Dean): installer gone with no successor; loan still due.
3. Freedom Forever PTO-stuck: highest urgency but unquantified, lender-controlled remedy, legal/lien risk, likely small fraction convertible.
