# Utah (Salt Lake area) rules and channels for door-knock, cold-call and advertising of solar repair, battery retrofits and diagnostics

Research method note: web search worked; direct page fetches of utah.gov hosts (le.utah.gov, commerce.utah.gov, dcp.utah.gov, slc.gov) failed in this sandbox (DNS blocked), so everything below comes from search-result summaries, not full-text reads of the statutes. Treat all of it as "reported, verify at the primary URL". This is not legal advice.

## 1. Door-to-door: cancellation notice, city permits, signage, hours, what to carry

### Takeaway
Utah gives home-solicitation buyers a three-business-day cancellation right with required notice language, and Salt Lake City requires a business license (or employment under one) plus a background-check report for solicitors. I could not confirm solicitor rules for Sandy, Draper, West Jordan, South Jordan, Lehi, Provo or Orem, or any state or city "No Soliciting" sign, hours or badge details, so those are open.

### Cited Findings
- Utah Code 70C-5 (home solicitation sales, credit sales made at the buyer's home): buyer may cancel until the end of the third business day after signing; chapter was amended in 2025 — [Utah Code 70C-5-102](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title70C/Chapter5/C70C-5-S102_1800010118000101.pdf), [chapter 70C-5](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title70C/Chapter5/C70C-5_1800010118000101.xml)
- Utah Admin. Code R152-11-9: a supplier that does not give notice of the three-business-day cancellation right on a direct solicitation sale over $25 commits a deceptive practice; the rule excludes sales covered by the Home Solicitation Sales Act and the Telephone Fraud Prevention Act; Saturdays, Sundays and holidays are not business days — [R152-11-9](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R152-11-9)
- The statute's required cancellation-notice language is in Utah Code 13-11-4 and was recently revised (2025 and 2026 versions exist) — [13-11-4 (2026 version)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter11/C13-11-S4_2026050620260506.pdf)
- The FTC Cooling-Off Rule adds a parallel three-day right for in-home sales, requiring disclosure of the right and cancellation forms — [LegalFix summary](https://www.legalfix.com/topics/consumers/door-to-door-sales-contracts/ut) (secondary source)
- A KSL "Get Gephardt" story describes a Utah buyer struggling to cancel a door-to-door sale; shows cancellation disputes get press — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/50477603/get-gephardt-utah-man-struggles-to-cancel-door-to-door-sale)
- Salt Lake City: city ordinance 5.64 governs solicitation; a solicitor must apply for a business license or be an employee under a current business license, and applicants must upload a BCI (criminal background check) report; business licensing contact business.license@slcgov.com — [SLC Finance, business licensing](https://www.slc.gov/Finance/?p=416)
- A third-party aggregator says SLC also requires a "Direct Sales" registration and that individual agents must be registered; I could not confirm this on the city site — [cityrulelookup.com](https://cityrulelookup.com/soliciting/solicitor-permits/salt-lake-city-ut) (unverified)
- Other Utah cities each have their own solicitor ordinances (examples found: Lehi Ord. 18-2013, Cottonwood Heights, Logan solicitor registration, Ogden, Syracuse, Washington, Price, River Heights, Park City) — e.g. [Lehi Ord 18-2013](https://www.lehi-ut.gov/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Ord-18-2013.pdf), [Logan solicitor registration intro](https://cms9files.revize.com/loganut/document_center/Business%20Licensing/Introduction%20to%20Solicitor%20Registration.pdf), [Cottonwood Heights](https://cottonwoodheights.utah.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/4157/637382001886900000). These are documents located, not read in full; contents unverified.

### Inferences
- Because solicitor rules are city-by-city in Utah, the safe plan is to get an SLC business license (or have reps work under one) before any knocking, and to call each city's business-licensing office for the specific suburb before first knock. A visiting out-of-state company likely needs a Utah business registration and city license first.
- Every in-home sale needs the cancellation notice and form in the contract; building this into the Utah contract template is a one-time fix.

### Gaps
- Text of SLC Ch. 5.64 (fees, badge, hours, exemptions, "No Soliciting" sign effect) not read.
- Sandy, Draper, West Jordan, South Jordan, Lehi (current), Provo, Orem: no verified current requirements found.
- Whether Utah state law makes ignoring a posted "No Soliciting" sign a violation: not found.
- Allowed door-knocking hours: not found for any city.

## 2. Telemarketing, Do-Not-Call, calling hours, recording, texting

### Takeaway
Utah requires most telephone sellers to register annually (with a bond) under the Telephone Fraud Prevention Act, bars calls to residential/cell numbers without prior express consent from 9 p.m. to 8 a.m., on Sundays and legal holidays, and bars unsolicited calls to Do-Not-Call numbers. Texting inclusion and a possible 2026 renumbering are unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Utah Code 13-25a-103: without prior express consent, no telephone solicitation to a residential or cellular phone between 9 p.m. and 8 a.m. local time, on Sundays, or on legal holidays; automated dialing systems for solicitation barred unless the called person consented to that solicitor; must also comply with 47 U.S.C. 227 — [13-25a-103](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter25A/C13-25a-S103_2022050420220504.html)
- Utah Code 13-25a-108 prohibits unsolicited calls to people in Utah, including numbers on the federal Do-Not-Call registry (reported from a 2020 version) — [13-25a-108](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter25a/C13-25a-S108_2020051220200512.html)
- Chapter 13-25a is titled the Telephone and Facsimile Solicitation Act; a 2022 bill (HB 102) proposed adding text messages to regulated communications; the final wording was not confirmed — [HB 102 (2022)](https://le.utah.gov/~2022/bills/hbillint/HB0102.htm)
- Telephone Fraud Prevention Act (Title 13, Ch. 26): unless exempt, each telephone soliciting business must register annually with the Division of Consumer Protection and post a bond for restitution — [Division page](https://dcp.utah.gov/for-businesses/seller-telephone-soliciting/), [Title 13 Ch. 26](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter26/C13-26_1800010118000101.pdf), [Admin. Code R152-26](https://dcp.utah.gov/downloads/R152-26.pdf)
- Telephone-sale cancellation: Division FAQ describes a three-day right; statute 13-26-5 sets mail-in cancellation and joint liability of solicitor and seller — [Division FAQ](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/for-businesses/seller-telephone-soliciting/frequently-asked-questions/), [13-26-5 (2024)](https://law.justia.com/codes/utah/2024/title-13/chapter-26/section-5/)
- Third-party summary says the Act was renumbered to 13-26-101 to -108 effective May 6, 2026, and that the hours rule has no business-to-business exemption — [Trellus](https://www.trellus.ai/calling-laws/utah) (unverified, third-party)

### Inferences
- Cold-calling Utah homeowners from California will probably require Utah telemarketing registration plus bond unless an exemption applies (e.g. calls only to existing customers or only after a requested visit; exemptions not confirmed). Scrub against the federal DNC list and respect 8 a.m.–9 p.m. Mon–Sat.

### Gaps
- Exemptions from registration, bond amount, and fee: not found.
- Utah recording consent: Utah is widely understood as one-party consent, but I found no source in this session. If the dialer records, remember California's all-party rule applies to calls with California residents; confirm independently.
- Whether Utah's text-messaging coverage was enacted: not confirmed.

## 3. Solar-specific: retailer registration, contractor licensing, sales-rep rules

### Takeaway
Utah HB 57 (2025) requires "residential solar panel retailers" to register with the Division of Consumer Protection by July 1, 2026, with bond, background checks and a reported W-2-employee requirement for sales reps. A follow-up 2026 bill (HB 428) appears not to have passed. Whether a repair/battery/diagnostics-only company counts as a "retailer" is not resolved from sources found.

### Cited Findings
- HB 57 (Residential Solar Panel Consumer Protection Amendments) enacted May 7, 2025; on or after July 1, 2026 a solar retailer may not operate in Utah without registration; each sale without registration is a separate violation (Utah Code 13-52-302(4)(d)) — [Utah Commerce, Residential Solar Panel Retailers](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/residential-solar-panel-retailer/), [enrolled HB 57](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/enrolled/HB0057.pdf)
- Application requires fingerprint cards for each participant, consent to background check, and a surety bond or deposit control agreement; registration must be updated within 30 days of changes; SEC-registered public corporations are excepted from background check — [Commerce registration page](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/residential-solar-panel-retailer/residential-solar-panel-retailer-apply-for-registration/), [2026 instructions PDF](https://commerce.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Residential-Solar-Panel-Retailer-Instructions-2026.pdf)
- Bond amount is tiered: fewer than ten employees and under 500 kW sold annually versus ten or more employees or over 500 kW; exact dollars not found — [Commerce page](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/residential-solar-panel-retailer/)
- Registration fees create new revenue starting FY 2026 (amounts not found) — [Fiscal note HB 57S02](https://legiscan.com/UT/supplement/HB0057/id/530296/Utah-2025-HB0057-Fiscal_Note_HB0057S02.pdf)
- Third substitute of HB 57 required a solar retailer's sales representative to be a W-2 employee; the enrolled text's final wording could not be confirmed in these results (enrolled version defines "sales representative" as someone in a business relationship with a retailer to sell) — [HB 57 S03](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/introduced/HB0057S03.pdf), [enrolled](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/enrolled/HB0057.pdf)
- The Division's page applies to businesses that sell or propose to sell a residential solar energy system under a system purchase agreement — [Commerce page](https://commerce.utah.gov/?p=33833)
- HB 428 (2026, Solar Energy Amendments) would add compensation disclosure, NABCEP-style certification and a $25,000 bond for sales reps; a tracker (AI summary, last updated Aug 29 2026) shows "House Comm - Not Considered", i.e. apparently stalled — [Better Utah bill tracker](https://billtracker.betterutahinstitute.org/bills/HB0428), [HB 428 text](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2026/bills/introduced/HB0428.pdf)
- Contractor licensing (DOPL): E200 general electrical contractor; S202 solar PV classification excludes NEC-governed wiring but may subcontract to an E200/E201 contractor — [R156-55a-301](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R156-55a-301); third-party guide agrees, noting a licensed electrical contractor can do solar PV electrical work without S202 — [GreenLancer](https://www.greenlancer.com/post/utah-solar-permit-and-interconnection)
- Enforcement precedent: Utah DCP sued Elan Solar (Orem, 2024) over alleged deceptive sales to about 140 customers, and the company was ordered to halt business — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/news/business/a-new-industry-aims-to-help-frustrated-homeowners-out-of-rooftop-solar-deals/51490104) (reported in search summary)

### Inferences
- A company selling repair/diagnostics might not be a "retailer" (the definition centers on selling a residential solar energy system), but battery retrofit packages, panel additions or any system-sale contract may trigger it. Ask the Division in writing and keep the answer.
- Using California 1099 reps in Utah is the biggest exposure if the W-2 rule survived; either confirm exemption or employ Utah reps as W-2.
- A company that sells and subcontracts install likely needs the installing subcontractor holding the DOPL license, but whether the seller itself needs a contractor license under Utah Code 58-55 was not resolved.

### Gaps
- Exact bond amounts, fees, W-2 final text, definition of "residential solar energy system" (does it include battery-only?), and contractor-license requirement for a seller that subcontracts: not found.
- Utah three-business-day notice and contract-content rules specific to solar contracts: not read.

## 5. Rocky Mountain Power programs and pitch drivers

### Takeaway
Utah has three export tiers: Schedule 135 (grandfathered net metering, closed to new applicants Nov 15, 2017), Schedule 136 (transition export credits) and Schedule 137 (net billing for interconnection applications on or after its effective date, credit paid on all exported energy), with credits reset annually and cut in the January 2026 filing. The Wattsmart battery program pays an upfront per-kW incentive plus annual credit, but amounts vary by source and date.

### Cited Findings
- Schedule 135 closed to new service November 15, 2017 — [PSC tariff/EXHIBIT](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/14docs/14035114/297824ExACommOCS11-08-2017.pdf), summary in search results
- Schedule 137 applies to customers submitting an interconnection application on or after its effective date; separate from grandfathered and transition customers; credits paid for all exported energy; no interval metering installed for Schedule 137 customers — [PSC order 17-035-61](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/17docs/1703561/3161911703561o10-30-2020.pdf), [RMP testimony](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/17docs/1703561/311964RMPDirectTestim2-3-2020.pdf)
- Export credits updated annually; the January 2026 annual filing lowered the credit (reported about 15% lower in summer); revisions effective March 1, 2026 — [Docket 26-035-T03 filing](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/26docs/26035T03/343554Trf1-26-2026.pdf), [PSC approval letter](https://pscdocs.utah.gov/electric/26docs/26035T03/343975TrfAprvlLtrfromPSC2-23-2026.pdf)
- A 2023-era third-party page lists about 5.16 ¢/kWh (Jun–Sep) and 4.462 ¢/kWh (Oct–May), outdated for 2026 — [EnergyBot](https://www.energybot.com/incentives/utah/net-billing-743.html)
- Wattsmart battery program: four-year initial term with annual renewal; upfront $400/kW of storage and $15/kW annual (2024 article); a retrofit-to-existing-solar tier reported at $600/kW (partial source); batteries must be new; RMP can use batteries daily March–October and only for emergencies November–February; amounts can change with 30 days' notice — [SolarReviews](https://www.solarreviews.com/blog/rocky-mountain-power-wattsmart-battery-program), [sonnen FAQ](https://www.sonnenusa.com/frequently-asked-questions/faq-wattsmart-program), [Utah Clean Energy](https://utahcleanenergy.org/rocky-mountain-power-wattsmart-battery-program/), [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2024/09/rocky-mountain-power-is-paying-solar-customers-more-incentives-if-they-add-storage-later/)

### Inferences
- With Schedule 137 credits falling and no hourly arbitrage on Schedule 135/136 export, a battery retrofit pitch rests on backup plus the Wattsmart payment, and should be priced against current amounts only.

### Gaps
- Schedule 136 end date / any 2035 sunset: not found. Current 2026 export credit values and current Wattsmart amounts and approved manufacturers: not confirmed; official page is rockymountainpower.net/savings-energy-choices/wattsmart-battery-program.html (not fetched).
