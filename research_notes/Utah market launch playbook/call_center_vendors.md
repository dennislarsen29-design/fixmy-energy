# Outsourced call-center / appointment-setting options for a Salt Lake City solar repair launch

Research limits: 9 searches (snippet-level; WebFetch of commerce.utah.gov failed because the host is not allowed in this session). No vendor pricing page was read directly. Almost all price and benchmark figures come from vendor blogs, directories or freelancer listings and are unaudited. Treat every number as a planning range to confirm on a quote.

## Which US and nearshore/offshore providers do outbound solar/home-services appointment setting, and what do they charge?

### Takeaway
Named inbound answering services (Smith.ai, Ruby, Abby Connect) are priced per call or per minute and are not outbound dialers. For outbound residential appointment setting the credible options found are offshore BPOs (Philippines) at roughly $8-18/hr, US-based setters at $25-45/hr, per-appointment freelancers/agencies at roughly $50-190 per set or sit, and AI-assisted vendors on retainer plus per-appointment fees. I could not verify Boldr, Hello Rache, Outsource Access, Virtual Latinos or Go Answer for solar outbound or pricing.

### Cited Findings
- Smith.ai bills per call: one comparison lists $300/30 calls, $810/90 calls, $2,100/300 calls, overage $8.50-11.50/call; AI plans ~ $95/mo for ~50 calls (third-party estimate). Competitor-written and third-party, so unreliable — [JustCall Smith.ai alternatives](https://justcall.io/blog/best-smith-ai-alternatives.html)
- Abby Connect bills per minute: $165 for 50 minutes in one comparison; another lists $329 for 100 minutes (sources conflict) — [Abby vs Smith.ai](https://www.abby.com/comparisons/abby-vs-smith-ai/), [Geekflare](https://geekflare.com/guides/best-business-answering-services/)

- Belkins (B2B, not relevant to residential) pay-per-appointment from $750 — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/pay-per-appointment)

### Inferences
- Realistic budget for a 1-2 agent offshore pilot is about $1,200-5,000/month; US-based dedicated is 2-3x that. Hourly dedicated beats per-appointment for a pilot because you learn your own conversion data and per-appointment vendors may game quality (VA Masters' argument, a vendor view).
- Inbound services (Smith.ai/Ruby/Abby) are only useful for answering returning calls, not for outbound.

### Gaps
- Pricing pages for Boldr, Hello Rache, Outsource Access, Virtual Latinos and Go Answer were not found; whether they do residential solar outbound is unverified.
- No solar-specific SDR vendor with published rates found; no Latin America vendor evidence found.

## Benchmarks and pilot design

### Takeaway
Published benchmarks are vendor blog planning targets, not audited data: set rate of contacts roughly 25-45% (digital leads) to 40-60% (canvass/referral); show rate 65-80% for well-run teams, 60-75% for inbound digital leads. No reliable contact-rate (dials to conversations) benchmark found.

### Cited Findings
- Contact-to-set 40-60% (canvassing/referral), 25-45% (digital); setters booking 70-80% likely not qualifying — [Rework solar KPIs](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-sales-kpis-metrics)
- Show rate 65-80% of sets for well-run residential solar teams, under 60% signals qualification/confirmation problem; confirmation rate under 75% means too-passive process — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-sales-kpis-metrics)
- Self-generated 75-85% and inbound digital 60-75% show rates — [Rework no-show guide](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/no-show-reschedule-reduction)
- Elevarus found no independent published solar no-show benchmark — [Elevarus](https://elevarus.com/buy-solar-leads-appointment-set-vs-raw-economics/)

### Inferences
- A sensible pilot (my design, not sourced): 2 agents, 4-6 weeks, ~2 hrs/day training week one, track dials, contacts, sets, held appointments (not just sets), cost per held appointment, DNC/complaint count, call QA score. Set success thresholds such as show rate at least 60% and a cost per held appointment below the company's value ceiling. Note the company's own leads (defunct-installer permit lists) are cold, so Rework's digital/canvass ranges may not transfer.

### Gaps
- Contact rate and dial-to-set benchmarks for cold permit-list calling were not found.

## Compliance: TCPA, DNC, Utah registration, recording, liability

### Takeaway
Utah requires sellers/telemarketers to register under the Telephone Fraud Prevention Act with a Utah agent and bond (rules tightened by SB 38 effective May 6, 2026); calling hours are 8am-9pm local, no Sundays/holidays; Utah is one-party consent for recording but California-style all-party states may apply to other parties. Seller liability for an outsourced caller's violations should be assumed.

### Cited Findings
- Utah SB 38 (effective May 6, 2026) changed registration; letters of credit no longer accepted, only certificate of deposit or surety bond — [Utah DCP Seller Telephone Soliciting](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/for-businesses/seller-telephone-soliciting/apply-for-registration/) (search snippet; page not opened)
- Third-party summary: unless exempt, sellers whose solicitations originate or are received in Utah register annually, name a Utah agent, submit fingerprints, keep a surety bond — [Trellus Utah laws](https://www.trellus.ai/calling-laws/utah)
- Registration form must designate a Utah-resident agent for service — [Utah Code 13-26](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter26/C13-26_1800010118000101.pdf)
- Calling not permitted between 9pm and 8am local, on Sundays or legal holidays without prior express consent; solicitors must honor the National Do Not Call Registry and stop calling on request — [Utah Code 13-25a](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter25a/C13-25a_1800010118000101.pdf)
- Utah is a one-party-consent recording state (Utah Code 77-23a-4); a party or consenting party may record — [Utah Code 77-23a](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title77/Chapter23A/C77-23a_1800010118000101.pdf), [RCFP Utah guide](https://www.rcfp.org/reporters-recording-guide/utah/)
- FCC one-to-one consent rule was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit in early 2025 and formally eliminated in September 2025 — [Mofo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule), [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2025/09/the-fcc-issues-final-rule-formally-eliminating-the-one-to-one-consent-requirement)
- TCPA "revoke-all" provision timing conflicts: one source says April 11, 2026, another says delayed to January 31, 2027 — [Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2025/09/fccs-final-rule-on-consent-kills-one-to-one-consent-requirement/); contradicted by [DEV Community TCPA 2026](https://dev.to/gamlin/tcpa-2026-changes-what-call-centers-need-to-know-before-they-get-sued-g1l) (vendor blog)

### Inferences
- A contract should require: vendor registered/bonded in Utah (or confirm the seller registration covers them), scrubbing against the National DNC and your internal DNC before every dial, honoring revocations within the legal window, no autodialed/prerecorded calls or texts to cell phones without written consent, recording disclosure and retention of recordings, caller-ID/STIR-SHAKEN attestation on numbers used, indemnification and audit rights, and a right to terminate on complaints. These are my recommendations, not found in a source; have a lawyer review.

### Gaps
- Exact Utah bond amount unverified (secondary source suggested $25,000/$50,000/$75,000 tiers, could not confirm); commerce.utah.gov could not be fetched.
- Seller vicarious-liability standard for outsourced telemarketers and STIR-SHAKEN responsibilities were not sourced in this pass.
- Whether a company calling its own prior/permit lists is exempt from Utah registration was not determined.

## Integration with GoHighLevel / custom CRM

### Takeaway
GHL supports outside staff via sub-account users with restricted roles and assigned-only data visibility, and calendar assignment; call recording depends on Twilio number settings. Details on recordings access for restricted users were not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Sub-account roles are Admin or User; users can be limited per module and to only assigned records — [GHL user roles](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000002544-sub-account-user-roles-permissions-and-assigned-data)
- A restricted user sees only calendars they are assigned to; granular calendar permissions were a feature request — [GHL ideas](https://ideas.gohighlevel.com/scheduling-calendar/p/granular-calendar-permissions-for-restricted-users)
- Unofficial support-docs dataset says call recording is enabled per Twilio number and each location needs its own Twilio sub-account — [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/asucada/ghl-support-docs-raw) (secondary; verify)

### Inferences
- Simplest integration: give vendor agents restricted GHL (or company dialer) logins, have them book onto the shared evaluation calendar, and require a lead-source tag and a call note on every set. The company's existing in-house dialer and calendar booking flow could be reused.

### Gaps
- Vendor-specific GHL integration practices not found.
- Handoff process when a homeowner agrees (warm transfer vs booking) is vendor-specific and was not sourced.

## Pros/cons vs local part-time Utah setters vs in-house dialer, and recommendation

### Takeaway
Offshore BPO is cheapest per hour but carries accent, compliance and quality risk; US-based is costlier; local part-time setters or the in-house dialer give control and cleaner compliance. Evidence here does not support naming three vetted solar-outbound vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Hit Rate Solutions notes accents may still be noticeable on certain accounts, so agent selection matters — [Saleshive](https://saleshive.com/?p=62461)
- Cost spread: ~$8-18/hr offshore vs $25-45/hr US — [VA Masters](https://vamasters.com/how-much-does-appointment-setter-cost/)

### Inferences
- Candidate shortlist to request quotes from (unverified for solar outbound; chosen from what surfaced): (1) Hit Rate Solutions (Philippines, contractor estimate-scheduling case study, from $7/hr), (2) Outsource BPO (US-run, Philippine teams, claims contractor/solar appointment setting), (3) Flatworld Solutions (solar/energy appointment case study) or a dedicated offshore setter hired directly. Alternatively pilot 1-2 local part-time Utah setters on the in-house dialer as the control.
- Ask on every sales call: solar/home-services references, who is the registered seller of record, Utah registration and bond status, DNC scrubbing method, recording and retention, caller ID/STIR-SHAKEN practices, hourly vs per-held-appointment pricing, no-show and replacement terms, agent dedication/turnover, onboarding time, QA process, CRM/calendar access, termination terms, indemnification.

### Gaps
- No Reddit r/solar or SolarSalesProfessionals threads, G2/Trustpilot reviews, or pricing pages were reviewed; vendor ranking is therefore not evidence-based.
