# Competitor programs: residential TPO (lease / PPA / lease-to-own) for solar and batteries — benchmark for a Solar Review Powerwall 3 lease

Method note (read first): web search worked, but WebFetch was blocked by the session's network policy for every site tried (solarpowerworldonline.com, energysage.com, renewablesnow.com). So every finding below comes from search-result summaries, not the full pages, and nothing was cross-read in full. Most sources are installer marketing, broker sites and trade press, not contracts or court dockets. Treat figures as leads to confirm. Internal facts from this repo's CLAUDE.md are labeled "(internal)".

## 1. Participate Energy — who they are, structure, dealer terms, health

### Takeaway
Participate Energy is a small, private, Torrance-CA prepaid-lease (lease-to-own) provider founded 2022 that owns the system, claims the commercial 48E credit, and gives the homeowner roughly a 30% discount in exchange for one upfront payment; its investors, funding facilities and dealer funding timing are not public in anything found. Solar Review already sells it and has experienced Participate not funding Cosmic's completion payment (internal).

### Cited Findings
- Profile: Participate.Energy LLC, privately held, founded 2022, HQ Torrance, CA; LinkedIn lists 2–10 employees — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/participate-energy)
- CEO named as Stephen Pollock in a SUNation Energy financing announcement (~April 2026); no founder or equity/debt raise announcement found — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/sunation-energy-secures-financing-deal-with-participate-energy-93CH-4620284)
- Funding source: one listing says its marketplace was developed with U.S. Department of Energy funding (unverified, single source) — [EnergySage supplier profile](https://www.energysage.com/supplier/29235/participate-energy/)
- Structure: Participate owns the system for an initial period, claims the federal commercial credit (48E), and passes part of it back as a lower lease price — [EnergySage review](https://www.energysage.com/solar/participate-energy-review/); [Long Island installer press release](https://www.lipowersolutions.com/press-release-power-solutions-offering-long-island-solar-installations-through-the-participate-energy-program/)
- Price: one installer says the homeowner pays a one-time amount equal to 70% of the lease value (i.e., ~30% off); EnergySage says the discount varies by location and installer — [Power Solutions release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/916587033/power-solutions-offering-long-island-solar-installations-through-the-participate-energy-program); [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/participate-energy-review/)
- Term / buyout: 25-year initial term (Aurora integration docs); homeowner can buy the system after year six at fair market value; one installer (Sunergy, WA) lists a 25-year solar lease and a 10-year battery lease, both transferable on sale — [Aurora help](https://help.aurorasolar.com/hc/en-us/articles/53645684634259-How-to-use-the-Participate-Energy-integration-in-Sales-Mode); [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/participate-energy-review/); [Sunergy Systems](https://sunergysystems.com/residential/prepaid-lease/)
- Credit: no FICO/credit check required; no standard UCC-1 on every home (third-party lenders financing the prepayment may file one) — [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/participate-energy-review/)
- Footprint (April 2026): AZ, CA, CO, HI, ID, NJ, NY, TX, UT; later installer announcements suggest expansion — [Solar Power World via search summary](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/04/prepaid-leases-provide-pathway-to-home-owned-solar-projects/)
- Channel/software: integrated into Aurora and Artemis design/proposal tools; dealer partners include SUNation (NY) and Citadel Roofing & Solar — [Artemis release](https://www.prweb.com/releases/artemis-integrates-participate-energy-prepaid-lease-for-solar-installers-302736033.html); [Citadel](https://blog.citadelrs.com/news/citadel-roofing-solar-partners-with-participate-energy-to-expand-solar-access-after-tax-credit-expiration)
- Participate was one of seven financing partners the Freedom Forever bankruptcy court allowed to enforce remedies and finish stranded installs (with Credit Human, EnFin, EverBright, GoodLeap, Project Solar, Sunrun) — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/09/freedom-forever-bankruptcy-converted-to-chapter-7-liquidation-key-dates-set/); [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/freedom-forever-files-for-bankruptcy/)
- (internal) Solar Review sells Participate as a "lease-to-own" with a fixed 30% discount used as urgency; Participate redline in Solar Review's Axia/QCells presets is $2.19/W; commission is computed on the full System Price, not the discounted price — CLAUDE.md (2026-08-27, 2026-09-06 entries)
- (internal) Cosmic's 50% completion payment is unpaid "while Participate is not funding" (2026-10-23 expansion notes) — CLAUDE.md

### Inferences
- A 2–10 person company carrying a 25-year asset book implies the capital comes from outside tax-equity/debt partners not disclosed publicly; counterparty concentration risk for dealers is real and unmeasurable from public data.
- Solar Review's own "Participate not funding" experience is the strongest available evidence of slow/paused dealer funding; no public complaint record was found to corroborate or contradict it.
- The structure Solar Review would copy is simple: one upfront payment, ~30% off, owner keeps the tax credit, buyout after year 6 (after the 5-year ITC recapture window).

### Gaps
- Investors, credit/tax-equity facilities, dealer onboarding requirements, dealer funding milestones and timing, and any public complaints about slow funding: none found (fetch blocked; not in search summaries).
- Financial health 2025–2026: no filings or ratings found.

## 2. Maxwell Power (formerly HDM Renewable Finance / HDM Capital)

### Takeaway
The relevant "Maxwell" is Maxwell Power, a San Diego TPO platform that rebranded from HDM Renewable Finance in June 2026; it sells a prepaid PPA (marketed as a prepaid lease) for solar, solar + Powerwall, or Powerwall only, backed by large Fairtide Partners commitments (reported $750M, then +$800M, ~$1–1.8B total), with ownership reportedly passing to the homeowner around year 6.

### Cited Findings
- Rebrand: HDM Renewable Finance rebranded as Maxwell Power and raised additional funding (June 2, 2026) — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/06/02/hdm-renewable-finance-rebrands-as-maxwell-power-raises-additional-funding/); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hdm-renewable-finance-rebrands-as-maxwell-power-302785812.html)
- Capital: $750M commitment from Fairtide Partners reported earlier; later report of an additional $800M bringing total to $1.8B; another source says total commitment exceeds $1B — figures conflict — [Mercom](https://mercomcapital.com/maxwell-power-secures-750-million-investment-from-fairtide-partners/); [Renewables Now](https://renewablesnow.com/news/maxwell-power-secures-usd-800m-from-for-consumer-solar-storage-1302263/); [Solar.com](https://www.solar.com/learn/hdm-prepaid-solar-ppa/)
- Products: Powerwall only, solar + Powerwall, or solar only — [solarwithwatts (installer marketing)](https://www.solarwithwatts.com/maxwell-power-prepaid-solar)
- Term / ownership: Maxwell describes 20 years of guaranteed power for a one-time payment; installer marketing says "own your solar + Powerwall free at year 6"; press release describes a six-year "full-protection" period (production guarantee, monitoring, insurance); Solar.com describes the category as having a Year-5 buyout at market value — sources conflict — [Solar.com](https://www.solar.com/learn/hdm-prepaid-solar-ppa/); [solarwithwatts](https://www.solarwithwatts.com/maxwell-power-prepaid-solar)
- Discount: installer marketing claims 30%+ off the standard price (marketing copy) — [solarwithwatts](https://www.solarwithwatts.com/maxwell-power-prepaid-solar)
- Channel: works mainly through sales/installation partners; Artemis is its core design-and-underwriting tech — [Artemis release](https://www.prweb.com/releases/artemis-partners-with-maxwell-power-previously-hdm-capital-and-becomes-their-core-design-and-underwriting-technology-for-solar-and-battery-projects-302836802.html)
- Going consumer-facing: HDM Capital LLC d/b/a Maxwell Power registered as a Texas retail electric broker (June 2026) and is relaunching as a full-service consumer power brand — [Energy Choice Matters](https://www.energychoicematters.com/stories/20260617a.html)
- (internal) Kin's LTO example in CLAUDE.md: "$24,000 at 23% (Maxwell)" — i.e., a 23% discount Kin applies on Maxwell deals; and "no Maxwell flip option for now" in the Cosmic notes — CLAUDE.md (2026-10-14, 2026-10-23)

### Inferences
- Maxwell is the most direct template for a Powerwall-only prepaid program and is San Diego-based (same market as Solar Review), so it is both benchmark and competitor.
- Moving to consumer-facing/retail-electric status suggests it may compete with its own dealers over time — a dealer-channel risk.

### Gaps
- Dealer program terms, redlines, funding milestones and timing: not found.
- Exact buyout/transfer year in the contract (5 vs 6) unresolved.

## 3. Other providers: battery-only leases, escalators, terms, dealer pay

### Takeaway
Battery-only leases exist (Palmetto LightReach "Energy Backup", Tesla's Texas Powerwall lease, Maxwell, Participate's 10-yr battery lease) but public pricing/dealer terms are thin; the industry dealer-payment pattern is a fixed $/W "EPC rate" paid in milestones with most money at M2 (PTO), with clawbacks if milestones slip.

### Cited Findings
- Palmetto LightReach Energy Backup: standalone battery lease, no upfront cost, fixed monthly payment; per-manufacturer battery price caps by utility plus a total system cap — [Enerflo help](https://help.enerflo.io/en/articles/14115492-lightreach-battery-only-leases-palmetto-energybackup)
- LightReach milestones: Sales/NTP (M0) and Installation (M1) named (Puerto Rico article); LightReach can cancel files not reaching M1 within 160 days and claw back funds on files not reaching M2 within 115 days; equipment bought via Direct Pay is netted against EPC payments; customer's first payment typically 30 days after PTO — [Palmetto help](https://help.palmetto.finance/en/articles/12316003-puerto-rico-lightreach-energy-plans-overview); [LightReach funding info](https://intercom.help/lightreachsolar/en/articles/8312684-general-funding-information); [LightReach Direct Pay guide](https://intercom.help/lightreachsolar/en/articles/9869445-solar-energy-plan-direct-pay-installer-guide)
- Energy Backup end-of-term options exist (details not in snippets) — [Palmetto help](https://help.palmetto.finance/en/articles/12070242-energy-backup-end-of-term-options)
- (internal) Solar Review's Axia/QCells redlines: LightReach $2.10/W, EnFin $1.95/W (DC credit baked in), Participate $2.19/W — CLAUDE.md
- General TPO dealer pay: finance partner pays a fixed $/W rate for a standard install that does not track actual build cost; M1 ≈ signing/install start, M2 ≈ PTO; most funds paid at M2, so M2 rejections hurt installer cash flow — [Enerflo lexicon](https://intercom.help/enerflo/en/articles/12805727-lexicon-solar-industry-terms); [Solar Builder](https://solarbuildermag.com/?p=56591); [Enerflo battery adder note](https://intercom.help/enerflo/en/articles/15926263-battery-adder-shows-negative-on-tpo-deals-but-positive-on-cash-deals)
- Tesla Powerwall-only lease (Texas, Aug 2026): advertised $35/mo is a net price; underlying lease ~$122/mo year 1 with 3% annual escalator; $35 requires Tesla dispatch rights and staying on Tesla Electric; 12-year term; retail-choice Texas only, no solar — [GadgetReview](https://www.gadgetreview.com/teslas-35-month-powerwall-lease-sounds-like-a-bargain-until-you-read-the-fine-print); [Basenor](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/tesla-powerwall-lease-launches-in-texas-5-details-that-matter); [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/08/texas-homeowners-can-lease-tesla-powerwall-batteries-straight-from-company/)
- Tesla national solar + Powerwall lease: 25-year term, payments start after activation/PTO (launched Oct 2025) — [Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3195/tesla-energy-launches-solar-and-powerwall-lease-program); [Tesla North](https://teslanorth.com/2025/10/02/tesla-launches-new-lease-option-for-solar-powerwall-in-u-s)
- Sunrun: >95% of sales via lease/subscription (secondary source); CEO reportedly said TPO >80% of 2026 originations — [solarguide](https://solarguide.pages.dev/articles/sunrun-vs-tesla-solar.html); [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/us-residential-solar-market-trends-2026)
- Escalators: 2026 PPA escalators typically 0–3.5%/yr (broker); 2–4%/yr with $150→$270 example (law-firm blog) — [solarwithwatts](https://www.solarwithwatts.com/power-purchase-agreements); [Prevost Law](https://blog.prevostlawfirm.com/everbright-solar-review/)
- EverBright: prepay remaining PPA balance any time / buy outright (review); a broker page says EverBright was "temporarily paused" (unverified) — [Prevost Law](https://blog.prevostlawfirm.com/everbright-solar-review/); [solarwithwatts](https://www.solarwithwatts.com/everbright-ppa)
- GoodLeap: broker describes a buyout path after year five; GoodLeap launched an HVAC lease marketed "with no surprise buyout" — [solarwithwatts](https://www.solarwithwatts.com/power-purchase-agreements); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/goodleap-launches-hvac-lease-to-give-homeowners-a-clear-path-to-ownership--with-no-surprise-buyout-302861242.html)

### Inferences
- A Solar Review program could differentiate on (a) predictable dealer pay timing tied to install, not just PTO, (b) no escalator, (c) a clear published buyout schedule after year 5/6, and (d) no dispatch-rights tie-in — the friction points visible in the incumbents above.
- "What share of system value the dealer receives" is set by the redline: dealer gross = price − redline; e.g., Solar Review's own battery deals (Revenue $13,950 vs Redline $12,500) leave ~10% gross margin (internal, CLAUDE.md), which shows how thin dealer economics are under Cosmic/Participate-style funding.

### Gaps
- Battery-only availability/terms for EverBright, GoodLeap, EnFin, Solrite, Shift: not found.
- Published M1/M2/M3 percentages for any provider (LightReach only partially), Sunnova's former dealer schedule: not found.

## 4. Failures and counterparty lessons

### Takeaway
In every recent failure, customers' leases/PPAs/loans survived and moved to a successor servicer (often SunStrong), but dealers were left as unsecured creditors (Sunnova owed dealers ~$367M) and installer workmanship warranties were often orphaned — the core lesson is that dealer receivables from a TPO counterparty are the exposed asset.

### Cited Findings
- Sunnova: filed Chapter 11 June 2025 to sell all assets and wind down; debtors estimated ~$367M owed to dealers; many dealers stopped building due to late payments; dealers/subcontractors tried to place mechanic's liens on customer homes (most dealer agreements bar this); dealer claims settled under the plan with a Creditor Trustee — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/case-summary-sunnova-energy-enters-chapter-11/)
- Sunnova customers: contracts not cancelled; assets sold to Solaris, servicing to SunStrong Management; SunStrong does not maintain loan or paid-off customers; reports of slow service; plan confirmed ~Nov 12, effective ~Nov 14, 2025 (sources differ) — [Helios](https://heliosenergyglobal.com/guides/sunnova-bankruptcy-who-services-sunnova-solar-california); [solarpanelexit](https://solarpanelexit.com/sunnova-bankruptcy/); [Texas Solar Cancellation](https://texassolarcancellation.com/sunnova-bankruptcy-what-it-means-for-your-solar-lease-ppa-or-loan/)
- PosiGen: Chapter 11 Nov 24, 2025 (S.D. Tex.), reportedly to stop primary lender Brookfield from seizing most customer assets; liquidating plan confirmed Feb 24, 2026, effective Feb 26, 2026; servicing moved to non-debtor project companies — most systems to Renewbrook Energy (Omnidian technical support), others to SunStrong, unfinished systems to GoodLeap (sources differ); claims bar date 2026-09-29 — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/12/08/residential-solar-installer-posigen-files-for-bankruptcy/); [Kroll docket doc](https://cases.ra.kroll.com/PosiGen/Home-DownloadPDF?id1=Mjc5NzYwNw%3D%3D&id2=-1); [solardisclosure](https://solardisclosure.io/articles/posigen-bankruptcy-renewbrook-who-services-my-system.html); [elevenflo](https://elevenflo.com/cases/76)
- Mosaic (lender): Chapter 11 June 6, 2025 (S.D. Tex.); reported emerged Sep 22, 2025 with servicing by Solar Servicing LLC (Forbright Bank) (tracker source) — [solarpanelexit tracker / NRG summary via search](https://solarpanelexit.com/solar-company-bankruptcy-list/)
- Freedom Forever: Chapter 11 Apr 15, 2026 (Del., 26-10522), converted to Chapter 7 (Jul 31 notice / Aug 7, 2026 effective, sources differ); Mosaic largest creditor (~$110–120M); court let finance partners hire new contractors to reach PTO; workmanship warranties not assumed by anyone — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/09/09/freedom-forever-bankruptcy-converted-to-chapter-7-liquidation-key-dates-set/); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/what-freedom-forevers-bankruptcy-says-about-residential-solar-today/); [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/freedom-forever-files-for-bankruptcy/)

### Inferences
- For a small lessor: customers will be protected by assignment of contracts to a servicer, but the lessor must pre-arrange a backup servicer and keep asset-level SPVs so a lender can't seize the whole book (PosiGen/Brookfield dynamic).
- For Solar Review as a dealer: concentration on one funder that "is not funding" is the exact Sunnova pattern; diversify funders or self-fund.

### Gaps
- Actual dealer recovery % in Sunnova/PosiGen settlements: not found.

## 5. Post-OBBBA market shift: TPO share 2025–2026

### Takeaway
25D ended Dec 31, 2025; TPO (which can still use 48E) is estimated at roughly 60–69% of 2026 residential installs vs ~44–45% in 2025, but these are secondary projections, not primary Wood Mackenzie/Ohm figures; total residential installs are forecast down ~18% in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- 25D ended Dec 31, 2025 under OBBBA; TPO providers still claim commercial 48E — [Artemis](https://artemispower.com/blog-posts/residential-solar-2026-solar-tax-credit-installers); [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/us-solar-tax-credit-2026-guide)
- TPO share projections: 69% in 2026 vs 45% in 2025 (GoSolo); 60–69% (LeadGen Economy); 65% vs 44% (vahorizon) — [GoSolo](https://gosolo.io/blog/2025-solar-market-outlook-post-25d/); [LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/first-quarter-without-25d-solar-credit-cpl-reset/); [vahorizon](https://www.vahorizon.site/solar/statistics/market/)
- SEIA/Wood Mackenzie forecast ~18% drop in 2026 residential installs (secondary) — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/us-residential-solar-market-trends-2026)
- Wood Mackenzie (primary, older): customer-owned 54% in 2024; TPO >50% for first time since 2016 in a 2024 quarter; expects customer ownership to regain majority from 2027 — [Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/us-residential-solar-turbulence-persisted-through-2024/)
- Ohm Analytics: TPO 43% in Q3 2024 (56% in 12 key TPO markets) — [Solar Builder](https://solarbuildermag.com/news/residential-solar-declined-32-percent-in-q3-is-recovery-on-the-way/)
- EnergySage: installers quoting TPO rose ~14% → 41% Jan–Jun 2026 (quotes, not installs); residential prices +3.2% in H1 2026 as TPO gains (pv magazine, Oct 23 2026, headline only) — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/24/residential-solar-prices-rise-3-2-as-third-party-ownership-gains-ground/)
- Aurora 2026 Solar Snapshot reports shift toward TPO — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/25/aurora-solar-releases-2026-solar-snapshot-report-detailing-shift-toward-third-party-ownership)
- New entrants/expanders: Tesla direct leases (Oct 2025 national; Aug 2026 Texas Powerwall-only); Maxwell Power ($1B+ commitments, consumer brand); Participate expanding states; GoodLeap HVAC lease — sources above

### Inferences
- Demand for a battery lease is structurally rising because cash/loan buyers lost 30%; a small lessor that can capture 48E (and the energy-community/domestic-content adders where they apply) has the same price edge the incumbents have.

### Gaps
- Primary Wood Mackenzie/SEIA or Ohm 2026 TPO share: not found. California- and Utah-specific TPO shares: not found.

## 6. Small/regional lessors that self-fund

### Takeaway
No credible U.S. example of a small regional installer self-funding its own residential lease book was found; general sources say this segment is too small for institutional tax equity and too large for plain bank loans, which is the gap a small lessor must solve.

### Cited Findings
- C&I guide: small projects are "too small to attract institutional tax equity, too large for a standard bank construction loan"; direct ownership gives the best lifetime economics but shifts performance, O&M and technology risk to the owner; commercial solar loan rates ~4.5–9% — [Tactical Energy Group](https://tac-nrg.com/guides/ci-solar-leases-and-loans)
- Under IFRS 16 many PPAs are leases on the balance sheet (accounting note, non-U.S. GAAP) — [World Kinect](https://www.world-kinect.com/news-insights/financing-onsite-solar-comparing-ownership-vs-third-party-models)
- Only analog found: Orb Energy (India) in-house SME financing from 2016 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orb_Energy)

### Inferences
- A Solar Review lessor would likely need: an SPV owning the Powerwalls, a small warehouse line or private capital, a tax-credit transfer buyer (48E transferability) rather than tax equity, and a backup servicer — but none of these structures were evidenced by search here.

### Gaps
- Real U.S. examples (size, funding source) of small self-funded residential lessors; costs of 48E credit transfer for small portfolios; warehouse lenders active at sub-$5M scale — all unanswered; a targeted search (e.g., "48E transferability small portfolio", "solar warehouse facility small installer") is recommended.
