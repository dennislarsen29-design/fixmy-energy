# Capital stack for a small residential battery leasing company (Solar Review Corp, 2026)

Scope: how a very small California S-Corp could own and lease 20–50 Tesla Powerwall 3 systems (~$13k–$18k installed each) and monetize the federal 48E ITC by credit transfer (Section 6418) instead of tax equity. Not financial, tax or legal advice. Research limits: page fetches failed from this sandbox (pv-magazine-usa.com and cruxclimate.com would not resolve), so most findings below come from search-result summaries rather than full documents. Treat all figures as indicative and confirm against the primary source before relying on them.

## 1. Is the credit still available for a leased home battery in 2026?

### Takeaway
Probably yes. Commentary on the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, July 2025) says Section 25D (homeowner-owned) ended for expenses after 2025, but a business that owns and leases the battery can still claim the 48E credit. Standalone storage keeps the full credit if construction begins before 2033. FEOC (foreign entity of concern) sourcing rules apply from 2026. Verify the details against the statute and IRS guidance.

### Cited Findings
- 25D repeal applies to expenses incurred after Dec 31, 2025; residential systems leased from third parties remain eligible through 48E — [Grant Thornton OBBBA alert](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/alerts/tax/2025/insights/energy-incentives-under-obbba-what-you-need-to-know); [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/oil-gas-electricity/1647826/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-becomes-law-high-level-summary-of-renewable-energy-tax-provisions)
- The leased-residential exclusion in the final bill covers only solar water heating property and small wind property, not storage or PV — [Rehmann](https://www.rehmann.com/resource/accelerated-phase-outs-and-new-restrictions-the-obbbs-reshaping-of-energy-credits/); [JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/one-big-beautiful-bill-signed-into-law-4153182)
- Storage: full credit if construction begins before 2033; one summary gives 75% in 2034, 50% in 2035, 0% from 2036; another says fully phased out by 2035 (sources differ) — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/?p=18305); [RER summary](https://www.rer.org/wp-content/uploads/Clean-Energy-Tax-Incentives-and-the-One-Big-Beautiful-Bill-OB3-Act.pdf)
- Solar PV (not storage) under 48E must begin construction by July 4, 2026 or be placed in service by end-2027; one vendor blog wrongly implies this deadline applies broadly — [Nuwatt Energy (vendor)](https://nuwattenergy.com/en/solar-lease-ppa-2026/section-48-deadline-guide); [Solar Permit Solutions (vendor)](https://www.solarpermitsolutions.com/blog/solar-battery-tax-credit-2026)
- FEOC rules apply to 48E projects from 2026; one vendor blog cites a 55% non-prohibited-foreign-entity content threshold for storage in 2026 (unverified) — [Solar Permit Solutions](https://www.solarpermitsolutions.com/blog/solar-battery-tax-credit-2026); [JD Supra FEOC framework](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/understanding-the-obbba-s-feoc-framework-8431935)

### Inferences
- A standalone Powerwall 3 lease is the cleaner credit story than PV in 2026: storage keeps the base 30% well past this decade, while PV faces the 2026/2027 cliff.
- FEOC compliance for Powerwall 3 (cell sourcing) is the biggest eligibility risk. Get a written FEOC / material-assistance certification from Tesla or the distributor before relying on the credit; credit buyers and insurers will ask for it.
- Bonus adders (energy community, low-income) were not researched here; assume the base 30% only.

### Gaps
- No primary source (statute text, IRS notice) was read. The storage phase-down years and the FEOC threshold for 2026 storage are unconfirmed.
- Whether Powerwall 3 meets the 2026 FEOC material-assistance test was not found.

## 2. Tax equity vs. Section 6418 credit transfer vs. hybrid

### Takeaway
Tax equity is out of reach at 20–50 systems (~$0.3–0.9M of hardware, ~$0.1–0.27M of credits). Credit transfer is the realistic path, but even transfer has a practical floor around $250k of credits and small deals price lower. Hybrid (T-flip) needs a tax equity partner, so it is also out of reach at this size.

### Cited Findings
- Tax equity: minimum deals historically started at $25–50M with specialized counsel; many investors won't work with portfolios under ~3 MW; average 2023 deals were ~$100M (older sources) — [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2024/12/tax-transferability-a-win-for-the-little-guys-in-solar/); [Yale Clean Energy Forum](https://cleanenergyforum.yale.edu/2022/05/16/current-challenges-to-tax-equity-part-three); [CohnReznick (2017)](https://www.ourenergypolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/CohnReznick_USRenewableEnergyBrief_TaxEquityLandscape.pdf)
- Tax equity commits ~18 months ahead; ITC deals now carry ~45% of the capital stack; 2026 outlook lists traditional tax equity, hybrid, preferred equity and direct credit sales as the monetization routes — [Norton Rose Fulbright, Cost of Capital 2026 Outlook](https://www.projectfinance.law/publications/cost-of-capital-2026-outlook)
- 2025 tax equity + transfer volume roughly $45–50B — [Currents podcast summary of NRF outlook](https://pod.wave.co/podcast/currents/ep331-cost-of-capital-2026-outlook)
- Transfer market: up to $21B in H1 2026; Q2 2026 a Crux record $14.9B; full-year 2026 forecasts conflict ($49B vs earlier $64–69.5B) — [pv-tech H1 2026](https://www.pv-tech.org/us-clean-energy-tax-credit-transfers-us21-billion-h1-2026-crux/); [pv-tech up to $49B](https://www.pv-tech.org/clean-energy-tax-credit-transfers-fall-crux-us-49-billion/); [Crux 2025 report](https://www.cruxclimate.com/blog/cruxs-2025-market-intelligence-report-what-tax-credit-buyers-need-to-know)
- Most buyers are large corporations needing $10–20M of credits to "move the needle"; aggregators expected to pool smaller deals over 3–5 years (May 2024) — [Morgan Lewis](https://morganlewis.com/news/2024/05/clean-energy-tax-credit-market-poised-to-grow-under-new-transferability-rules)
- Practical minimum ~$250k of credits; pricing 70–90 cents (vendor guide, unverified) — [Veckta](https://veckta.com/selling-tax-credits/)
- Smallest public example: Basis Climate closed a $600k ITC transfer (1.2 MW Florida project, WeWould Solar to Creditable Capital); pv magazine estimated ~85–87 cents — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2024/06/25/solar-tax-transfer-for-smaller-projects-dissecting-a-600000-tax-credit-transaction); [Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/solar/how-low-can-you-go-basis-climate-closes-one-of-the-smallest-itc-deals-on-1-2-mw-florida-solar-project/)
- For a ~$1M solar credit, sale price 85–95 cents but net >80 cents after costs — [Veckta 2026](https://veckta.com/2026/02/17/selling-solar-tax-credits-why-sell-when-to-sell-and-navigating-the-process/)
- Price per credit dollar rises with deal size; some 2024–25 residential portfolio pricing was depressed by forced sales from distressed residential solar companies — [pv magazine USA (Crux mid-2025)](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/09/23/solar-and-storage-rising-as-wind-fades-in-20-billion-clean-energy-tax-credit-market/)
- Q1 2026: overall pricing roughly flat from Q4 2025; the residential ITC market "struggling" — [Crux 1Q2026 Q&A](https://www.cruxclimate.com/blog/1q2026-market-update-qa-josh-price)
- Storage ITCs historically priced highest among clean energy types (older Crux data) — [Energy-Storage.news](https://energy-storage.news/energy-storage-tax-credits-in-us-priced-highest-among-all-clean-energy-types)
- Residential = 16.3% of solar credits sold in H1 2025 — [ESS News (Crux)](https://www.ess-news.com/?p=6683)
- Buyers' main residential concern is 5-year recapture (e.g., one or two houses in a portfolio) — [Crux Connects / Orrick](https://www.cruxclimate.com/blog/crux-connects-residential-solar-tax-credits); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2025/01/A-Conversation-with-Crux-Residential-Solar-Tax-Credits-Offer-Risk-and-Reward)
- Aggregators / marketplaces: Basis Climate (marketplace with embedded insurance products), Crux (marketplace and data), Creditable Capital (buyer on the $600k deal) — [Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/solar/how-low-can-you-go-basis-climate-closes-one-of-the-smallest-itc-deals-on-1-2-mw-florida-solar-project/)

### Inferences
- Scale math: 50 systems × ~$15k × 30% ≈ $225k of credits, right at the ~$250k practical floor; 20–25 systems (~$90–110k) is below it. Expect worse pricing (plausibly high-70s to low-80s cents net after legal, insurance and diligence costs) than the 85–95 cent headline. Batch one year's systems into a single sale.
- Easiest route for a sub-$250k batch: sell through an installer-finance partner or aggregator that pools credits, or wait until a vintage reaches ~$250k+.
- Self-use alternative: the S-Corp can keep the credit, but it flows to the shareholder. Equipment leasing is generally a "rental activity" under passive-loss rules, so the credit may only offset passive-income tax unless an exception applies. This is an inference from general tax law, not a sourced finding; confirm with the CPA before planning on self-use.
- Depreciation: the lessor also gets depreciation (basis reduced by half the ITC). Whether 100% bonus depreciation applies in 2026 was not researched here.

### Gaps
- No 2026 residential-storage cents-per-dollar figure found; Crux's full mid-year tables would have it.
- No named aggregator with a stated minimum for residential storage portfolios (Reunion, Atlas and others not found in results).

## 3. Timing, insurance and buyer diligence

### Takeaway
A credit buyer usually pays after the systems are placed in service, sometimes as late as the buyer's tax-payment dates (e.g., March 15 of the following year). A contracted forward sale can support a bridge loan at a high advance rate. Buyers commonly require tax credit insurance, often paid by the seller, at ~2–3% one-time premium.

### Cited Findings
- Transfer bridge loans are repaid from transfer proceeds once credits are generated and sold; buyer payment may not come until March 15 of the year after placed-in-service — [Crux on bridge loans](https://www.cruxclimate.com/blog/tax-credit-bridge-loans-in-clean-energy-capital-markets); [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Transferable-Inflation-Reduction-Act-IRA-Tax-Credits-Considerations-for-Tax-Credit-Bridge-Lenders-1.pdf)
- "Naked" bridge (no signed transfer agreement) vs "contracted" bridge (secured by signed agreement); advance rates generally near 100% with a committed creditworthy buyer — [Norton Rose / Strafford webinar](https://www.projectfinance.law/media/5921/strafford-tax-equity-bridge-loan-webinar-nrf-_12925.pdf); [Latham](https://www.lw.com/admin/upload/SiteAttachments/Tax-Credit-Transfer-Bridge-Loans-Structuring-Issues-and-Considerations.pdf)
- Tax credit insurance: typically a one-time premium of ~2–3% of maximum coverage; often paid by the seller; covering 100% of credits may be insufficient due to interest and penalties — [Norton Rose Tax Equity News](https://www.projectfinance.law/tax-equity-news/tax-credit-insurance-mitigates-risk-for-tax-credit-transfers-but-due-diligence-is-still-necessary/); [Crux insurance guide](https://www.cruxclimate.com/insights/tax-credit-insurance-coverage-cost-and-how-to-procure); [Vanbridge](https://www.vanbridge.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Tax-Credit-Insurance_Vanbridge_fnl-1.pdf)
- Final transferability regulations shift recapture risk to buyers, which drives indemnity and insurance demands — [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tax-insights-and-commentary/new-clean-energy-tax-credit-rules-transfer-deal-risks-to-buyers); [Weil final regs summary](https://www.weil.com/-/media/mailings/2024/q2/treasury-and-irs-release-final-regulations-on-credit-transferability-24-05-20.pdf)

### Inferences
- Insurance has minimum premiums and underwriting costs that weigh more on a $100–250k deal than the 2–3% rate suggests; budget legal + insurance + diligence at several percent of credit value.
- Buyers will ask for: placed-in-service evidence per system, cost basis (invoices, cost segregation for basis), lease contracts, FEOC certification, proof the lessor owns the system, and a recapture plan (what happens if a home sells or the lease ends in years 1–5).
- Recapture risk drivers for home batteries: customer buyout or transfer of ownership within 5 years, system removal, lessor insolvency. Lease terms should prevent title passing to the homeowner within 5 years.

### Gaps
- No source on current premiums or minimum premiums for small residential portfolios.
- Treasury rules on prepayment for forward sales were not confirmed.

## 4. Debt options for a small originator

### Takeaway
Big-platform tools (warehouse lines, securitization, back-leverage) are sized in the hundreds of millions and are not available to a 20–50 system company. Realistic debt for a pilot is a small business term loan or equipment line, a CDFI or credit union loan (possibly with an IBank guarantee), or a contracted credit-transfer bridge once a buyer is signed.

### Cited Findings
- Warehouse advance rates are set off downside-case cash flows and recalculated quarterly; SolarCity's "ITC downside case" assumed a 30% fair-market-value haircut (rates redacted in filings) — [Vivint Solar 10-Q exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001607716/000156459017009845/vslr-ex101_474.htm); [SolarCity 10-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001408356/000156459017003084/scty-ex1019aa_1258.htm)
- Example scale: Vivint $325M warehouse (accordion to $400M); $200M asset facility tied to safe-harbor purchases — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/?p=89260); [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/?p=91902)
- California IBank Climate Catalyst Fund lists "Distributed Generation and Storage" (including stand-alone storage) as a priority sector, but an IBank FAQ says no funding is currently available; IBank guarantees/participations (up to 80% of a CDFI or bank loan under $5M) work through banks, credit unions and CDFIs — [IBank products](https://cdn.ibank.ca.gov/climate-financing/our-products/); [IBank sectors](https://ibank.ca.gov/climate-financing/sectors-of-focus)
- SBA 7(a): eligible uses include buying or leasing equipment; SOP 50 10 8 (June 2025) and 50 10 8.1 (Oct 1, 2026) govern; passive businesses are generally ineligible unless structured as an Eligible Passive Company. No source settled whether an equipment lessor to consumers qualifies — [SOP 50 10 8](https://colemanreport.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/SOP-50-10-8-effective-6.1.2025-Final.pdf); [Starfield & Smith](https://starfieldsmith.com/2025/08/best-practices-sba-loan-program-eligibility-key-points-for-sba-lenders/)

### Inferences
- Solar Review's operating installer business (revenue, history) is the borrower a bank or SBA lender will underwrite; a separate leasing SPV with no history will struggle to borrow on its own.
- Wheelhouse CU already finances Solar Review's customers; ask whether it would offer a business equipment line or participate with an IBank/CDFI guarantee.

### Gaps
- No current small-originator warehouse or ITC bridge pricing found (rates, advance %, minimum sizes).
- SBA treatment of consumer equipment lessors unconfirmed.
- No research on green banks beyond IBank (e.g., Coalition for Green Capital network, GGRF/NCIF recipients) or CPACE (CPACE is for commercial property and likely not applicable to residential leases).

## 5. Crowdfunding and private raises

### Takeaway
Small-project platforms exist but mostly fund their own portfolios or C&I loans. A Reg CF or Reg D raise of a few hundred thousand dollars is feasible in principle but adds securities compliance and investor reporting.

### Cited Findings
- Wunder Capital: Reg D, accredited investors, $1,000 minimum, funds solar loans to small businesses and institutions — [YieldTalk](https://yieldtalk.com/wunder-review/); [Newfi profile](https://securities.gitbook.io/newfi/investment-platforms-1/crowdfunding/wunder-capital)
- Energea: Reg CF and Reg A+, $100 minimum per portfolio, prior Reg A compliance issues noted — [AltStreet review](https://altstreet.investments/platforms/reviews/energea)
- Raise Green: solar/storage/efficiency investments; reportedly acquired by Honeycomb Credit (unconfirmed) — [Greentown Labs](https://greentownlabs.com/members/raise-green/); [Yale CBEY](https://community.cbey.yale.edu/news/24662)

### Inferences
- Friends-and-family or accredited-investor notes (Reg D 506(b)) secured on lease receivables are the simplest private option; securities counsel needed.

## 6. How the large players raise capital (reference scale)

## 7. Self-funding math and recommended stages

### Takeaway
At ~$15k per system, the credit is worth ~$4.5k, which nets ~$3.5–4.0k after a small-deal discount and costs, arriving months after install. With a prepaid lease (customer pays ~70% upfront, mirroring Participate's 30%-discount model), cash tied up per system is small; with a monthly lease it is ~$11k+ per system for years.

### Cited Findings
- Credit pricing and timing inputs: see sections 2–3 (80–95 cent range, payment possibly as late as March 15 of the following year).

### Inferences (illustrative arithmetic, not sourced figures)
- Per system at $15,000 installed cost: 30% ITC = $4,500; at 80–85 cents net ≈ $3,600–3,825.
- Prepaid lease (customer pays 70% = $10,500 at install): cash out $15,000 − $10,500 = $4,500, recovered by the credit sale (~$3,700) → net cash at risk ~$800 per system until the credit is sold, plus any margin built into the price. 25 systems ≈ $20k net, but ~$112k of gross float until the credit sale closes (bridge or own cash).
- Monthly lease (no prepay): cash out $15,000, credit ~$3,700 back after 3–15 months, the rest repaid over the lease term → ~$11k per system long-term; 25 systems ≈ $280k, 50 ≈ $565k. This is where debt is required.
- Recommended stages:
  - **Pilot (20–50 systems):** prepaid-lease product; fund the float from operating cash or a small bank/credit-union/CDFI line; sell the vintage's credits in one batch through a marketplace (Basis/Crux) or a partner aggregator once ≥ ~$150–250k; buy tax credit insurance only if the buyer requires it; document FEOC compliance and title retention for 5 years.
  - **Scale (100–500 systems; ~$0.45–2.25M of credits per year):** forward credit-sale agreement with a named buyer + contracted bridge loan at high advance rate; add monthly-lease product funded by an equipment/receivables facility from a specialty lender; consider a fund/SPV per vintage.
  - **Beyond:** a warehouse line or partnership with an established platform (LightReach, Participate, EverBright) instead of building one.

### Gaps
- Real small-deal net pricing, insurance minimum premiums, and lender terms for a sub-$1M originator were not found and should be quoted directly from Basis, Crux and a specialty lender.
- The passive-activity treatment of retained credits and the 2026 bonus-depreciation status need CPA confirmation.
