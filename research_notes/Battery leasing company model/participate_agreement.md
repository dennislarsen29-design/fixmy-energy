## What exactly does the customer pay, when, and what do they get?

### Takeaway
It is a **prepaid lease with $0 monthly payments**: the customer pays one "Net Amount Due" (Baseline Lease Amount minus incentives the System Owner receives) in two progress payments made to the installer as Participate's collection agent, and gets the right to use the system (battery term 10 years, PV term 25 years) with Participate-provided maintenance, monitoring and warranty labor. No production or savings guarantee; no escalator.

### Cited Findings
- **Structure:** "This is a Prepaid Lease. You will make progress payments totaling the Net Amount Due during the installation phase. Monthly Lease Payments: $0.00" — [PE p.8](file:///root/.claude/uploads/770fd2b9-36aa-5fea-be24-88904b143f5e/42bc33f7-CA_-_5_Day_Participate_Energy_-_Customer_Agreement__Installation_v.1.0_-_20260601.pdf). Lessor is "Participate Holdings, LLC" (Claymont, DE) — p.3, p.9.
- **Price formula:** Baseline Lease Amount − "Value of Included Incentives (if available)" ("Upfront Discount for State incentives", "Upfront Discount for utility incentives") = Net Amount Due; "Included Incentives are received by System Owner and provide additional discounts to the Net Amount Due" — p.9; §3.1 "The Baseline Lease Amount will be further reduced by $___ for incentives received by System Owner ('Included Incentives')"; add-on products adjust the Baseline amount — p.12–13.
- **Who receives the money:** "Customer shall remit the Net Amount Due directly to the Installer, on System Owner's behalf" (§3.1) — p.13; Exhibit A: "payments must be made directly to the Installer, acting solely as System Owner's agent for collection" — p.29.
- **Schedule:** Exhibit A has two progress payments; "Payment #1 shall not exceed the lesser of one thousand dollars ($1,000.00) or ten percent (10%) of the Net Amount Due" — p.29 (mirrors CSLB notice on p.2). First payment due "Upon execution of the Agreement (Subject to statutory limits)" — p.8. Payment trigger events are left blank in the template ("Due Upon") — p.29.
- **No escalator / not a PPA:** lease escalator and PPA fields marked "N/A" — p.4–5.
- **Optional financing of the prepayment:** the customer "may be choosing to finance the Net Amount Due"; "Installer is not legally affiliated with any lender" — p.3. §6.3.3: if a third-party "Capital Provider" finances the lease amounts and Participate approved a lien (e.g. UCC-1), Participate may **disable the system** once the loan is 60+ days past due; after the 5th anniversary of placed-in-service the Capital Provider may repossess — p.16–17. Checkbox "A Capital Provider ☐ IS ☐ IS NOT issuing a loan" — p.17.
- **Other charges:** transaction/sales taxes extra (§6.3.1); late payments accrue "the lesser of twelve percent (12%) annually or the maximum allowable" (§6.3.2) — p.16. Property tax: customer pays real-property tax; Participate pays the initial personal-property tax on the system "however, you agree to reimburse us for these taxes if we invoice you"; customer pays any new/increased taxes (§6.4) — p.17.
- **Hell-or-high-water:** obligation to pay is "ABSOLUTE AND UNCONDITIONAL … NOT … SUBJECT TO ABATEMENT, DEFENSE, COUNTERCLAIM, SETOFF" including by "HEIRS AND ESTATE" (§6.8) — p.18.
- **Term:** begins on the "System Installation Date" (utility activation) and ends "ten (10) years thereafter for Batteries ('Battery Term')" and "twenty-five (25) years thereafter for PV Systems" (§2.1) — p.12; same on p.3 and p.11.
- **What the customer gets:** "limited leasehold right to use the System and receive the Electricity it produces" (§9.1) — p.19; backup power during outages "If configured to do so" (§5.1) — p.14; 24/7 monitoring at no cost (Exh. B §5(a)) — p.31; maintenance/repair (see O&M below).
- **No guarantee:** "WE DO NOT WARRANT OR GUARANTEE THE AMOUNT OF ENERGY PRODUCED OR STORED … OR THAT YOU WILL REALIZE ANY SAVINGS" (§3.4) — p.13; "System Owner IS NOT guaranteeing these savings" — p.11.
- **Customer duties that cost money:** keep a working internet connection "within eighty (80) feet of the System's AC/DC inverter(s)" at customer cost (§6.1(xv)); trim trees; not use the system "to heat a swimming pool" — p.15–16. Optional (not included) shade management and panel cleaning — p.11.
- **Scope changes:** if design changes by more than "a three percent (3%) decrease or a ten percent (10%) increase" or the Net Amount Due changes, installer re-papers terms and the customer has "seven (7) days … to reject" (IO §7) — p.39.
- **Installer contract price is $0:** "The contract price for the goods and/or services performed in connection with this Installation Agreement is $0.00" (IO §5) — p.38; restated in Annex 1 "Effect on Total Payment and Deal Structure" — p.52.

### Inferences
- For a battery-only Powerwall 3 lease, Participate's economics are: customer prepays the discounted price up front; Participate owns the asset and monetizes tax benefits and grid services over the term. Customer cash-flow profile looks like a cash purchase at a discount, which is why reps market it as "lease to own."
- Routing payment through the installer as "agent for collection" plus a $0 installer contract price keeps the installer's CSLB down-payment/progress-payment rules intact while making Participate the only party the customer owes.

### Gaps
- Actual dollar amounts, discount percentage and Exhibit A trigger events are blank in this template.
- How the Baseline Lease Amount is computed (installer price vs. Participate price) is not stated.

## When and how can the customer buy the system, and how is the price set?

### Takeaway
Early purchase is available only **on the 6th anniversary and each anniversary after**, at any time after year 6 when selling the home, or if Participate ceases operations without a substitute; price = **fair market value minus remaining obligations owed to the customer, floor $0**, with an independent appraiser only if the parties disagree. The FMV method is not defined.

### Cited Findings
- §10: "(i) on the six (6) year anniversary of the beginning of the Term and every annual anniversary after the six (6) year anniversary; and (ii) at any time after the six (6) year anniversary … when you sell your Property; and (iii) if we ever cease our operations and fail to provide for a substitute System provider." Requires no ongoing Customer Default and "at least one (1) month, but not more than three (3) months' prior written notice" — [PE p.21](file:///root/.claude/uploads/770fd2b9-36aa-5fea-be24-88904b143f5e/42bc33f7-CA_-_5_Day_Participate_Energy_-_Customer_Agreement__Installation_v.1.0_-_20260601.pdf).
- §10.2 price: "the System's fair market value ('FMV') and offset by the value of any remaining obligations owed to you by System Owner … resulting in a price no less than $0. If you and the System Owner cannot agree upon FMV, a third-party independent appraiser will be retained" — p.21. Disclosure: "price will be fair market value minus any offsetting obligations owed by System Owner at a number no less than $0.00" — p.10.
- No appraisal method, appraiser selection process, or cost allocation is specified (p.21). Note the section numbering jumps from §10 to §10.2 (no §10.1) — p.21.
- §10.3: after purchase "all of our obligations to you … shall terminate"; Installation Order obligations survive and transfer — p.21. Warranties assigned on purchase "may exclude coverage for … labor costs" and "have remaining coverage periods that are shorter than the remaining Term" (§7) — p.19; Exh. B §2(b) manufacturer warranties transfer — p.30.
- End of term (§2.3, keyed to the **PV Term**): renew "for additional one-year terms at the then-current fair rental rate"; purchase under §10; or terminate and Participate removes the system — p.12; disclosure says terminate "at no cost to you" — p.10.
- Battery-specific end: after the 10-year Battery Term, "System Owner may remove such Battery at any time" and keeps any Grid Services revenue; "you will not have any rights to receive power from the Battery" (§2.2) — p.12. §5.2: after Battery Term, "if the Battery has not been replaced or purchased by you, System Owner shall thereafter be entitled to retain 100% of all net proceeds" — p.14.
- **Comparison (search snippets only, not verified against primary documents):** EnergySage describes Participate's buyout as "fair market value at the time of request, minus credits for Participate's future obligations" and says the year-six price is not guaranteed — [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/participate-energy-review/); Aurora's help docs say the buyout can start as early as year 6 — [Aurora](https://help.aurorasolar.com/hc/en-us/articles/53645414364435-How-to-configure-the-Participate-Energy-integration); some marketing says "after year five" — [solar.com](https://solar.com/financing/). Industry guides say prepaid-lease contracts typically transfer "at fair market value … 'in good faith'" and that buyouts typically follow "the 6-year federal tax credit recapture period" — [EnergySage prepaid guide](https://www.energysage.com/solar/pre-paid-solar-leases-and-ppas), [solar.com lease-to-own](https://solar.com/learn/lease-to-own-solar-panels/). Tesla's support page (snippet) describes a lease buyout "after year five" — [Tesla](https://tesla.com/support/energy/solar-panels/learn/leasing-solar). A forum anecdote reports a year-6 FMV buyout quote over $16k on a lease — [SolarPanelTalk](https://www.solarpaneltalk.com/forum/solar-panels-for-home/solar-panels-for-your-home/421313-ppa-end-of-year-5-buyout-option-worth-it-or-prepay-or-keep-the-lease) (anecdotal).

### Inferences
- The FMV-with-$0-floor buyout is standard for tax-equity leases; the "minus remaining obligations" offset is what lets a seller tell customers the year-6 buyout should be small/near zero for a fully prepaid system, but nothing in the contract guarantees that.
- For a 10-year battery term, the year-6 window means the customer prepays and then waits ≥6 years to own; at year 10 the battery reverts (owner may remove it) unless bought. A Solar Review program would need its own explicit battery end-of-term option, since Participate's §2.3 options are tied to the PV term.

### Gaps
- No defined FMV methodology (cost vs. income approach), appraiser choice, or who pays the appraiser.
- No stated buyout price schedule or illustrative value.

## Which party keeps which incentives, and what does that imply about Participate's revenue model?

### Takeaway
Participate keeps **all** tax credits, depreciation, RECs, utility rebates and grid-service rights, except those listed in Exhibit C. Grid-service (VPP) revenue is split **30% customer / 70% Participate** when Participate developed the opportunity, **70% / 30%** when it didn't (Participate decides which), and **100% Participate** after the Battery Term or if the customer enrolls on their own. The customer must sign utility/rebate/VPP paperwork in their own name and assign the benefit.

### Cited Findings
- "Any state or federal tax credits generated by the System WILL NOT be owned by you but WILL be owned by System Owner"; RECs likewise "unless otherwise included in Exhibit C" — [PE p.11](file:///root/.claude/uploads/770fd2b9-36aa-5fea-be24-88904b143f5e/42bc33f7-CA_-_5_Day_Participate_Energy_-_Customer_Agreement__Installation_v.1.0_-_20260601.pdf).
- §9.2.1 "System Interests": "(i) all federal, state, and local tax credits, deductions, and allowances; (ii) all renewable energy credits … (iii) all utility rebates, grants, or incentives … and (iv) all rights to participate in Grid Services or demand response programs … unless otherwise stated in Exhibit C" — p.19.
- §9.3.1(ii): customer executes "interconnection agreements, net-metering agreements, Grid Service related agreements, and REC registrations … in the name of the Customer solely to comply with utility/regulatory requirements, and the ultimate benefit … is assigned to System Owner"; (i) customer may not grant any third party rights to monetize System Interests — p.20. Return incentive forms "within seven (7) days" (§6.1(xiv)) — p.16. Installer gets power of attorney for "INCENTIVE AND REBATE APPLICATIONS" — p.38.
- Incentives received by the owner flow to the customer only as the upfront "Included Incentives" discount (p.9, §3.1 p.12). §3.2: Additional Incentives paid to the customer are in Exhibit C, but "if the economic benefit of any Additional Incentive is not received by System Owner, then there is no additional discount" — p.13.
- Exhibit C: incentives may be "first come, first served" and flagged "At-Risk"; if the customer doesn't qualify or funds run out, "either of us may cancel your order any time up to 14 calendar days prior to the planned System installation without penalty"; table columns include "Beneficiary" — p.33 (table blank in template).
- §5.2 Grid Services: owner may "automatically and without additional consent, enroll" the system in "any grid service, virtual power plant, demand response program"; customer receives "30% of all net proceeds" for owner-developed programs, "70%" for programs "not partially or fully developed by System Owner"; "System Owner shall be entitled to determine, at its sole discretion" which applies; owner ensures "your overall electricity costs … shall not increase"; if customer enrolls without consent "100% of the Grid Service proceeds shall be paid to the System Owner"; settings "paused" during events — p.14. Owner controls charge/discharge "in our sole discretion" subject to customer app preferences (§5.1) — p.14. Exh. B §5(c) owner may change enrollment/providers "at the System Owner's sole discretion" — p.31.
- Default damages explicitly value **lost grid-service income** ("discounted to net present value at a discount rate of 3%") and **ITC and 5-year accelerated depreciation** recapture "on a net after tax basis assuming a tax rate of 35%" (§14.2(viii)) — p.24.
- **Not mentioned anywhere in the agreement:** SGIP, SDCP, Domestic Content, energy community, §48E/§25D by name, or a credit check (text search of the full document found none). Incentives are referenced only generically ("State incentives", "utility incentives", Exhibit C).
- External (snippet): consumer guides report the Participate discount is "often 30–35%" / "up to 30%" — [solar.com guide](https://www.solar.com/learn/participate-energys-prepaid-lease-a-consumer-guide/), [solalt](https://www.solalt.com/?p=7540).

### Inferences
- Revenue model: (1) federal ITC on the commercial (third-party-owned) side plus 5-year MACRS depreciation, monetized by Participate/its tax-equity partners; (2) state/utility rebates (e.g. SGIP or SDCP in California, though unnamed) captured and partly passed back as the upfront discount; (3) ongoing VPP/demand-response revenue at a 70% (or 30%) owner share, rising to 100% after the battery term; (4) residual value via year-6+ FMV buyouts or post-term renewals at "fair rental rate". The upfront discount (~30%) is roughly the size of the ITC, consistent with passing most of the credit to the customer and keeping depreciation, grid services and residual.
- Because Participate decides at its "sole discretion" whether a VPP was owner-developed, the 30/70 split is effectively Participate's call; a Solar Review program could compete by fixing the customer share.
- The absence of domestic-content / energy-community language suggests any adder value stays inside Participate's tax economics rather than being contractually shared.

### Gaps
- Whether SDCP/SGIP are typically "Included Incentives" (owner-received) or Exhibit C (customer-paid) cannot be determined from the blank template.
- Which federal credit section and adders Participate claims is not stated.

## Which clauses protect the lessor's tax position?

### Takeaway
The agreement is engineered to remain a true lease for tax purposes: purchase is blocked until year 6, price is FMV (not a nominal/fixed amount), the system is declared the owner's personal property and "not a fixture", the customer disclaims all ownership, and the most drastic remedies (lender termination on foreclosure, capital-provider repossession) only open after year 5. Default damages include grossed-up ITC and depreciation recapture.

### Cited Findings
- Early purchase only from the "six (6) year anniversary" (§10) — [PE p.21](file:///root/.claude/uploads/770fd2b9-36aa-5fea-be24-88904b143f5e/42bc33f7-CA_-_5_Day_Participate_Energy_-_Customer_Agreement__Installation_v.1.0_-_20260601.pdf); and home-sale purchase only "at any time after the six (6) year anniversary" — p.21.
- Foreclosing lender may terminate only "if after the fifth (5th) anniversary of the start of Term" — p.22. Capital Provider repossession only "After the fifth (5th) anniversary of the System being placed in service for federal U.S. income tax purposes" — p.17.
- "This Agreement is for the lease of the System, not the sale"; customer "expressly disclaims and irrevocably assigns to System Owner any and all right, title, and interest you may acquire, by operation of law or otherwise" (§9.1) — p.19.
- "You agree that the System is not a fixture" (§6.6.2) — p.18; "the System is the System Owner's personal property … not a contract to sell the System … nor … to make improvements to Your Property" (§9.4) — p.20; UCC-1 fixture filing "is not a concession that the System is a fixture" (§9.5) — p.20.
- Modifications become owner's property; no alterations that void warranty (§6.5) — p.17.
- Default remedy includes "compensation, on a net after tax basis assuming a tax rate of 35%, for the loss or recapture of (A) the investment tax credit … and (B) accelerated depreciation over five (5) years" — p.24.
- Owner may assign the system and agreement "without your consent" (§8 p.19; §20 p.26) — supports tax-equity/partnership financing.

### Inferences
- The 5/6-year gates line up with the 5-year ITC recapture period: a transfer of ownership before year 5 would recapture credits, so the contract makes year 6 the first purchase date and lets lenders/capital providers act only after year 5.
- A Solar Review lease that intends the lessor to claim the ITC would need the same features (FMV buyout, no purchase before recapture ends, personal-property/not-a-fixture language); these are a tax-counsel question, not something to copy blindly.

### Gaps
- No explicit statement of which tax rules drive the gates; inferred from the 5-year language and recapture provisions.

### Inferences
- **Standard for third-party-owned residential leases:** FMV buyout after the recapture period, hell-or-high-water, owner keeps tax/REC benefits, UCC-1 "notice of ownership", transfer-on-sale, AAA arbitration with class waiver, customer insurance/loss-payee, self-help disconnection, roof R&R at customer cost.
- **Less common / notable:** prepaid with $0 monthly; installer collects as owner's agent with a $0 installation contract; split battery (10-yr) and PV (25-yr) terms with battery reverting to the owner; automatic VPP enrollment with an owner-determined 30%/70% split and 100% to owner after battery term; tax-grossed default damages including lost VPP income; owner may disable the system for a third-party lender's 60-day delinquency; customer pays internet; replacement-installer consent; trip fee $350.
- For Solar Review's own Powerwall 3 lease: the biggest design choices are (a) fixed vs. discretionary VPP split, (b) battery end-of-term (Participate's battery simply reverts), (c) defining an FMV method or a published buyout schedule, (d) who holds the installer contract (Solar Review is not a licensed contractor; Participate's structure keeps the licensed work with Cosmic), and (e) CA notices (CSLB Supporting Information, §7169 disclosure, CLA disclosures, 3/5-day cancel).
