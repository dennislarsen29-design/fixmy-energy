## 1. Is the proposed structure a "true lease" for federal tax purposes?

### Takeaway
A homeowner lease with a purchase option that floats at fair market value (FMV), exercisable only after the lessor's credit-recapture period, is the standard way residential third-party-owned (TPO) prepaid leases preserve the lessor's ITC and depreciation. The biggest risks in the proposed design are not the FMV option itself but (a) a $0/month lease with no rent at all (looks like a gift of use or, if there is a large upfront payment, a disguised sale), (b) any FMV "formula" or fixed price that makes exercise reasonably certain, and (c) Solar Review Corp being an S corporation (possible noncorporate-lessor limits, unconfirmed whether still in force).

### Cited Findings
- The IRS has had advance-ruling guidelines since 1975 on when an equipment "lease" is a lease for tax purposes; Rev. Proc. 2001-28 modified and superseded Rev. Proc. 75-21. — [Norton Rose Fulbright, "New true lease guidelines were issued by the IRS" (2001)](https://www.projectfinance.law/publications/2001/june/new-true-lease-guidelines-were-issued-by-the-irs-norton-rose-fulbright?hs_amp=true); [Tax Notes (Rev. Proc. 2001-28 text)](https://taxnotes.com/lr/resolve//dph6)
- Rev. Proc. 2001-28: the lessor's "Minimum Investment" must be at least 20% of the property's cost when first placed in service, must be unconditional, and must remain at least 20% of cost at all times throughout the entire lease term. — [Tax Notes (Rev. Proc. 2001-28)](https://taxnotes.com/lr/resolve//dph6)
- Rev. Proc. 2001-28 is described as disallowing fixed-price lessee purchase options (even at or above expected value); a lessee purchase option that floats with FMV is acceptable. (Secondary slide-deck summary, not the procedure text.) — [Akin Gump, Sale Leasebacks (D. Burton, Feb 2015)](https://akingump.com/images/content/3/5/v2/35209/Sale-Leasbacks-Feb-2015-D-Burton.pdf)
- Predecessor Rev. Proc. 75-21 required the lessor to show a remaining useful life at lease end of the longer of one year or 20% of the originally estimated useful life. Whether 2001-28 uses identical wording was not confirmed. — [Tax Notes (Rev. Proc. 75-21)](https://taxnotes.com/lr/resolve//dmd8)
- Rev. Proc. 2001-29 sets the information/representations required for advance ruling requests under 2001-28 (it is procedural, not a substantive test). — [OMB.report, Rev. Proc. 2001-29](https://omb.report/icr/201307-1545-031/doc/41030001)
- Section 467 regulations contain an "uneven rent test"; an earlier version was based on Rev. Proc. 75-21. Relevant if rent is front-loaded (large upfront payment, then $0). — [26 CFR 1.467-3 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.467-3)
- A Norton Rose Fulbright partner's slides state that if a lessee wants to keep the project at lease end it must repurchase it, and that any lessee purchase option cannot be priced so that exercise is reasonably likely. — summarized in search results for [Prepaid leases (pv magazine USA, 2026-04-15)](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/04/15/prepaid-leases-emerge-as-residential-solar-customer-pathway-to-accessing-federal-tax-credits/) query; slide deck itself not retrieved.
- Residential prepaid leases in 2026: the provider/financier holds title and claims the ~30% commercial credit; the homeowner's prepayment is typically ~70% of system value; ownership option arrives after the tax hold/recapture period (often ~6 years), at FMV. — [Solar.com, Prepaid Solar Leases & PPAs (2026)](https://www.solar.com/learn/prepaid-solar-leases-ppas/); [Solar.com, Lease to Own (2026)](https://www.solar.com/learn/lease-to-own-solar-panels/); [Solarinsure buyer's guide](https://www.solarinsure.com/buyers-guide-prepaid-solar-5-common-questions)
- Some prepaid TPO contracts use a defined year-5 market-value buyout specifically to manage recapture risk from transferring the system too early. — [Solarinsure, Going solar in 2026](https://www.solarinsure.com/going-solar-in-2026-3-ways-pros-and-cons)
- Sun Valley Solar markets a prepaid lease with "clear ownership options after year 5" (FMV purchase or continue). — [Sun Valley Solar blog](https://www.sunvalleysolar.com/blog/new-solar-lease-model-upfront-savings-tax-credit-benefits-ownership)
- One source warns homeowners of risk if the TPO provider takes aggressive tax positions; another says the tax risk sits with the credit claimant, not the homeowner. — [Solarinsure](https://www.solarinsure.com/buyers-guide-prepaid-solar-5-common-questions)
- Noncorporate lessor rule: under former §46(e)(3), a noncorporate lessor — including an S corporation — could claim ITC only if (A) it manufactured the property, or (B) the lease term was under 50% of useful life AND §162 deductions (other than rent/reimbursed amounts) exceeded 15% of rental income in the first 12 months. Courts applied a "realistic contemplation" test to lease term (Schiff, 6th Cir. 1991). — [Charlson v. Commissioner (opinion excerpt)](https://law.onecle.com/tax/2001/charlsonopn.tcm.wpd04.html); [Schiff v. U.S., 942 F.2d 348](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/942/348/282390/); [26 CFR 1.46-4 (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090113091733/http://edocket.access.gpo.gov/cfr_2002/aprqtr/26cfr1.46-4.htm)
- §179 still uses identical noncorporate-lessor tests, and pre-1991 ITC guidance/cases on §46(e)(3) remain relevant there. — [Journal of Accountancy (Jul 2011)](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2011/jul/20114107/); [Iowa State CALT](https://www.calt.iastate.edu/article/expense-method-depreciation-and-non-corporate-lessor-rules)
- §50(b)(2) bars credits for property used predominantly to furnish lodging, but subparagraph (D) excepts "any energy property" — so a battery at a residence is not knocked out by the lodging rule. — [26 USC 50 (govinfo, 2017 ed.)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2017-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapA-partIV-subpartE-sec50.htm); [The Tax Adviser (Oct 2017)](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/newsletters/2017/oct/credit-residential-solar-panels.html)
- Credit basis reduction is 50% of the credit under §50(c)(3) (secondary forum/site summaries). — [TheTaxBook forum](https://forum.thetaxbook.com/forum/discussion-forums/main-forum-tax-discussion/22824-pv-system-depreciation-for-rental?p=237385)

### Inferences
- **FMV purchase option after year 6: generally fine**, provided the price genuinely floats at FMV (independent appraisal or appraisal-backed formula), the lessee is not economically compelled to buy, and the lessor bears real residual risk. A formula that predictably produces a token price (e.g., "FMV, not to exceed $1") would be read as a bargain purchase option → disguised sale/financing → lessee is tax owner → no lessor ITC/depreciation.
- **Rev. Proc. 2001-28 is an advance-ruling safe harbor for leveraged leases, not the law.** Courts apply the substance-over-form factors (Frank Lyon Co. v. U.S., 435 U.S. 561 (1978); Rev. Rul. 55-540 conditional-sale factors — background knowledge, unverified in this session). Small residential lessors almost never seek rulings; the guidelines are used as a benchmark.
- **The $0/month design is the weakest point.** A lease with no rent at all has no lease economics: the lessor has no rental income (hurting the profit-motive test that Frank Lyon-style analysis and 2001-28 both require, and making the 15% deduction test impossible to meet if the noncorporate rule applies). If the homeowner instead pays a large upfront amount (a "prepaid lease"), that prepayment is rent; but if it approaches the system's full value, the homeowner looks like the economic owner (lessor recovers its investment on day 1, so its at-risk investment does not "remain 20% throughout"). Industry practice (~70% prepay, FMV buyout after the hold period) suggests a partial prepayment with the lessor retaining real residual value and earning revenue from incentives/VPP is the recognized pattern — but the lessor's ongoing return should be documented (SGIP/SDCP/VPP assignment, maintenance obligations, residual).
- **S-corporation lessor risk (Solar Review Corp is an S-corp per CLAUDE.md):** if the old noncorporate-lessor rule still applies to §48/48E credits via §50(d) or otherwise, a 6-year lease of a battery with a 10+-year useful life could pass the "<50% of useful life" prong only if the useful life is genuinely >12 years, and the 15% deduction test would be very hard with $0 rent. This was NOT confirmed current law — top question for counsel. A C-corp or partnership/fund lessor may avoid it.
- **Tax-exempt use (§168(h)/§50(b)(3)-(4)):** a homeowner is an individual, not a tax-exempt entity, so tax-exempt use property rules should not apply to typical customers; they would matter only if leasing to a nonprofit, school, or government. (Background reasoning, verify.)
- **ASC 842 (background knowledge, unverified):** a lessor with an FMV-only purchase option and no transfer of ownership usually classifies as an operating lease unless the lease term is a major part of economic life or PV of payments ≈ substantially all fair value; a large prepayment could push toward sales-type classification. Tax and book classification can differ.

### Gaps

- Whether the noncorporate-lessor rule (old §46(e)(3)) currently limits §48/48E credits for S-corp lessors — no source found either way. Critical.
- No primary authority found specifically on $0-rent residential leases or prepaid residential battery leases; no Keith Martin/Norton Rose article on residential prepaid leases retrieved. How Tesla, Sunrun, Participate, and Maxwell paper their prepaid / lease-to-own deals was not found in primary documents.

## 2. Recapture vs. a year-6 buyout; FMV appraisal

### Takeaway
A sale to the homeowner after the 5-year recapture period ends should avoid ITC recapture; the industry standard is an FMV buyout at or after year 5–6. The FMV must be genuine (appraisal or appraisal-validated formula).

### Cited Findings
- Prepaid TPO contracts use a defined year-5 market-value buyout to manage recapture risk; others offer ownership after about six years when the tax hold period ends. — [Solarinsure](https://www.solarinsure.com/going-solar-in-2026-3-ways-pros-and-cons); [Solar.com](https://www.solar.com/learn/lease-to-own-solar-panels/)
- Recapture under §50(a)(1) (background knowledge, unverified in this session): disposition within year 1 = 100%, year 2 = 80%, year 3 = 60%, year 4 = 40%, year 5 = 20%; none after 5 full years from placed-in-service.

### Inferences
- "First day after year 6" is comfortably outside recapture. Measure from the placed-in-service date, not contract signing; avoid any side agreement giving the homeowner the asset earlier (a pre-agreed early sale or a "deemed purchase" on default could trigger recapture or ownership recharacterization).
- Set buyout price as the greater of an independent appraisal or a published formula that tracks real used-equipment values; never cap it at a nominal amount.

### Gaps
- No source found on how specific lessors (Sunrun, Tesla, Participate) set FMV buyout prices for batteries.

## 3. California revenue streams for a lessor-owned battery

### Takeaway
SDCP's Solar Battery Savings program (upfront $/kWh + $0.10/kWh evening performance payments) is the most reliable local stream in San Diego; SGIP equity budgets pay much more but funding/waitlist status in 2026 is unclear; DSGS paid ~$350/Powerwall/season in 2024 but its 2026 funding is contested. Who may claim each as a third-party owner is mostly unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **SDCP Solar Battery Savings:** ~$350/kWh usable capacity for new solar+battery; ~$250/kWh when adding a battery to existing solar; CARE/FERA/Communities of Concern higher (CARE/FERA adding to existing solar: $350/kWh; Panasonic page says up to $500/kWh); plus $0.10/kWh discharged during weekday evenings (~4–9pm), with a 90% monthly discharge requirement; partial clawback if unenrolled within 5 years; max often reported as $10,000 (unverified). — [Enphase/SolarQuarter (Nov 2025)](https://solarquarter.com/2025/11/06/enphase-iq-batteries-qualify-for-san-diego-community-powers-solar-battery-savings-program); [Quitcarbon CARE/FERA bonus](https://www.quitcarbon.com/rebates/san-diego-community-power-solar-battery-savings-rebate-care-fera-bonus-); [Panasonic program page](https://solar.na.panasonic.com/homeowner/utility-programs/sd-solar-battery-savings-program?hsLang=en); [Solarinsure SDCP](https://solarinsure.com/san-diego-community-power-solar-battery-program)
  - Consistent with Solar Review's own numbers: $250/kWh standard → $3,375 per 13.5 kWh PW3; $350/kWh CARE → $4,725 (CLAUDE.md, 2026-10-20 correction).
  - Tesla's SDCP Participation Agreement grants device control to the aggregator (per CLAUDE.md doc review), meaning Tesla is the aggregator in that program.
- **SGIP:** 2026 guides report Equity Resiliency ~$1,000/kWh, General Market ~$150/kWh, RSSE $1,100/kWh battery; conflicting site caps (20 vs 30 kWh); CPUC page lists Equity Resiliency as available through 2025; aggregator reports say every budget was fully reserved/waitlisted as of March 2026. RSSE allows third-party ownership; CPUC document says TPO projects must take the 30% federal credit. — [FranklinWH SGIP 2026 guide](https://www.franklinwh.com/blog/a-complete-guide-to-california-sgip-program-in-2026); [CPUC SGIP page](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/sgip); [CPUC resolution PDF](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Efile/G000/M599/K960/599960147.PDF); [CPUC RSSE fact sheet](https://webproda.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/divisions/energy-division/documents/self-generation-incentive-program/sgip-fact-sheet---residential-solar-and-storage-equity.pdf); [Exspenditure (2026)](https://exspenditure.com/?p=1311)
- **DSGS (CEC):** Tesla's page gives 2024-season rates from $7/kW (May) to $14.97/kW (September), up to ~$350/Powerwall/year. 2026 funding contested: reports of the Governor/Legislature cutting funding and a May 2026 budget revision casting doubt; a Sept 9, 2026 Tesla+Sunrun dispatch reportedly delivered >580 MW. — [Tesla DSGS support page](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/virtual-power-plant/dsgs); [Canary Media (funding cut)](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/virtual-power-plants/california-dsgs-funding-2025-legislation); [Canary Media (reprieve)](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/virtual-power-plants/california-demand-side-grid-support-benefits); [Utility Dive (Brattle)](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/californias-virtual-power-plant-could-save-206m-by-2028-brattle/758145); [Energy-Storage.news (580 MW)](https://www.energy-storage.news/sunrun-and-tesla-deliver-record-580mw-to-california-grid-amid-heatwave/)
- **Federal (for context):** 25D homeowner credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025; TPO under 48E is the remaining federal path. — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/07/08/residential-solar-at-risk-under-one-big-beautiful-bill-act); [Soligent](https://www.soligent.net/post/why-solar-installers-must-prioritize-energy-storage-sales-after-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)

### Inferences
- In SDCP territory, a lessor could plausibly capture ~$3,375–$4,725 upfront per PW3 plus evening-discharge payments (~$0.10 × kWh discharged), if the program lets a third-party owner be the applicant/payee or lets the customer assign it. Not confirmed.

- DSGS is a Tesla-aggregated program; payment flows through Tesla's app to the enrolled account holder (likely the homeowner). Lessor would need a contractual assignment.

## 5. Can the lessor require enrollment and take assignment of incentive/VPP payments?

### Takeaway
Yes in principle — TPO companies routinely reserve rebates, incentives, and environmental attributes to the owner in the lease — but no primary customer lease addressing VPP payment assignment was found.

### Cited Findings
- Sunrun's filings define "Environmental Attributes" broadly (credits, emissions allowances, green tags, RECs) and treat upfront state/utility rebates as lease payments recognized straight-line over the lease; Sunrun assigns ITCs to tax-equity investors with the future lease payments. — [Sunrun 10-K FY2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001469367/000146936718000031/sunrun10k2017.htm); [Sunrun S-4/A 2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1469367/000119312520237327/d937729ds4a.htm)
- Battery storage may be added to residential solar leases/PPAs with the system owner's permission. — [Solar.com, Adding battery to a solar lease](https://solar.com/learn/adding-battery-storage-to-a-solar-lease/)

### Inferences
- The lease should: (1) make the lessor the program applicant/payee where programs allow; (2) where only the utility account holder can enroll (likely SDCP/DSGS/Wattsmart), require the homeowner to enroll and keep the battery enrolled, grant the lessor/aggregator dispatch control, and assign payments (or credit them against rent); (3) allocate clawback liability (SDCP 5-year and Wattsmart 4-year clawbacks) if the homeowner un-enrolls or sells.
- Because programs often pay the account holder directly, an assignment may be practical only as a contractual pass-through, not a program-level payee change. Consumer-law disclosure of this assignment is advisable.

### Gaps
- Actual Sunrun/Tesla consumer lease language on VPP/grid-service payments not found.

## 6. FMV of a 6-year-old Powerwall 3

### Takeaway
Tesla's US warranty runs 10 years from installation with ~70% capacity retention for self-consumption/backup use, so at year 6 about 4 years of warranty remain; no source on used-Powerwall market prices or warranty transferability was found.

### Cited Findings
- US Powerwall warranty: free from defects for 10 years from initial installation; 70% capacity at 10 years with unlimited cycles for solar self-consumption/backup; for other applications, 10 years or 37,800 kWh throughput, whichever first. — [Tesla Powerwall Warranty US-EN (PDF)](https://digitalassets.tesla.com/tesla-contents/image/upload/Powerwall_Warranty_US-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- VPP/grid-export use could count as "other applications," shortening warranty via the 37,800 kWh cap — matters for heavy VPP dispatch. Verify in the PW3-specific warranty.
- A rough FMV approach: replacement cost × remaining-life share (≈4/10 warranty, longer physical life) adjusted for degradation; an independent appraisal should set the actual buyout.

### Gaps
- Warranty transferability on sale/buyout, PW3-specific warranty terms, and any secondary-market price data — not found.
