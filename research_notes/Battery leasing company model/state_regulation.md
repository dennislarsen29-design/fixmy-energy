# State Legal & Regulatory Requirements for a Residential Battery Lessor (California + Utah)

Scope: Solar Review Corp owning Tesla Powerwall 3 units and leasing them to homeowners (~6-year term, fair-market-value (FMV) purchase option), with a licensed contractor (Cosmic, C-46/C-10 in CA) doing the install.

**Method caveat (read first):** Research ran 2026-11-06 from a sandbox where direct page fetches to le.utah.gov and commerce.utah.gov failed (DNS blocked), so statute text could not be read in full. Findings below come from search-result summaries of primary sources (CSLB, CPUC, Federal Register, Utah DCP, Utah Code PDFs) and some secondary/vendor pages, which are flagged. Nothing here is legal advice; every item marked "verify" should go to California and Utah counsel before launch.

## 1. California: Does the lessor need a CSLB license if a licensed C-46/C-10 contractor installs?

### Takeaway
No source squarely answers this for a non-installing lessor. The CPUC's consumer guide treats the "solar provider" that contracts with the homeowner as needing an active C-46, C-10 or B license, and its salespeople need HIS registration, so the safe assumption is that the company signing the home-improvement contract with the homeowner must be licensed (or the contract must be signed by the licensed contractor). Counsel must decide.

### Cited Findings
- CPUC guide summary: the solar provider's CSLB license "must be active and in classification C-46 (Solar Contractor), C-10 (Electrical Contractor), or B (General Building Contractor)"; a B-only contractor must subcontract solar work to a C-46/C-10 — [CPUC California Solar Consumer Protection Guide](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/divisions/energy-division/documents/solar-guide/solarguide22_011922.pdf) (via search summary)
- Salespeople need Home Improvement Salesperson (HIS) registration under a licensed contractor; home improvement jobs over $500 need a written contract with specific contents — [IREC California licensing summary](https://irecusa.org/blog/solar_licensing/california/) and secondary consumer guides (search summary)
- The contract and CSLB disclosure document identify the company that owns the system (third-party owner) — secondary consumer guides (search summary; not independently verified)

### Inferences
- If Solar Review is the contracting party with the homeowner for a battery that gets installed on/in the home, it is very likely acting as a home-improvement "contractor" under B&P 7026 regardless of who swings the tools. Two structures to put to counsel: (a) Solar Review holds its own CSLB license (B or C-10) and subcontracts Cosmic; (b) the licensed installer contracts for installation and Solar Review signs only an equipment lease (separate documents). Option (a) is the cleaner, more common pattern for third-party owners.
- Reps who sell the lease door-to-door likely still need HIS registration tied to the licensed entity.

### Gaps
- Could not read B&P 7026/7026.1 or any CSLB opinion on whether a pure equipment lessor (no installation) is a "contractor." Counsel question.

## 2. California: Solar Energy System Disclosure Document (B&P 7169) — lease version; does it cover storage?

### Takeaway
B&P 7169 requires the CSLB Solar Energy System Disclosure Document on the front of every residential solar sale, financing or lease contract (16-pt bold), and since D.22-12-056 / D.23-11-068 it is part of the utility interconnection packet. Whether a battery-only (storage-only) lease triggers it is unconfirmed; real-world leases bundle storage into the solar contract.

### Cited Findings
- Effective Jan 1, 2019; must be provided before completion of a sale, financing or lease; printed on the front/cover page of every solar energy contract in 16-point bold; topics include total cost/payments incl. financing, system malfunctions, and the difference between a lease and a purchase; must be in the language of the sales presentation; not for new-construction standard features — [CSLB Solar Energy Disclosure Document](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Solar_Requirements.aspx); [CSLB bulletin Aug 30 2018](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Media_Room/Industry_Bulletins/2018/August_30.aspx); [B&P 7169](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_7169)
- CSLB provides "Supporting Information Documents" with the disclosure — [CSLB bulletin June 29 2018](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Media_Room/Industry_Bulletins/2018/June_29.aspx)
- CPUC Resolution E-5364 (approved ~March 2025) updates the bill-savings inputs/assumptions used in the CSLB disclosure under B&P 7169(c) — [Stoel Rives regulatory update Mar 18 2025](https://www.stoel.com/insights/reports/march-18-2025); [CPUC draft resolution](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Published/G000/M556/K398/556398285.PDF)
- Under D.23-11-068 and D.22-12-056 the disclosure is part of the paperwork required to interconnect residential solar AND storage under NEM/Net Billing; a complete Net Billing application includes a signed contract, single-line diagram, complete CSLB Disclosure Document, and a signed CPUC Solar Consumer Protection Guide acknowledgment — [CPUC draft resolution (.docx)](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Published/G000/M556/K398/556398285.docx)
- Example third-party leases (Sunrun 2025, Vivint PPA) bundle battery storage into the "Equipment" under the lease — sample documents surfaced in search (e.g., [CRMLS-hosted lease PDF](https://media.crmls.org/medias/3df4134f-e6fa-4c29-acf2-45d261976243.pdf)); not official text

### Inferences
- Because the utility requires the disclosure + CPUC guide acknowledgment for storage interconnections paired with solar, a battery lease on a home with existing solar should be presumed to need the lease version of the CSLB disclosure and the CPUC guide (lease section) in the contract package. This matches the project's existing CPUC-guide signing flow, but the lease form differs from the purchase form.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed whether the CSLB form has a storage-only variant or whether a storage-only retrofit triggers 7169 by statute (vs. only via utility interconnection rules).

## 3. California: Home Solicitation / cancellation rights

### Takeaway
Contracts signed at the home are home solicitation contracts: 3 business days to cancel, extended to 5 business days for buyers 65+ (AB 2471, eff. Jan 1, 2021), with a prescribed notice form.

### Cited Findings
- AB 2471 (2020) extended the cancellation right for persons 65+ from 3 to 5 business days for home solicitation, home improvement, PACE, service/repair and seminar sales contracts; effective Jan 1, 2021; prescribes form/content of the notice — [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2020/10/14/california-extends-cancellation-period-for-senior-citizens-under-certain-door-to-door-contracts/); [chaptered text](https://www.legiscan.com/CA/text/AB2471/2019); [CSLB 2021 new laws bulletin](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/IndustryBulletins/2020/20-25_New_Laws.pdf)
- AB 2993 (2023–24) addressed home-improvement loans tied to solar; passed Assembly May 22 2024; final status not confirmed — [Plural Policy](https://open.pluralpolicy.com/ca/bills/20232024/AB2993)

### Gaps
- "SB 1031" from the brief could not be tied to anything solar-lease specific. Status of AB 2993 and any 2025–26 solar-sales bills not confirmed.

## 4. California: Consumer leasing law (state + federal Reg M), rental-purchase, credit-sale characterization

### Takeaway
A ~6-year residential battery lease to an individual is a "consumer lease" under the federal Consumer Leasing Act/Reg M if total contractual obligation is ≤ $73,400 (2026) — almost certainly true for a Powerwall. California's Civil Code 2985.7 et seq. is the Vehicle Leasing Act (motor vehicles only), and the Karnette Rental-Purchase Act covers only renewable ≤4-month terms, so neither should apply. The key structural risk is the purchase option: a nominal/no-cost purchase option makes it a disguised credit sale (security interest → TILA/Reg Z, and possibly the California Financing Law); a genuine FMV option supports true-lease treatment.

### Cited Findings
- Reg M exemption threshold raised from $71,900 to $73,400 effective Jan 1, 2026; leases at or below are covered by disclosures/protections; a lease above the threshold at consummation stays exempt — [Federal Register 2025-22813](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/xml/2025/12/15/2025-22813.xml); [Federal Reserve press release Dec 15 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20251215a.htm); [CFPB Reg M](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/consumer-leasing-regulation-m/)
- Federal CLA requires an obligation of more than four months to be a "consumer lease" — summarized in [Karnette search results / DCA Legal Guide S-10](https://www.dca.ca.gov/publications/legal_guides/s_10.shtml)
- Civil Code 2985.8 sits in the Vehicle Leasing Act; "motor vehicle" means a vehicle registered under the Vehicle Code; requires Reg M disclosures regardless of Reg M applicability; repealed and re-added by Stats. 2025, Ch. 354 — [FindLaw 2985.8](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-2985-8/); [Counsel Stack](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ca/CIV/2985.8.)
- Karnette Rental-Purchase Act (Civ. Code 1812.620 et seq.) covers household personal property leases with an initial term ≤4 months that may be renewed and give an ownership option; excludes leases that are security interests and consumer credit contracts — [Justia 1812.622](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-2-96/section-1812-622/); [DCA Legal Guide S-10](https://www.dca.ca.gov/publications/legal_guides/s_10.shtml)
- California: a lease letting the lessee become owner at term end for nominal or no additional consideration is a security agreement as a matter of law; a lease with a 10% purchase option was upheld as a true lease — [Mondaq: California Finance Lenders Law](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/antitrust-eu-competition/11048/california-finance-lenders-law)

### Inferences
- Draft the lease to be a true lease: purchase option at FMV determined at exercise (not a fixed nominal figure), lessor retains residual risk, term shorter than useful life. Then comply with Reg M (lease disclosures, advertising rules) rather than TILA.
- If the purchase option is nominal ($1/$100) or payments substantially cover full cost + return with ownership expected, expect TILA/Reg Z, Commercial Code Article 9 treatment, and CFL licensing exposure.

### Gaps
- Not checked: whether Reg M's lease disclosure is satisfied by the CSLB lease disclosure (no — they are separate, but confirm format integration with counsel).

## 5. California: California Financing Law (CFL) license — does leasing require one?

### Takeaway
A true lease generally isn't a "loan," but the CFL doesn't define "loan" and DFPI looks through form; a lease that is in substance a financed sale can require a CFL finance lender license (NMLS). Recommend counsel opinion; many solar lessors hold CFL licenses (e.g., Mosaic, a lender, does).

### Cited Findings
- Anyone engaging in the business of a finance lender or broker in California must hold a CFL license, applied for via NMLS — [DFPI California Financing Law](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/california-financing-law/)
- The CFL does not define "loan"; DFPI proposed (March 2023) regulations to interpret "loan" more broadly; finalization status not confirmed — [JD Supra/legal commentary via search](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/california-regulator-reiterates-that-52632)
- DFPI has said credit sales cannot be used to evade the financing law — [JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/california-regulator-reiterates-that-52632)
- Mosaic (solar lender) holds a CA CFL license — [Mosaic CFL license](https://joinmosaic.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Mosaic-CA-CFL-License.pdf)
- SB 784 (solar/home-improvement financing requirements) and SB 666 (CFL fee rules) mentioned in commentary; enactment status not confirmed — [JD Supra topic page](https://production.jdsupra.com/topics/loans/california)

### Gaps
- No DFPI guidance found specifically on residential solar/battery leases. Status of DFPI "loan" definition regulation, SB 784, SB 666 unknown.

## 6. California: Property tax on lessor-owned battery (R&T Code 73) and UCC fixture filings

### Takeaway
The Section 73 active-solar new-construction exclusion reportedly sunsets for systems completed on/after Jan 1, 2027, and its application to a battery (especially storage without new solar, or lessor-owned equipment) is uncertain. A 2027+ battery lease should assume the equipment may be assessable (possibly as lessor-owned business personal property) until the county assessor/BOE confirms otherwise.

### Cited Findings
- BOE guidelines for active solar energy systems new construction exclusion — [BOE LTA 12/053](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta12053.pdf); related [BOE LTA 14/037](https://boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta14037.pdf), [BOE LTA 22/054](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta22054.pdf) (content not read in full)
- Vendor sources say the exclusion ends for systems completed on/after Jan 1, 2027 and that SB 710 (Oct 2025) lets pre-2027 systems keep it while the current owner holds the property — [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/california-solar-property-tax-exclusion/) (vendor source; verify against statute)
- Vendor sources conflict on batteries: one says storage installed with a pre-2027 solar system is excluded; another says storage alone may not qualify; advise asking the assessor — [Cali Energy](https://cali-energy.com/blog/solar-property-tax-exclusion-california); [California Solar Exit](https://www.californiasolarexit.com/solar-property-tax-exclusion-california-2027)
- Older BOE guidance indicates sale-leaseback/partnership-flip systems can retain the exclusion; vendor says leased/PPA systems are covered but to confirm with assessor — [BOE LTA 12/053](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta12053.pdf) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- Budget for possible personal property tax (and annual Form 571-L business property statements to counties) on lessor-owned batteries leased from 2027 onward; build pass-through language into the lease.
- UCC-1 fixture filings: no sources found. Standard practice for solar lessors is a UCC-1 (often a precautionary fixture filing) to protect ownership against the homeowner's mortgage lender/buyers; counsel should confirm form and county recording.

### Gaps
- R&T §73 text and current BOE guidance on standalone storage not read; SB 710 not verified from primary source. No source on UCC fixture filings for leased batteries.

## 8. Utah: HB 57 (2025) solar retailer registration and bond — does it cover lessors and battery-only?

### Takeaway
Utah requires solar retailers to register with the Division of Consumer Protection and post a bond/CD starting July 1, 2026 (each unregistered sale is a violation). Leases are covered ("system lease agreement"). Whether battery-only fits the "residential solar energy system" definition is unclear: the existing definition is a system that generates electricity from solar; 2026 HB 428 would add battery storage components, status unconfirmed. Since Solar Review is already selling in Utah, registration is likely required now for any solar or solar+battery deal.

### Cited Findings
- On or after July 1, 2026 a solar retailer may not operate without registration; each sale without registration after that date is a violation — [Utah DCP Residential Solar Panel Retailer FAQ](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/residential-solar-panel-retailer/residential-solar-panel-retailer-frequently-asked-questions/); [DCP registration page](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/residential-solar-panel-retailer/residential-solar-panel-retailer-apply-for-registration/)
- Retailer must post a bond from an authorized surety or a certificate of deposit; one tier is $100,000 (full schedule cut off) — [DCP FAQ](https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/residential-solar-panel-retailer/residential-solar-panel-retailer-frequently-asked-questions/); a machine summary says $100,000–$300,000 by size/history — [BillTrack50 UT HB0057](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1761484) (AI summary, verify)
- State can claim the bond for damaged customers; registration can be denied/suspended/revoked for violations in the preceding five years — [Soar/Utah summary](https://cpo.soar.com/articles/2178834/Utah/Utah-sets-strict-registration-rules-for-solar-retailers-to-prevent-fraud)
- HB 57 defines "system lease agreement" as an agreement under which a customer leases a residential solar energy system from a solar retailer; it also requires production estimates within a set percentage of actual output and fixing underperformance — [enrolled HB 57](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/enrolled/HB0057.pdf) (search summary; fetch blocked)
- Existing definition (§13-52-102): "residential solar energy system" = installed in Utah, generates electricity primarily for household on-site use, ≤4 residential units, capacity >1 kW; "solar energy system" collects and uses solar energy to generate electricity — [Justia 2022 Utah Code 13-52-102](https://law.justia.com/codes/utah/2022/title-13/chapter-52/part-1/section-102/)
- 2026 HB 428 would amend §13-52-102 so "solar energy system" includes a battery storage component that stores electricity converted from solar energy, and would require sales-rep certification and a bond; enactment status not confirmed — [HB 428 S01](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2026/bills/introduced/HB0428S01.pdf)
- Sales reps as W-2 employees: BillTrack50 AI summary says HB 57 would mandate reps be direct employees, not independent contractors — [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1761484); NOT confirmed in enacted text
- Utah disclosure contents section 13-52-202 effective 5/7/2025 — [Utah Code 13-52-202 PDF](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter52/C13-52-S202_2025050720250507.pdf) (not read)

### Inferences
- Treat Solar Review as needing Utah solar-retailer registration + bond now for any deal involving solar; a standalone battery lease charged by solar (Powerwall 3 has a built-in solar inverter) is arguably a "solar energy system" component — counsel to confirm.
- The W-2-only rep rule, if enacted, conflicts with 1099 reps and is a top counsel question.

### Gaps
- Bond tiers, rep-employment rule, HB 428 status, and the §13-52 disclosure form contents could not be read (le.utah.gov and commerce.utah.gov blocked from this sandbox).

## 9. Utah: Consumer credit / lease rules and home solicitation cancel

### Takeaway
Utah home solicitation sales carry a 3-business-day cancel right with a prescribed "BUYER'S RIGHT TO CANCEL" notice (Title 70C ch. 5, amended 2025). Utah's Consumer Credit Code covers credit; consumer lease concepts appear in UCC Article 2A (Title 70A ch. 2a) and §70C-2-203 (additional security in consumer leases). A true FMV lease is likely governed by 2A + Reg M; a disguised sale would bring in 70C credit rules.

### Cited Findings
- Buyer may cancel a home solicitation sale until the end of the third business day after signing; §70C-5-102 amended by Ch. 302, 2025 General Session; emergency exception — [Utah Code 70C-5-102 PDF](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title70C/Chapter5/C70C-5-S102_1800010118000101.pdf); [Counsel Stack](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ut/70C-5-102)
- §70C-5-103 prescribes the "BUYER'S RIGHT TO CANCEL" notice with seller name/address — [LegalFix 70C-5-103](https://www.legalfix.com/statutes/state-codes/utah/title-70c/chapter-5/section-103)
- Utah Admin. Code R152-11-9: failing to give the three-day notice on a sale over $25 can violate the rule; "business day" excludes weekends/holidays — [Cornell LII R152-11-9](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R152-11-9)
- Utah Consumer Credit Code applies to credit extended to an individual mainly for personal/family/household purposes (70C-1-201); 70C-2-203 addresses (prohibits) additional security in consumer leases — [Counsel Stack 70C-1-201](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ut/70C-1-201); [Counsel Stack 70C-2-203](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ut/70C-2-203)
- UCC 2A (Title 70A ch. 2a) defines "consumer lease" (lease by a regularly engaged lessor to an individual primarily for personal/household purposes) — [Utah Code 70A-2a PDF](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title70A/Chapter2A/C70A-2a_1800010118000101.pdf) (snippet only)
- Many Utah cities require solicitor permits (e.g., Logan, Lehi) — [Logan code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/loganut/latest/logan_ut/0-0-0-3346); [Lehi code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/lehiut/latest/lehi_ut/0-0-0-1455)

### Gaps
- Whether Utah requires a consumer-lender/lessor license (Utah Consumer Credit Notification/registration with DFI) for a battery lessor — not found.

## Draft compliance checklist (for counsel review)

California
1. Decide licensing structure: Solar Review obtains its own CSLB license (C-10 or B) and subcontracts Cosmic, or a licensed contractor is the contracting party. Reps need HIS registration.
2. Lease package: CSLB Solar Energy System Disclosure Document (lease version) on the cover page; CPUC Consumer Protection Guide with signed acknowledgment; home-improvement contract requirements; 3-day cancel (5-day for 65+) notice; Reg M consumer lease disclosures (obligation ≤ $73,400 in 2026).
3. Structure as a true lease: FMV purchase option at exercise, no nominal buyout; otherwise TILA + possible CFL license.
4. Get counsel opinion on CFL license need; consider applying via NMLS if any doubt.
5. Property tax: ask county assessor/BOE how lessor-owned batteries are assessed (esp. completions in 2027+ after §73 sunset); plan 571-L filings and lease pass-through.
6. UCC-1 / fixture filing to protect ownership.
7. Interconnection: homeowner as customer of record; confirm SDG&E form handling of third-party ownership.

Utah
1. Register as a solar retailer with DCP + bond/CD (required from July 1, 2026); confirm battery-only coverage and HB 428 status.
2. Confirm whether reps must be W-2 employees and/or individually certified/bonded.
3. Utah disclosures (§13-52-202), production-estimate rules, 3-business-day BUYER'S RIGHT TO CANCEL notice (70C-5-103), city solicitor permits.
4. True-lease structure under UCC 2A; Reg M disclosures; check whether Utah DFI requires lessor registration.
5. Installer holds S202 and/or E200/E201; confirm whether lessor needs a contractor license.
6. Personal property tax on lessor-owned equipment (assessor).
7. Rocky Mountain Power interconnection with third-party-owned storage.

Both: insurance (property on owned equipment, GL, installer indemnity/additional insured); privacy policy for monitoring data.
