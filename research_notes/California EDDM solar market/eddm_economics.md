# EDDM economics and mechanics for residential solar services in California (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: ~12 searches. WebFetch failed for several vendor hosts (directmail.io, 4over4.com: DNS not allowed in this session), so most findings come from search-result summaries, not full pages. Most pricing/benchmark sources are print/mail vendor blogs with an interest in selling. No solar-specific EDDM benchmark was found. Anything under "Inferences" is a guess, not a fact.

## 1. 2026 USPS EDDM rates and limits

### Takeaway
EDDM Retail is about $0.260/piece (effective July 12, 2026, per vendor citations of USPS Notice 123; one USPS-hosted page also lists $0.26). Limits: 200 to 5,000 pieces per ZIP per day, whole routes only. Vendor sources conflict on whether EDDM changed in July 2026, so confirm on Postal Explorer before budgeting.

### Cited Findings
- EDDM Retail $0.260/piece effective July 12, 2026 (previous $0.247 through July 11, 2026), flat for pieces up to 3.3 oz; retail capped at 5,000 pieces/day per ZIP; EDDM BMEU (permit) rate $0.259 with no daily cap. Vendor-reported — [Mailpro 2026 rates summary via search](https://www.mailpro.org/post/usps-rate-changes-july-2026/)
- One vendor says EDDM was not scheduled to rise in July 2026; USPS filed ~4.8% average increase effective July 12, 2026, and I did not find confirmation of final PRC approval in snippets — [Royle](https://www.royle.com/blog/usps-postage-increases-are-comingheres-what-it-means-for-your-print-strategy); [Walsworth](https://www.walsworth.com/blog/usps-update-july-2026-rate-changes)
- USPS: EDDM Retail minimum 200 and maximum 5,000 pieces per day per ZIP Code — [USPS EDDM page](https://www.usps.com/business/every-door-direct-mail.htm); [PostalPro](https://postalpro.usps.com/node/3468)
- DMM 143: 200-piece minimum can be met by 50+ lbs; a whole-ZIP mailing can be under 200 pieces if the ZIP has fewer than 200 deliverable addresses; pieces enter at the Post Office serving the routes — [USPS DMM 143](https://pe.usps.com/text/DMM300/143.htm)
- Routes are all-or-nothing: every active delivery on a selected route gets a piece; you cannot select streets or households (vendor guide) — [CATDI targeting limitations](https://www.catdi.com/guides/eddm/targeting-limitations)
- Stale/conflicting rates in circulation ($0.178, $0.185, $0.21, $0.226, $0.247); disregard older ones — same Mailpro/4over4 search results

### Inferences
- Budget $0.26 postage plus printing; a hedge of $0.27 to $0.28 covers a possible rate move in 2027 (guess).

### Gaps
- Did not read USPS Notice 123 or the Postal Explorer price list directly; the $0.260 figure rests on vendor citations. Whether EDDM Marketing Mail (BMEU/permit) has any different 2026 limits beyond what's above was not confirmed.

## 2. Printing costs (5,000 to 50,000 pieces; Minuteman-style vendors)

### Takeaway
Vendor tables suggest roughly $0.27 to $0.35 per 6x11 postcard at 10,000 to 25,000, giving about $0.41 to $0.47 all-in per piece with postage at 10,000. No Minuteman Press quote was found; local franchises price by quote.

### Cited Findings
- Sample vendor table, 6x11 postcards: 1,000 = 60¢, 2,500 = 35¢, 5,000 = 32¢, 10,000 = 30¢, 25,000 = 27¢ each (printing only) — [CATDI via search](https://www.catdi.com/direct-mail/postage-mailing-costs)
- 6.25x9 at 5,000 pieces: $0.14 to $0.18 per piece printing — search results (vendor sites, same query)
- All-in 10,000 standard postcards ≈ $4,100 (~$0.41/pc, design extra); another calculator ≈ $0.47/pc ($4,700) for 6.25x9 — [Mailpro EDDM cost calculator](https://mpa-homepage.pages.dev/eddm/cost-calculator/)
- Print-only per-piece estimate $0.08 to $0.10 (size unspecified) — vendor result; inconsistent with the table above

### Inferences
- Printing vendor spread is wide ($0.08 to $0.32); get 3 quotes including a local Minuteman franchise and an online printer. At 50,000 pieces, per-piece printing likely falls below the 10,000 figure (guess; no data found).

### Gaps

## 3. Response rates and cost per lead/appointment

### Takeaway
No audited solar EDDM response data found. Best available proxies: untargeted EDDM 0.2 to 0.4% response (home services), targeted-list mail 0.8 to 1.8%, and general direct mail 2 to 5% (ANA/DMA-cited, but includes house lists and is not comparable to cold mail). Existing-solar repair offers have no published benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Home-services: targeted list (home-value/age filters) 0.8 to 1.8% response, $50 to $113 per lead; untargeted EDDM 0.2 to 0.4%, $175 to $350 per lead — [directmail.io 2026 EDDM vs targeted](https://directmail.io/blog/eddm-vs-targeted-direct-mail-home-services-2026) (read via search summary only; vendor of targeted mail; citing a 2025 InfoTrends/Keypoint study I could not verify)
- General direct mail ~4.4% (ANA/DMA cited by a vendor); "2 to 5% is good" — [Amsive](https://newsletter.amsive.com/capabilities/direct-mail-solar/); vendor guides
- Solar: blended cost per lead ~$206 across channels, shared marketplace leads ~$110 — vendor blogs ([ActiveProspect](https://activeprospect.com/blog/how-much-do-solar-leads-cost/); [Abstrakt](https://www.abstraktmg.com/cost-of-solar-leads/))
- Appointment cost ranges are inconsistent: $150 to $500 per appointment; appointment-set solar leads $250 to $450; canvassing appointments $30 to $80 (SurgePV, via [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-digital-marketing-statistics)); Elevarus: "$25 vs $450" appointment — [Elevarus](https://elevarus.com/buy-solar-leads-appointment-set-vs-raw-economics/)
- A solar vendor case: $24.95 per lead, 111 leads, 5 installs (not direct mail) — [Get Green Leads](https://get-green-leads.beehiiv.com/p/case-study-summer-henderson-legacy-solar)
- SurgePV budgets $200 for an EDDM drop to ~300 homes inside a mixed neighborhood campaign, no results reported — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/neighborhood-solar-marketing)

### Inferences
- At 0.2 to 0.4% response and all-in ~$0.45 per piece: cost per response = $0.45 / 0.003 ≈ $112 to $225 (matches the cited $175 to $350 only roughly). Booked-and-held appointments will be a fraction of responses (assumption 30 to 50%), putting cost per held appointment around $250 to $750 for cold EDDM. Guess, not benchmark.
- A repair/battery offer to homeowners with existing solar is a narrower, higher-intent audience, so it may beat generic EDDM, but there is no data; plan on the low end until tested.

### Gaps
- No solar-specific EDDM response rate, no existing-solar repair-offer benchmark, no QR-scan/call-in split data.

## 4. Route targeting (ownership, value, income, solar penetration)

### Takeaway
USPS's tool filters routes by age, income and household size (Census averages) and residential counts, not owner-occupancy or solar. Solar penetration must be layered from California DG Stats ZIP-level data (ZIP only, not addresses), so route choice is ZIP/neighborhood-level.

### Cited Findings
- USPS says the EDDM tool maps ZIPs and filters by age, income or household size using Census data — [USPS EDDM](https://www.usps.com/business/every-door-direct-mail.htm); route figures are averages — [CATDI choosing routes](https://www.catdi.com/guides/eddm/choosing-carrier-routes)
- Owner-occupied targeting is not offered in the USPS tool per a vendor guide (not confirmed against USPS docs) — [CATDI](https://www.catdi.com/guides/eddm/targeting-limitations)
- California DG Stats: interconnected-applications dataset (as of Feb 28, 2026, 146.4 MB) with ZIP-level, not address-level, records; covers IOU NEM interconnections — [California DG Stats downloads](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/downloads); [FAQ](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/faq/)

### Inferences
- Method: rank ZIPs by installs older than ~5 to 6 years (older systems, expired warranty/orphaned installers) and high install share; then pick EDDM routes inside those ZIPs with higher median income/older age bands. Within a route you still pay for renters and non-solar homes, so expect waste (guess). Address-level targeting (existing-solar homes) needs a consumer list or the operator's own permit/NEM data, mailed via targeted (non-EDDM) mail.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether Project Sunroof or other tools give route-level solar presence. Did not open DG Stats column layout.

## 5. Offers, creative, cadence, seasonality

### Takeaway
The "orphaned-installer" angle and free evaluation are the operator's own plan; no published tests were found for them. Cadence guidance converges on three touches spaced 3 to 6 weeks apart; seasonality evidence was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Three touches ~21 days apart is the common rule of thumb; optimum varies by brand/market — [Click2Mail three-touch rule](https://blog.click2mail.com/2025/01/16/the-three-touch-rule-building-an-effective-direct-mail-sequence/); [Lob frequency](https://www.lob.com/blog/marketing-postcard-frequency-how-often-is-too-often) (3 to 6 weeks baseline); [Postalytics](https://www.postalytics.com/blog/multi-touch-direct-mail-campaigns/)
- Home-service guides: ~3 touches over 6 to 8 weeks; claimed 20 to 40% lift by 3rd/4th mailing and "3 touches to one list beats one drop to 3x names" (uncited) — same results; treat as unverified
- ATTN suggests at most one mailing every 21 to 30 days for home categories — [ATTN](https://www.attnagency.com/blog/direct-mail-retargeting-roi-maximization-strategies-2026)

### Inferences
- Reasonable creative test (guess): A) "Is your solar installer still in business? Free system check", B) "Check your solar production / bill" with QR and phone number. Single offer, one call-to-action, tracked phone number and QR per version.
- Avoid dropping where heavy summer heat/holidays crowd mailboxes; no data found either way.

### Gaps

## 6. California compliance

### Takeaway
License number in all advertising is required (B&P 7030.5). The Solar Energy System Disclosure applies to contracts, not mailers. Call-recording consent and TCPA rules matter for call-ins and callbacks; counsel should review.

### Cited Findings
- B&P §7030.5: licensees must include license number in contracts, subcontracts/bids and all forms of advertising; CSLB civil penalty $100 to $1,000 for a first offense — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-3/chapter-9/article-2/section-7030-5/); [CSLB newsletter 2019](https://www2.cslb.ca.gov/Newsletter/2019-Spring/3-Advertising-Rules.asp); specifics in CCR §861
- Solar Energy System Disclosure Document must be on the front/cover page of solar contracts in 16-pt bold, per CSLB; a CSLB Summer 2026 newsletter refers to a 5-day cancellation version vs older 3-day — unresolved — [CSLB Solar Requirements](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Solar_Requirements.aspx); [CSLB Summer 2026](https://cslb.ca.gov/Resources/Newsletters/2026/CLC_Newsletter_Summer2026.pdf)
- Call recording: Penal Code §632 (confidential calls) needs all-party consent; §632.7 covers cell/cordless; up to $5,000 per violation; 2024 appellate decisions touch callback/voicemail systems, holdings unconfirmed — [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/call-recording-cases-on-hold-court-of-appeal-rings-in-new-year-with-landmark-cipa-decision.html); [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/plows-counsel-and-the-other-cipa-docket-why-§632-7-cellular-recording-cases-are-the-quietly-larger-half-of-californias-wiretap-wave/)
- A secondary source says §7159 bars false savings advertising — unverified summary — [terms.law](https://terms.law/california-solar-contractor-compliance/)

### Inferences
- Because the operator's own sales model uses a contractor-partner (Cosmic), whose license holds the classification, the mailer may need the licensed contractor's number and name to appear (confirm with counsel/CSLB; guess).
- Make no savings guarantees or manufacturer-affiliation claims; script an all-party recording disclosure at call-in.

### Gaps
- Did not read CCR §861 or the CSLB Advertising Guidelines text; no TCPA-specific finding for mail-generated call-ins (inbound calls from the homeowner are generally lower risk than outbound telemarketing, but that was not sourced). Not legal advice.

## 7. Pilot design and break-even

### Takeaway
A defensible pilot is ~10,000 pieces over 3 touches of ~3,400 homes each, about $4,100 to $4,700 per 10,000-piece drop. Break-even depends on sale gross profit; numbers below are assumptions to replace with the operator's real data.

### Cited Findings
- All-in 10,000 pieces ≈ $4,100 to $4,700 — see Section 2 (Mailpro calculator)
- Rule of thumb of 3 touches at ~21-day spacing — see Section 5

### Inferences (all guesses; replace with actuals)
- Design: ~3,400 homes (about 6 to 10 routes in the 2 to 3 highest-solar ZIPs) mailed 3 times, 3 to 4 weeks apart = ~10,200 pieces, ~$4,600 at $0.45 all-in. A control: split two creatives (A/B) across routes, tracked phone/QR each.
- Expected leads: at 0.3% (untargeted EDDM) → ~31 responses; at 1% (hoped for existing-solar targeting) → ~100. Appointments held ~40% → 12 to 40. Close rate for diagnostic/repair-to-sale assumed 20 to 30% → ~3 to 12 sales (if appointments are paid diagnostics, add that revenue).
- Break-even: spend $4,600; at an assumed $1,500 gross profit per sale, ~3 sales to break even; at $500 per sale (diagnostic only), ~9. Pilot succeeds if cost per held appointment ≤ ~$300 and ≥1 sale per ~$1,500 of spend; kill or retarget if <0.2% response after touch 2.
- Duration: ~10 to 12 weeks incl. 2 to 3 weeks after the last drop for responses.

### Gaps
- Operator's gross profit per sale, close rate and existing-solar density per route are needed; not provided here.
