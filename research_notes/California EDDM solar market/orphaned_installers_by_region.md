## Where were the closed installers' California installs concentrated?

### Takeaway
No public source quantified stranded customers by California region. Only fragments exist: Freedom Forever is Temecula (Riverside Co.) based, Simply Solar is Rancho Cucamonga (Inland Empire), Sunworks was Roseville (Sacramento area), Harness Power was Pasadena, and Complete Solar's largest counties are San Bernardino, Los Angeles and San Diego. Regional ranking must come from interconnection or permit data.

### Cited Findings
- Sunworks, Inc. and three affiliates filed Chapter 7 on Feb 5, 2024 (D. ### Inferences
- Inland Empire / Riverside / San Bernardino looks richest for 2026-vintage distress (Freedom Forever HQ, Simply Solar HQ, Complete Solar's top county), but this rests on HQ location and one vendor profile, not install counts.
- Sacramento region is the natural Sunworks pocket; Bay Area / San Jose the SunPower-heritage pocket (SunPower's historical base is the Bay Area per general knowledge, not confirmed by a source here).

- Pink Energy (SC/NC-based) and Vision Solar (not shown as CA-heavy) are probably minor in CA; unverified.
- Sunnova / SunStrong systems are "serviced" for a fee, so those homes are less cleanly "orphaned" than Titan/Sunworks Chapter 7 customers (Titan/Sunworks warranties lost per Bloomberg Law via search summary).

## Which cities/counties publish solar permit data, and which vendors cover them?

### Takeaway
Open contractor-level permit data is patchy: San Jose has a CKAN open-data portal (contractor field unconfirmed); Sacramento appears in aggregators sourced from city feeds; no official open portal found for Riverside or Fresno. Vendors exist (Shovels, PermitMint, BuildZoom, Construction Monitor via LevelSet) but California coverage and cost are only partly verified.

### Cited Findings
- San Jose publishes building permits on data.sanjoseca.gov (CKAN, CC0, daily reloads) — [Firecrawl listing](https://www.firecrawl.dev/alexandria/agents/providers/data-sanjoseca-gov)
- PermitMint publishes per-contractor pages (Complete Solar, SunPower Corporation Systems, Vivint Solar Developer, Simply Solar) with Sacramento and San Jose permit counts, claiming city open-data sources (unverified) — [PermitMint Complete Solar](https://permitmint.com/contractors/ca/complete-solar-inc/); [PermitMint SunPower](https://permitmint.com/contractors/ca/sunpower-corporation-systems/)
- City of Sacramento uses a Public Permit Portal with SolarAPP+; no bulk download described — [Sacramento](https://cityofsacramento.gov/community-development/building/permit-services/residential-solar)
- SolarAPP+ lists City of Riverside and County of Fresno as fully adopted for PV, with counts (status table, not a dataset) — [SolarAPP Foundation](https://gosolarapp.org:443/adoption_at_a_glance)
- Shovels API: 130M+ permits, 2.3M+ contractor profiles; jurisdiction count disputed (1,800+ vs 2,800+); third-party guide cites $599/mo Basic and $999/mo Pro, flagged as unverified and internally inconsistent; confirm at shovels.ai/pricing — [Shovels API docs](https://shovels-v2.redoc.ly); [API Evangelist](https://apis.apievangelist.com/store/shovels-api)
- Apify scrapers cover ~11 city/county open-data APIs, from $1.60 per 1,000 permits — [Apify](https://apify.com/lentic_clockss/us-building-permits-scraper.md)
- BuildZoom has a solar page; LevelSet permit pages (Construction Monitor-sourced) skew commercial and show odd coverage — [BuildZoom](https://www.buildzoom.com/blog/solar); [LevelSet](https://www.levelset.com/contractors/california-solar-integrators/permits)

- Shovels is the most plausible statewide contractor-name source, but verify price and CA city coverage directly.

## Is CA interconnection (DG Stats) data obtainable by region?

### Takeaway
Yes at some level: California DG Stats offers downloadable interconnected-project-site data (current as of Feb 28, 2026, monthly archives through Jan 31, 2026), one row per project site, with a data key and Read Me. Whether installer name and street address are in the files is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Downloads page lists the Interconnected Project Sites dataset and a separate Applications dataset; Project Sites covers only interconnected solar PV NEM projects; PG&E generator/inverter columns were erroneously duplicated in 2024 and later fixed — [CA DG Stats downloads (via search summary)](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/downloads); [archives](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/archives/interconnection_rule21_projects)
- Solar.com's installer tallies rely on PG&E, SCE and SDG&E interconnection records, so such records carry installer names at least at the system level — [Solar.com county page](https://www.solar.com/best-solar-companies/california/los-angeles-county/)
- DG Stats also hosts a "Find an Active Solar or Storage Installer" tool — [DG Stats](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/)

### Inferences
- If the files include installer name plus ZIP/city, counting rows by defunct-installer name per ZIP would answer the regional question directly and for free. This is the first thing to test.
- Municipal utilities (LADWP, SMUD, etc.) are likely missing or partial.

### Gaps
- Exact fields (installer, street address vs ZIP-only), not verifiable: the DG Stats site could not be fetched here. Download the .xlsx data key and Read Me directly.

## Which regions have the highest share of NEM 1.0/2.0 systems more than 8 years old?

### Takeaway
No regional breakdown found. Statewide, about 60% of NEM systems at end-2019 were NEM 1.0 and 40% NEM 2.0; NEM 2.0 began mid-2016 to mid-2017 by utility, so anything interconnected before ~late 2018 is 8+ years old (arithmetic, not sourced).

### Cited Findings
- By end of 2019 over 1 million NEM generators on the three big IOUs, ~60% NEM 1.0, ~40% NEM 2.0 — [UCR summary of CPUC data (via search)](https://sciencetopolicy.ucr.edu/media/1451/download)
- NEM 2.0 transition dates mid-2016 to mid-2017 by IOU — same source
- Grandfathering is 20 years, but sources disagree on the start (interconnection date vs PTO date) — [Solar.com explainer](https://solar.com/learn/nem-3-0-proposal-and-impacts-for-california-homeowners/); the 20-year-from-application rule in CLAUDE.md may also differ
- AB 942 was reported as possibly ending NEM 1.0 early on July 1, 2026; status unverified here — [SolarReviews](https://www.solarreviews.com/blog/california-ab942-break-solar-promises)

### Inferences
- Older-than-8-years skew should favor early solar markets (Bay Area, Sacramento, LA, Inland Empire) over fast-growing later ones; an inference only.
- A homeowner with a NEM 1.0/2.0 system should add a battery, not panels (CLAUDE.md NEM notes), which fits the battery/repair pitch.

### Gaps
- County-level NEM vintage shares: not found; derive from DG Stats by interconnection date and county.
- Confirm AB 942 outcome before any NEM-based messaging.

## Region news, complaints and receptiveness (CSLB, news, BBB)

### Takeaway
Consumer-harm evidence is thin and mostly national or for non-target regions; the best California items are Bay Area SunPower complaints, a CSLB accusation against SunPower, and the Freedom Forever/Simply Solar events in Inland Empire.

### Cited Findings
- ABC7: Bay Area SunPower customers say they are out thousands; story references a CSLB accusation — [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/san-jose-sunpower-solar-panels-bankruptcy-cslb-accusation/15410343/)
- Bloomberg Law: customers of liquidated installers (Vision, Titan) lost warranties, often stuck on leases; as unsecured creditors typically recover nothing — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloombergtax.com/california-brief/solar-panel-bankruptcies-leave-frustrated-homeowners-in-limbo)
- CA residential installs fell 45% 2023→2024 per one source, tied to the April 2023 NEM 3.0 change — [search-summary, unverified original](https://www.pv-tech.org/could-californias-residential-solar-fortunes-turn-despite-nem-3-0/)
- Freedom Forever: a private California consultancy reports more cancellation requests (self-interested, unverified) — [24-7 Press Release](https://www.24-7pressrelease.com/press-release/536853/california-solar-exit-reports-rise-in-solar-cancellation-requests-following-freedom-forever-bankruptcy-filing)
- CSLB bond is small ($25,000, shared across all claimants per one industry source) — [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/your-solar-company-went-out-of-business/)

### Inferences
- Freedom Forever PTO-stranded homes (Inland Empire heartland) is the freshest, most receptive audience in the state right now, and matches CLAUDE.md's PTO-completion service.
- Receptiveness elsewhere is unmeasured; do not claim regional complaint rates.
