## 1. Travel from San Diego: flights, drives, day-trip feasibility

### Takeaway
Northern California is reachable with frequent nonstops (SFO, OAK, SJC, SMF, FAT), so a same-day round trip is feasible but long. Santa Barbara has no nonstop from SAN, and the LA/OC/Inland Empire markets are drive-distance from San Diego. Fares are typically low (about $60–$160 round trip in promos).

### Cited Findings
- SAN–SFO nonstops exist; sources disagree on the carriers (United, Southwest, Alaska, Frontier, Virgin Australia listed variously). Check current schedules. — [FlightConnections SAN–SFO](https://flightconnections.com/flights-from-san-to-sfo); [Roame SAN–SFO](https://roame.travel/flightmap/SAN/SFO)
- Alaska added 4 daily year-round SAN–OAK nonstops starting April 22, 2026; Southwest also flies SAN–OAK, with an average flight time of about 1h38m (another source says 2h15m). — [RoutesOnline](https://www.routesonline.com/airports/6881/oakland-san-francisco-bay-airport/news/299665956/alaska-airlines-announces-san-diego-nonstops-at-oak); [Southwest SAN–OAK](https://www.southwest.com/en/flights/flights-from-san-diego-to-oakland); [FlightsFrom SAN–OAK](https://www.flightsfrom.com/SAN-OAK)
- SAN–SJC: Southwest is listed at 11–21 flights/day and Alaska also serves SJC (aggregator data of mixed dates). — [AirportOverview SAN](https://www.airportoverview.com/routes/SAN)
- SAN–SMF: Southwest and Alaska each run roughly 10–20 flights/day (aggregator); Southwest lists about 1h42m, about 480 miles. Promo round trips of $117–$157 nonstop were reported. — [FlightConnections SAN–SMF](https://www.flightconnections.com/flights-from-san-to-smf); [Southwest SAN–SMF](https://www.southwest.com/en/flights/flights-from-san-diego-to-sacramento); [Slickdeals $157 RT](https://slickdeals.net/f/20171970-round-trip-sacramento-ca-san-diego-ca-or-vice-versa-157-nonstop-airfares-on-southwest-or-alaska-airlines-be-travel-november-march-2027)
- SAN–FAT: Alaska and Southwest nonstops, about 47 weekly departures, 1h15m–1h32m flight time; a promo round trip was $97 (basic economy), and another estimate was about $200 round trip. — [DirectFlights SAN–FAT](https://www.directflights.com/SAN-FAT); [FlightsFrom SAN–FAT](https://www.flightsfrom.com/SAN-FAT); [Slickdeals $97](https://slickdeals.net/f/19241074-round-trip-san-diego-fresno-ca-yosemite-sequoia-nat-l-parks-or-vice-versa-97-nonstop-airfares-on-alaska-or-southwest-airlines-be-spring-travel-march-may-2026)
- SBA–SAN: one source states no nonstop service exists; connections only. — [AirportOverview SBA–SAN](https://www.airportoverview.com/routes/SBA/SAN)
- San Diego–Los Angeles by car: about 120 miles via I-5, about 2–3 hours normally and over 4 hours in rush hour (low-quality aggregator pages). — [Pompeii Limousine](https://pompeiilimousine.com/san-diego-los-angeles-drive-time/); [DistanceFrom](https://distancefrom.co/distance/los-angeles-to-san-diego/)

### Inferences
- Day trips to SFO/OAK/SJC/SMF/FAT are feasible with an early out/late return, since flights are about 1.3–1.7 hours. Airport-to-job drive time in the Bay Area is an added cost (UNSOURCED). Weekly travel is workable; daily is not.
- ESTIMATE (not sourced): Orange County about 1–1.5h and Inland Empire (Riverside/San Bernardino) about 1.5–2h by car from San Diego; Santa Barbara/Ventura about 3.5–4.5h by car. LA basin and OC are routinely day-trip distance, and traffic is the main variable.
- Santa Barbara is best served by driving or by a Bay-Area-style hub flight (SBA via connection is poor), so it is the worst-connected candidate for an owner traveling frequently.

### Gaps
- No sourced drive times for OC, Inland Empire, Santa Barbara or Ventura (search returned only SD–LA).
- No verified current round-trip fare averages or exact flight frequency from airline schedules; figures are from aggregators and time-limited promos.
- Central Coast (San Luis Obispo/Monterey) airport options were not researched.

## 2. Supply of Tesla Powerwall-certified installers and Axia/QCells partners

### Takeaway
No reliable regional count of Tesla Certified Installers was found; Tesla's own locator is the way to get one. Axia by Qcells appears to be a direct-to-consumer installer operating its own business in California rather than a network of third-party partners, so "Axia partner availability" by region is not publicly documented.

### Cited Findings
- Tesla claimed over 2,000 certified installers in 14 countries (Sept 2024), without a state breakdown; its support page says to enter a zip code in the finder tool for local Certified Installers. — [Electrek, Sept 2024](https://electrek.co/2024/09/25/tesla-calls-certify-energy-installers-as-it-winds-down-solar-installations/); [Tesla support](https://tesla.com/support/energy/how-to-find-certified-installer)
- Tesla also designates some installers as "Premier" certified; San Diego installers advertise this status (installer marketing, not a count). — [Stellar Solar](https://stellarsolar.net/tesla-powerwall-installation-san-diego/)
- Axia by Qcells is the direct-to-consumer division of Qcells North America (Hanwha), with a California office in Irvine; California work is residential only, in California and Texas; Solar.com shows 1,537+ residential systems in investor-owned-utility interconnection records through May 2026, a 4.3 Google rating, and rank #193 of 240 scored California installers. — [Solar.com Axia](https://www.solar.com/installers/axia-solar/) (via search snippet; page itself could not be fetched); [EnergySage Axia](https://www.energysage.com/supplier/26162/axia-solar)
- A Stellar Solar page presents it as an Axia partner; this is installer marketing and not confirmation of a formal network. — [Stellar Solar](https://stellarsolar.net/?p=9011)

### Inferences
- Because Tesla Certified status is open to any qualified licensed contractor, supply likely tracks the overall solar/electrical contractor density (large in the Bay Area, LA/OC, Inland Empire and Sacramento; thinner in Fresno, Santa Barbara and the Central Coast). This is inference, not a cited count.
- Axia's California footprint appears concentrated around its Irvine base (Southern California); whether Axia serves Northern California with its own crews or through subcontractors is unknown.

### Gaps
- No count of Tesla Certified Installers per region/county (the locator could not be queried).
- No official Axia service-area map or partner-installer program details; need to ask Axia directly.
- No C-10/C-46 license counts by region (CSLB data not retrieved).

## 3. Virtual evaluation plus local electrician; electrician rates and labor-cost differences

### Takeaway
Virtual site assessments are a recognized practice with installer software support, and California just enacted remote-inspection authority. Bay Area electrician rates are higher than San Diego, but exact BLS medians for the Bay Area could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Installer tools (e.g., Solargraf's Virtual Site Assessment) let homeowners upload photos/measurements through an emailed link, or a rep fill the form; roof assessment is typically the first step. — [Solargraf](https://www.solargraf.com/how-to-use-solargraf-to-perform-a-virtual-site-assessment/); [EnergySage](https://news.energysage.com/how-to-prepare-for-virtual-site-visit/)
- Many installers want a signed contract or small deposit before doing a virtual site visit. — [EnergySage](https://news.energysage.com/how-to-prepare-for-virtual-site-visit/)
- Bay Area electrician pricing: about $50–$130/hour plus a $100–$200 service-call fee (Angi via a 2026 rates article; Housecall Pro national guide gives the same range). Weaker sources estimate a San Jose diagnostic average of about $165 and an Oakland average of about $150. — [Silicon Review, Oct 2026](https://thesiliconreview.com/2026/10/bay-area-electrician-rates-2026); [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-to-price-electrical-work/); [ConsumerAffairs SF](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/homeowners/electricians-san-francisco.html)
- Wage medians (aggregator republishing BLS-style data): Fresno electrician median about $71,710 (May 2025); San Diego about $70,800–$76,160 (May 2025, sources conflict). No clean Bay Area electrician figure was found; a $101,400 SF figure is unverified, and a $61,260 figure is for helpers and should be ignored. — [Wagedex Fresno](https://wagedex.com/jobs/electricians/metro/fresno-ca); [SalaryMetro San Diego](https://salarymetro.pages.dev/salary/electrician/san-diego-ca/)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE: Bay Area labor likely runs well above San Diego/Fresno (perhaps 20–40% on wages) given the general pattern of higher Bay Area wage scales, but this is not confirmed by a cited BLS table.
- A hybrid model (virtual evaluation, then a local electrician for diagnostics) is workable, and the key unknown is local electrician availability willing to take a flat-fee diagnostic.

### Gaps
- Official BLS OEWS May 2025 electrician wages for SF-Oakland, San Jose, Sacramento, LA, Riverside and Santa Barbara metros were not retrieved.
- Diagnostic rates for non-Bay-Area regions were not found.

## 4. Permitting friction for battery retrofits and solar repair

### Takeaway
State law requires most California jurisdictions to offer automated SolarAPP+ permitting for standard solar and storage, which narrows regional permit friction; panel upgrades and utility interconnection are the real timeline drivers.

### Cited Findings
- State law required cities/counties over 50,000 residents to adopt SolarAPP+ by Sept 30, 2023 and smaller ones by Sept 30, 2024; over 280 jurisdictions (161 in California) had adopted it. — [Public CEO](https://www.publicceo.com/2023/03/state-law-requires-cities-to-adopt-solar-permitting-platform/); [AVA Energy](https://avaenergy.org/?p=17021)
- NREL pilot: SolarAPP+ projects had a median review time of zero days (instant) versus a median of nine business days for traditional permits; pre-SolarAPP+ California averages were 13–19 days. — [NREL](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy22osti/81603.pdf); [NRG Clean Power guide](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/solar-permit-california/)
- Battery additions use the same streamlined path, but main panel upgrades trigger a separate electrical permit; permission to operate typically takes 2–6 weeks after inspection (vendor guide, treat cautiously). — [NRG Clean Power guide](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/solar-permit-california/)
- AB 1738 (remote virtual inspections for solar, batteries, heat pumps, new roofs) was signed Sept 18, 2026 (Chapter 271, Statutes of 2026); inspectors keep discretion to require in-person visits; compliance dates conflict across sources (July 1, 2027 vs 2028). — [Governor's office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/18/governor-newsom-issues-legislative-update-9-18-26/); [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/08/27/california-bill-enables-remote-inspection-for-residential-solar-batteries-heat-pumps-and-more/)
- General Powerwall installs are cited at 2–8 weeks, driven by permitting plus interconnection; SDG&E interconnection agreements about 2–6 weeks (installer claim). — [Stellar Solar](https://stellarsolar.net/2025/04/09/how-the-tesla-powerwall-3-and-solar-can-offset-sdge-rates-in-san-diego/)
- Statewide SGIP general-market residential budget reportedly closed in PG&E, SCE, SDG&E territory as of Oct 2026 (single source, verify). — [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/how-much-does-a-tesla-powerwall-3-cost/)

### Inferences
- Because SolarAPP+ is mandated, the Bay Area vs. SoCal difference in standard battery permits is likely smaller than historically; older/small AHJs and panel upgrades are where friction remains (inference).

### Gaps
- No Bay Area city-by-city or Powerwall-specific permit timelines; no PG&E interconnection timeline; no SB 379 compliance list by city.

## 5. Regulatory differences for an out-of-area contractor

### Takeaway
CSLB licensing is statewide, but many cities add local business-tax registration and can withhold permits until it is shown. Budget for city-by-city business licenses where jobs are done.

### Cited Findings
- No separate city contractor licenses exist, but local business tax registration and permits still apply; Business & Professions Code 7000.2 lets cities require proof of local tax compliance before issuing a permit. — [CSLB Building Officials Guide](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/GuidesAndPublications/BuildingOfficialsGuide.pdf); [BPC 7000.2](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_7000.2)
- Example: Gardena's 2026 guideline requires out-of-city contractors to obtain a business license (about $150 for A/B classes, $75 for C/D specialty, plus state fee). — [City of Gardena 2026](https://cityofgardena.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CONTRACTOR-LICENSE-GUIDELINE-2026.pdf)
- Secondary source: San Diego, LA and San Francisco each require a business tax certificate/registration for contractors. — [Bryant Surety Bonds](https://www.bryantsuretybonds.com/blog/how-to-get-a-california-contractors-license)

### Inferences
- A San Diego-licensed firm can legally work statewide under its CSLB license, but each new AHJ may need a business registration; plan for administrative cost per city.

### Gaps
- Direct-mail (EDDM) rules were not researched here. Per-city fee schedules for target markets and any C-46 license-holder requirements for the entity (versus subcontractor) were not retrieved.
