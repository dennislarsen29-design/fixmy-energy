# California regional demand (outside San Diego County and Bakersfield) for PPA/lease sales, diagnostics, repair and battery retrofits via direct mail

Research as of Nov 2026. Search-snippet based. californiadgstats.ca.gov and lab.data.ca.gov could not be fetched (not in the session's allowed network domains), so NO current county-level installed-system counts, NEM-vintage splits or system-age data were obtained. Rankings below are inferences, not measured.

## Which regions have the most and oldest installed residential solar systems?

### Takeaway
No current county or utility counts were found. Dated and statewide evidence only: LA County, San Diego and Santa Clara led rooftop capacity historically; the vast majority of rooftop customers are on NEM 1.0/2.0 (legacy), so older systems are concentrated in the big IOU territories (PG&E, SCE).

### Cited Findings
- DGStats (CPUC) covers PV systems interconnected under IOU NEM tariffs only, updated monthly with about a six-week lag; municipal utilities (SMUD, LADWP) are handled separately — [California DGStats FAQ](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/faq/)
- Circa 2013-14, LA County led rooftop capacity (171.4 MW), then San Diego (141 MW) and Santa Clara (110 MW) — [KCET](https://www.kcet.org/redefine/california-now-has-more-than-150000-solar-roofs) (old; not comparable to today)
- A residential-permit analysis (undated in the snippet) said Santa Clara County had the most permits, followed by LA and Riverside counties — [Planetizen](https://planetizen.com/node/133258)
- About 1.6 million households are grandfathered into NEM 1.0/2.0 statewide — [pv-tech / IVN summary](https://ivn.us/posts/how-californians-without-solar-got-stuck-paying-those-who-do-2025-08-19) (secondary)
- A 2025 Senate analysis said the vast majority of rooftop customers are on NEM 1.0 or NEM 2.0; no figures given — [CA Senate](https://seuc.senate.ca.gov/media/1288)
- NEM 2.0 closed to new applicants after April 14, 2023; NBT (NEM 3.0) applies since — [CPUC history of BTM generation](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/electrical-energy/demand-side-management/customer-generation/history-of-btm-renewable-energy-generation)
- NEM 2.0 projects applied for by Apr 14, 2023 get a 9-year (not 20-year) legacy period unless they meet a Feb 14, 2027 deadline (PG&E advice letter) — [PG&E ELEC 7619-E](https://www.pge.com/tariffs/assets/pdf/adviceletter/ELEC_7619-E.pdf)
- Statewide, rooftop is roughly 2 million systems / ~18,000 MW by one 2025 estimate — [Planetizen](https://planetizen.com/news/2024/12/133258-california-leads-solar-rooftops) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Most 2010-2019 vintage systems (10+ years old, out of or near end of install-company support) sit in PG&E/SCE territory; LA basin, Inland Empire, Santa Clara/East Bay and Sacramento likely hold the largest absolute counts. Unverified.
- Because SMUD and LADWP are not in DGStats, Sacramento (SMUD) and LA city (LADWP) are undercounted in IOU data.

### Gaps
- Per-county and per-utility residential counts, MW, install-year distribution, NEM1/2/3 share: DGStats, CEC and CPUC datasets were unreachable. Pull the CEC "1 MW and smaller solar PV capacity and number by county" dataset and DGStats downloads directly.

## Electricity rates by utility and region

### Takeaway
PG&E (Bay Area, Sacramento-area IOU, Central Valley) has the highest rates, SCE (LA basin, OC, Inland Empire, Ventura/Central Coast pockets) next; SMUD and LADWP are far cheaper, weakening the savings pitch there.

### Cited Findings
- PG&E average residential bundled non-CARE rate: 40.60 ¢/kWh as of Mar 1, 2026 (41.46 ¢ on Jan 1, 2026) — [PG&E rate advisory 0326](https://www.pge.com/assets/pge/docs/account/rate-plans/electric-rate-advisory-0326.pdf)
- SCE Jan 2026 average residential rate: 34.5 ¢/kWh (33.2 with Climate Credit) — [SCE rate advisory](https://www.sce.com/save-money/rates-financing/sce-rate-advisory/20260101)
- Third-party 2025 all-in prices: SMUD 18.93 ¢, LADWP 26.48 ¢, PG&E 37.52 ¢, SDG&E 45.77 ¢ — [OhmSnap](https://www.ohmsnap.com/utility-rate-comparison/california) (aggregator; treat as estimate)
- SMUD says its rates are on average more than 50% lower than neighboring PG&E; its solar export credit rose from 7.4 to 9.6 ¢/kWh on June 1, 2026 — [SMUD](https://www.smud.org/rates), [SMUD release 4/27/2026](https://www.smud.org/Corporate/About-us/News-and-Media/2026/2026/SMUD-boosts-Solar-and-Storage-Compensation-rate-for-rooftop-solar-customers)
- LADWP estimates range 22.5-28 ¢/kWh depending on source — [Solar.com](https://www.solar.com/learn/understanding-ladwp-electric-rates/) (secondary, conflicting)

### Inferences
- Rate-driven value ranking: PG&E territory > SCE > LADWP > SMUD.

### Gaps
- Official SMUD and LADWP 2026 residential averages not found.

## PSPS / outage exposure driving battery demand (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
PSPS volume has fallen sharply; 2025 events were small and rural/foothill. Outage-driven battery demand is thus weaker than 2019-2020 but real in North Bay/Sierra foothills, Central Coast foothills and SCE mountain/desert fringes. No consolidated county ranking was found.

### Cited Findings
- PG&E's own statement cites roughly 18,000 customers total de-energized in 2025 versus 2M+ in 2019 — [PG&E post-event report (summary via search)](https://www.pge.com/assets/pge/docs/outages-and-safety/outage-preparedness-and-support/06-19-2025-psps-post-event-report.pdf) (figure came through search snippet; verify)
- Jan 20-21, 2025 event: 583 customers in Kern County — [PG&E](https://pge.com/assets/pge/docs/outages-and-safety/outage-preparedness-and-support/01-20-2025-psps-post-event-report.pdf)
- June 19, 2025 event: Salinas Valley foothills; about 11,000-11,500 in scope across 15-16 counties, roughly 7,000 shut off — [PG&E newsroom / ABC30](https://abc30.com/post/pge-begins-public-safety-power-shutoffs-ahead-fire-weather/16792149/)
- SCE 2025 events: June 10 (969 customers, Tulare among counties) and Sept 2 (1,223 customers, Tulare) — [CPUC SCE report Jun 10](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/divisions/safety-and-enforcement-division/reports/psps-post-event-reports/2025/r1812005sce-psps-postevent-for-june-10-2025-deenergization.pdf), [Sept 2](https://webproda.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/divisions/safety-and-enforcement-division/reports/psps-post-event-reports/2025/r1812005-sce-psps-post-event-for-september-2-2025-de-energization.pdf)
- July 15, 2026 PG&E PSPS: about 7,800 customers across portions of 10 counties — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3286260/pge-monitoring-high-wind-event-is-preparing-for-possible-public-safety-power-shutoff-for-portions-of-10-counties)

### Inferences
- PSPS alone is a modest direct-mail driver outside foothill ZIPs; backup/VPP/TOU-arbitrage value is the stronger pitch in metro regions.

### Gaps
- Full 2025 and 2026 season totals, SCE totals, county breakdown, and non-PSPS reliability (SAIDI) by region not obtained.

## Battery attachment trend

### Takeaway
Statewide new-install attachment rose to about 74% in 2025 (58% in 2024). Regional split not found. Retrofit pool = pre-2023 solar-only systems.

### Cited Findings
- California residential battery attachment ~74% in 2025, up from 58% in 2024; counts only batteries paired with new solar, excluding retrofits and standalone — [LBNL Distributed Solar & Storage 2026 Data Update (via pv magazine)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/03/battery-storage-paired-with-37-of-new-u-s-residential-solar-systems-in-2025/); [LBNL 2026 update PDF](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/distributed_solar_storage-2026_data_update_final.pdf)
- About 20% of California shoppers added batteries in Oct 2023, shortly after NBT — [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2024/08/california-solar-installers-get-well-acquainted-with-batteries-in-nem-3-0-era/)
- Nationally 37% of new residential systems paired with storage in 2025 — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/10/03/battery-storage-paired-with-37-of-new-u-s-residential-solar-systems-in-2025/)
- Residential installs have been flat/low since NEM 3.0 — [pv-tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/lbnl-residential-solar-pv-installations-remain-flat-since-nem-3-0-in-california)

### Gaps
- Attachment by utility/region; retrofit counts. Not found.

## Tentative ranking of regions (inference only; not measured; excludes San Diego and Bakersfield)

Basis: rate level (PG&E>SCE>municipal), assumed size of legacy solar base, PSPS exposure, ownership/income. Treat as a hypothesis to validate with CEC/DGStats county counts.

| Rank | Diagnostics / repair / battery retrofit | PPA/lease new-system sales |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inland Empire (Riverside/San Bernardino): SCE rates, large 2012-2019 base, mid price homes | Bay Area East Bay/South Bay (highest PG&E rates) |
| 2 | Bay Area (Santa Clara, East Bay): PG&E rates, many older systems, PSPS in hills | Inland Empire |
| 3 | LA basin (SCE parts) / Orange County | Sacramento (PG&E parts; SMUD area weak) |
| 4 | Sacramento (PG&E side) | Central Valley/Fresno (PG&E, heat, lower prices) |
| 5 | Fresno/Central Valley | LA basin / OC |
| 6 | Ventura/Santa Barbara, Central Coast (smaller markets; wildfire-prone) | Ventura/Central Coast |
Municipal territories (LADWP, SMUD, Anaheim etc.) rank lowest on rates and sit outside DGStats; direct mail should exclude or de-prioritize them.
