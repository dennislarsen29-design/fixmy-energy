# California Residential Solar PPA/Lease (TPO) Market and Competitive Landscape, as of November 2026

Research limits: WebFetch was blocked for utilitydive.com and solarpowerworldonline.com (hosts not in the session's allowed network domains), so everything below comes from search-result summaries, not full pages. Several sources are installer or SEO blogs. Verify uncertain items against primary sources (CPUC DG Stats, LBNL, SEIA/Wood Mackenzie, court dockets, leginfo).

## TPO share of California residential solar vs cash/loan, and the effect of the 25D expiry

### Takeaway
TPO (lease/PPA) is now roughly 45% of California residential installs, about double its 2022 share, and it rose sharply after NEM 3.0 began in April 2023. Because 25D ended 12/31/2025, TPO is the only way to deliver the federal credit (via the commercial 48E credit); a 2026 California TPO share above 60% is circulating, but it is an unverified projection.

### Cited Findings
- CPUC interconnection data (analyzed by a vendor blog) shows TPO rising from 25% in 2022 to 45% in 2025, with LA County/SCE at 47% in 2025 — [Cali-Energy](https://cali-energy.com/blog/nem-3-solar-market-data-california)
- LBNL: TPO rose from 26% (standalone PV) and 11% (PV+storage) in the last 12 months under old NEM to 39% and 52% under NBT; LBNL attributes part of this to larger companies offering TPO, higher interest rates, and IRA bonus credits available only to third-party systems — [Utility Dive on LBNL](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/residential-solar-storage-california-after-nem-30-lbnl/716589/)
- Section 25D did not cover systems completed after Dec 31, 2025 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act); TPO owners can still claim the commercial 48E credit, generally 30%, and may pass part of the value into payments — [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/federal-solar-tax-credit-updates/) and [SunRaise Capital](https://sunraisecapital.com/blog/homeowner-solar-lease-guide-2026)
- National 2026 market contraction forecasts range from about 18–19% to 21% (SEIA/Wood Mackenzie Q2 2026 outlook); Jefferies about 30% (as summarized by search, not primary) — [Surge PV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/us-residential-solar-market-trends-2026)
- Sunrun's CEO said TPO would be over 80% of Sunrun's 2026 originations — [Sunrun filings and presentations](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_388f65e3f2a4e8bc4394537867ecfbfe/sunrun/db/395/4135/pdf/Sunrun+Investor+Presentation+-+May+2026.pdf) (the claim was relayed through a search summary, not read directly)
- A secondary source cites CALSSA saying California solar sales fell 60–80% year-over-year after NEM 3.0 — [Surge PV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/us-residential-solar-market-trends-2026)
- Unverified: a vendor blog projects TPO at up to 65–69% of sales in 2026 vs about 44–45% in 2025 (national or unclear) — [Vahorizon](https://www.vahorizon.site/solar/blog/tpo-majority-market-selling-solar-2026/); contradicts nothing directly but has no named primary source
- A Wood Mackenzie figure of TPO going from 40% to 52% of residential volume during 2024 appears in secondary coverage (national) — [Greenlancer](https://www.greenlancer.com/post/solar-tax-credit-ending)

### Inferences
- The 45% (2025) figure plus loss of 25D for cash/loan buyers makes a 2026 California TPO share in the 55–70% range plausible, but no measured 2026 number was found.
- The 25D expiry plausibly shrinks the cash/loan segment more than TPO, since TPO can still monetize 48E; TPO share rises even as total volume falls.

### Gaps
- No measured 2026 California TPO share (CPUC DG Stats / SEIA Q3 2026 not read).
- No Wood Mackenzie primary report was accessible.
- Whether 48E for TPO residential leases faces future restrictions (e.g., safe-harbor or FEOC rules) was not confirmed; one source says it depends on providers' safe-harbored projects.

## Competitors by region and exits/bankruptcies

### Takeaway
Sunrun dominates TPO (nationally about 19% of installs and 37% of subscription volume per its investor deck; California over 45% of its customer base), with Tesla mainly a battery/hardware player. A wave of 2024–2026 failures (SunPower, Titan, Sunnova, Mosaic, PosiGen, Freedom Forever) has left many orphaned California systems; regional share data by metro (Bay Area, Sacramento, Central Valley, LA/OC/Inland Empire) was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Sunrun: about 19% share of residential install volume and 37% of subscription volume per its May 2026 deck citing Wood Mackenzie/SEIA March 2026; another source says 12.7% national share in 2025 (metric likely differs) — [Sunrun May 2026 deck](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_388f65e3f2a4e8bc4394537867ecfbfe/sunrun/db/395/4135/pdf/Sunrun+Investor+Presentation+-+May+2026.pdf)
- Sunrun says California is over 45% of its customer base (as of Sept 30, 2026) — [Sunrun 10-Q Q3 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001469367/000162828026070158/run-20260930.htm)
- Tesla Powerwalls supplied 517 of 580 MW in a September California grid dispatch (~110,000 Powerwalls); Sunrun owns or operates 55% of those — [Electrek, Oct 14 2026](https://electrek.co/2026/10/14/tesla-powerwall-sunrun-record-745-mw-california-grid-dispatch/)
- Tesla residential solar share of 5–7% is an unsourced blog estimate on 2024 data (weak) — [LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/tesla-sunrun-customer-acquisition-strategies/)
- Freedom Forever (Temecula, CA): Chapter 11 filed Apr 15, 2026; converted to Chapter 7 liquidation Aug 7, 2026 per trackers; no successor servicer listed; workmanship and production warranty become claims against the estate (claim deadline reported as 2026-10-16, already passed) — [Solar Power World, Apr 2026](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/04/residential-solar-installer-freedom-forever-files-bankruptcy/), [Home Repair Dispatch tracker](https://homerepairdispatch.com/solar-company-bankruptcy-tracker/); the bankruptcy court is reportedly determining who takes lease/PPA contracts
- Sunnova: Chapter 11 June 2025; assets sold to Solaris Assets, plan confirmed Nov 2025; customers moved to SunStrong, with one report of systems "unrepaired for months" — [SolarDisclosure tracker](https://solardisclosure.io/solar-installer-bankruptcy-tracker.html), [NRG Clean Power guide](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/your-solar-company-went-out-of-business/)
- Mosaic (lender, Oakland CA): Chapter 11 June 2025; a servicer bought it and payment terms stay the same — [Home Repair Dispatch](https://homerepairdispatch.com/solar-company-bankruptcy-tracker/)
- PosiGen: ceased most operations Aug 2025, Chapter 11 Nov 24, 2025, liquidating plan effective Feb 26, 2026; leases sold to Renewbrook and SunStrong (case 25-90787, S.D. Tex.) — [Vahorizon timeline](https://www.vahorizon.site/solar/blog/solar-bankruptcy-timeline/)
- Titan Solar: Chapter 7 June 2024. SunPower: Chapter 11 Aug 2024; Blue Raven and dealer network sold to Complete Solaria ($45M), which rebranded as SunPower in 2025 — [Vahorizon bankruptcy list](https://www.vahorizon.site/solar/statistics/bankruptcies/)
- Scale: trackers count 26 installers/financiers failed since 2017 or 100+ US solar companies since 2023; counts conflict — [Solar Power World, Jan 2026](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/01/solar-layoffs-and-closures-run-rampant-in-trumps-first-year-back/)
- California-specific: a homeowner can file a proof of claim through the claims agent (Kroll/Stretto), and the CSLB Solar Energy System Disclosure Document can support contract-violation claims — [NRG Clean Power](https://nrgcleanpower.com/learning-center/your-solar-company-went-out-of-business/)

### Inferences
- Because Sunrun, Tesla and Axia/Qcells are the scaled TPO/battery names left, a small partner company most plausibly competes on local service, repair and orphan-customer rescue rather than head-to-head new TPO sales.
- Freedom Forever's liquidation leaves a large Inland Empire/Southern California orphan pool (Temecula base), though customer counts were not found.

### Gaps
- No regional (Bay Area, Sacramento, Central Valley, LA/OC/IE) competitor share data.
- No confirmation of which entity took over Freedom Forever leases or whether Sunnova/Freedom Forever customers in California are being serviced (blogs conflict).
- Names of regional TPO players (e.g., Palmetto/LightReach installers, Sunnova dealers, Zeo, etc.) not researched.

## Demand for add-on batteries and for repair/monitoring/diagnostics

### Takeaway
Storage is now near-standard on new California solar (about 74% of 2025 new systems per LBNL), but no measured demand figure for retrofits onto existing solar or for repair/diagnostic services was found; only cost and anecdotal signals exist.

### Cited Findings
- About 74% of new California residential solar systems in 2025 included storage, up from 58% in 2024 (excludes later retrofits and standalone batteries) — [Utility Dive on LBNL](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/residential-solar-storage-california-after-nem-30-lbnl/716589/) and [Surge PV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-storage-attachment-rates-comparison); other figures (94% Q3 2026, 69%, 35–45%) conflict and come from low-quality sites
- Battery attached to 37% of new US residential systems in 2025 — [pv magazine via Now.solar, Oct 2026](https://now.solar/2026/10/03/battery-storage-paired-with-37-of-new-u-s-residential-solar-systems-in-2025-pv-magazine-global/)
- Retrofit cost: single battery about $11,000–$17,500 installed before incentives, plus about $1,500–$3,000 extra permitting/mobilization versus bundling (vendor estimate) — [HomeLink Solar](https://homelinksolar.com/add-battery-to-existing-solar-california/)
- Powerwall fleet is large enough to dispatch 745 MW record in California grid events, suggesting VPP programs exist as a battery value driver — [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/10/14/tesla-powerwall-sunrun-record-745-mw-california-grid-dispatch/)

### Inferences
- A large pool of 2016–2022 NEM 1.0/2.0 systems without storage exists; NEM 3.0 economics and VPP incentives support battery retrofit demand, but size of that market was not quantified.
- Orphaned-system closures (above) create repair/monitoring demand; no survey or revenue data found.

### Gaps
- No retrofit attach-rate, count of NEM 1.0/2.0 systems without storage, or repair/monitoring market size.
- SGIP/ELRP incentive amounts and availability in 2026 were not verified.

## Axia/Qcells and TPO lender requirements; California contractor rules

### Takeaway
Axia (Qcells' residential arm, California and Texas) works with Qcells' Q.PARTNER program and EnFin; no public Axia partner terms were found. Lender FICO floors cluster around 620–670 with conflicting sources, and California contractor rules (CSLB license, disclosure document, 3-day cancel, down-payment cap) apply to any seller.

### Cited Findings
- Axia is Qcells' residential arm offering solar, storage, EV chargers in California and Texas; Q.PARTNER is Qcells' installer program; Axia's 30-year warranty applies when financed through EnFin's loan — [EnergySage Axia profile](https://www.energysage.com/supplier/26162/axia-solar), [pv-tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/qcells-rolls-out-leasing-led-solar-platform-for-us-residential-sector/)
- LightReach: FICO 650–670 (sources conflict; 660 also cited), no bankruptcies in a 7-year lookback, DTI not factored, score-based monthly payment caps (e.g., $395 for 700–724 in California per one table), homeowner must be on title, installer affirms site eligibility, soft pull only — [Palmetto help center](https://help.palmetto.finance/en/articles/8284543-solar-energy-plan-credit-parameters-sending-contracts), [8M Solar](https://8msolar.com/what-is-lightreach-solar-financing/)
- EnFin: minimum FICO 620 in one dealer knowledge base (which lists single-family residential as not supported there), versus 650+ elsewhere — conflicting — [Equity Solar knowledge base](https://equitysolarknowledgebase.helpkit.so/financing-options/5YFtbshPwbzKpnw8R4NoYe/enfin/7ZvGvy6PuQhcmaoyYGXxZJ), [Heliogold](https://heliogold.com/financing/enfin-solar)
- CSLB license must be active in C-46 (Solar), C-10 (Electrical) or B (General Building); door-to-door salespeople need a home improvement salesperson registration — [CPUC CSLB disclosure page](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/electrical-energy/demand-side-management/customer-generation/california-solar-consumer-protection-guide/cslb-disclosure-documents)
- Solar Energy System Disclosure Document must be on the contract cover page in 16-point bold (BPC 7169); CSLB's document has a 3-business-day and a newer 5-day-cancel version; the longer Supporting Information document has applied since Nov 1, 2025 — [CSLB disclosure document](https://web.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/Contractors/SolarDisclosureDoc.pdf), [CSLB Summer 2026 newsletter](https://cslb.ca.gov/Resources/Newsletters/2026/CLC_Newsletter_Summer2026.pdf)
- BPC 7159: contract must show name as on CSLB license, address, license number; down payment capped at the lesser of $1,000 or 10% (§7159.5); home-solicitation sales carry a 3-business-day right to cancel (Civil Code 1689.5 et seq.) — [WattBuild](https://www.wattbuild.com/blog/california-solar-contract-cancellation-rights) and [BreakYourSolarContract](https://breakyoursolarcontract.com/solar-contract-laws/california) (consumer blogs)
- BPC 7030.5 reportedly requires the license number on contracts, bids, and advertising — secondary source, see [ContractBuilderAI](https://contractbuilderai.com/blog/what-contractor-license-information-must-appear-in-a-california-home-improvement-contract)

### Inferences
- EDDM mailers need the CSLB license number printed; the partner's (or company's) license number must appear, and registration of door-to-door sales reps is required.
- A small non-licensed marketer partnering with a licensed contractor must ensure the contracting entity's license appears on all ads and contracts.

### Gaps
- No primary-source Axia or Qcells installer requirements, roof or utility territory rules.
- Participate Energy's credit/roof requirements not found.
- Claims of a five-day cancellation for seniors "under SB 784 (2026)" and of "SB 1022" were not confirmed; treat as unverified. Home-solicitation thresholds ($25 vs $500) conflict across blogs.
- No found rules specific to direct mail (EDDM) content beyond license-number advertising.
