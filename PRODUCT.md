## Platform

web

## Users
Primary (confirmed 2026-11-24): homeowners who already have rooftop solar that is broken, underperforming, or unmonitored, very often because the company that installed it went out of business (SunPower, Titan, Sunnova, Freedom Forever and others) or no longer answers. They usually arrive from a search for their installer's name, an inverter error code, a "solar not producing" problem, or a paid ad, often on a phone, and want to know what is wrong and who can fix it.

Secondary, in this order: homeowners adding a Tesla Powerwall battery to existing solar; homeowners buying new solar (sold through the Axia/QCells partnership). The public site leads with repair.

Other audiences with their own pages: job applicants (careers, electricians) and existing customers (portal, Sign & Pay, proposals). The portal (`portal.html`) is an internal operations tool, not a marketing surface.

## Product Purpose
Solar Review (domain fixmy.energy) diagnoses and fixes home solar systems from any installer: diagnostics, inverter replacements, warranty claims (RMAs), battery add-ons, system expansions and new solar. The website's job is to turn a worried homeowner into a booked evaluation. Success = a booked evaluation (the `/book` → `/booked` flow, or a call to (619) 777-6527).

Terminology: an **Evaluation** is the free first visit or call by a Tech. A **Diagnostic** is the paid on-site visit by a licensed electrician, and its fee is credited toward any work the customer approves.

## Positioning
The company that shows up for orphaned solar customers: it services systems from any installer, including companies that have closed, starting with a free evaluation that has no commitment. It is owner-led: Dennis, a homeowner with about ten years in the solar industry, started it after the company he worked for went bankrupt and he kept meeting homeowners nobody would help.

## Operating Context
- Visitors book on `/book` (time slots are shown directly in the form; keep that page free of extra fields) or call. Far-away homes get a virtual evaluation.
- Installer pages (`/sunpower`, `/titan`, …, built into `installers/` from `check-preview.html` data), city/county pages (`solar-repair-*.html`) and blog posts carry search traffic; problem pages cover inverter replacement, monitoring offline, panels not producing, Powerwall add-on.
- Service area named in marketing: California (mainly San Diego and Southern California), Utah, Texas, Wyoming and Colorado. The team is San Diego-based; county/city pages must not imply local offices that don't exist.
- Static HTML on Netlify, no build step; several pages are generated by scripts in `scripts/` (re-run the script instead of hand-editing the output).

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Never state or imply a partnership or certification with a manufacturer unless documented.
- No invented statistics, savings figures, prices, incentive amounts, guarantees, or "#1/best" claims in marketing copy. Incentives may be mentioned as existing, never with amounts or as guaranteed.
- Visible FAQ text and its FAQPage JSON-LD must always match exactly.
- Owner names from county parcel data must never appear on a public page.
- Booking tracking (GA4, Google Ads, Meta Pixel) must keep working on `/book`, `/booked`, `/thank-you` and the installer pages.

## Product Principles
1. Answer the homeowner's real question ("what's wrong, and can someone fix it?") before asking for anything.
2. One clear next step on every page: book the free evaluation, with the phone number as a fallback.
3. Truth over persuasion: no claim the business can't back with real evidence.
4. Repair first; battery and new solar are offered when they fit the homeowner's situation, not pushed.
5. Easy on a phone: most visitors arrive on mobile from search or ads.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Working target: WCAG 2.1 AA for the public pages (contrast, labeled form fields, readable text sizes, tap targets). Not a formal legal requirement on record; many visitors are older homeowners, so readability matters.
