Your utility decides what upgrades are worth

Most of Riverside County is served by Southern California Edison (SCE). Several cities run their own electric utilities, including Riverside (Riverside Public Utilities), Corona and Moreno Valley, and parts of the Coachella Valley buy power through Desert Community Energy, a community choice program.

These differences affect export credits and rates. Systems approved before April 2023 on SCE may still be on an earlier net metering schedule, so we check your bill and approval date before suggesting changes that could alter your rate rules.

What is hard on solar here

Sustained heat is the defining condition. Inverters and power optimizers lose efficiency and live shorter lives when they run hot for months, and failures often cluster in the first summer heat wave after a mild spring. In the Coachella Valley and the Inland Empire, dust and fine soil settle on panels and cut output between rains. A system that looks fine on the app can still be losing a meaningful share of what it should make, which we check against what your roof should produce.

Common questions

Can heat really kill an inverter?

Heat shortens inverter life and can trigger faults and shutdowns. It is one of several causes we check, along with failed strings, wiring and communication problems.

Do you serve Palm Springs and the Coachella Valley?

Yes. We serve homeowners across Riverside County, including the Coachella Valley. Tell us your address when you book and we will confirm the best way to start.

My installer was Simply Solar. Now what?

We can evaluate the system, find any fault, and explain what the manufacturer warranty still covers. You do not need the original installer.

Will adding panels or a battery change my utility rules?

It can. Expanding a system can move it onto newer rules, while adding a battery generally keeps your existing arrangement. We confirm with your bill before you commit.
