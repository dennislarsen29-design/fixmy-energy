## Business Lines & Accounting SOPs (2026-07-07, per Dennis — AUTHORITATIVE)
These are Standard Operating Procedures. Do not re-ask how the business lines work.

### 1. FixMy.Energy (internal line name; customer-facing brand is Solar Review)
- **Services:** Diagnostics, Inverter Swaps, Warranty Replacements, Batteries.
- **Revenue:** Solar Review charges the CUSTOMER directly for diagnostics, inverter swaps, warranty replacements, and battery sales **when batteries are sold in-house**.   - Track operations costs of doing business per job ("sub sheet"): pending vs completed payments to ops/partners.
  - Rep commissions on sold jobs for ALL sales reps EXCEPT Dennis (Dennis-sold = no commission).
  - Future: monthly net must break out into equity distributions (design for it; don't block on it).
  - Reports must be real, easy to follow for accounting + payroll. Automate as much as possible.

### Commission math (2026-07-07, per Dennis — AUTHORITATIVE)
- **FixMy rep commission:** `Revenue − Sub-Sheet Costs = Net Revenue`; rep earns **40% of Net Revenue**. Dennis-sold jobs (rep_id `tech4`) = no commission. Commission finalizes only after job costs are entered — costs first, then commission.
- **Dialer/Setter split (2026-07-27, per Dennis — AUTHORITATIVE):** the **40%-of-net rep-pay pool is FIXED — company/equity margin is identical no matter who closes.** Rep pay is directed to whoever set/closed: **same person sets & closes → 40% to them; a distinct setter (dialer) → 20% setter / 20% closer** (still 40% total). **No owner/Dennis carve-out on the split** — zeroing a closer's share would change the margin, which must stay constant (this SUPERSEDES the old "Dennis-sold = no commission" behavior *inside the split math*; Dennis's own draw vs. distribution is a payout/distribution matter, not a margin one). Dialer = the setter role (reuses `customers.setter_name`); a Tech can dial, a setter-only can't close. The dialer's "Booked!" (`bbDialCommit`) stamps `setter_name` = the dialing rep; admin reassigning a different closer (inline Tech dropdown on the card, or the editor's Setter/Dialer + Assigned Rep selects) is what triggers the split. Computed + locked in from the FixMy job editor's accounting panel: **`edRecordFixmySplit(cid)`** recomputes net authoritatively at click time and inserts a `rep_commission` (closer, 20%/40%) and a **`setter_commission`** (setter, 20%; new `commissions.kind`, no migration — `kind` is free text) row, both `sold`→`paid`. Both book to P&L **COGS "Incentives - Sales"** (`finComputePnl`/`finMonthlySeries`) and show per-payee in the Finance **Commissions** sub-tab (`kindLbl.setter_commission='setter split'`). Worked examples (verified): PW3 $13,950−$12,500 = $1,450 net → pool $580 (same person) or $290 setter + $290 closer (split); net $4,000 → $800/$800; Diagnostic $699−$300 = $399 → $159.60 or $79.80/$79.80.
- **Top Tier / New Solar sold commissions:** entered MANUALLY per deal when the provider confirms.
- **Manager overrides (TT + NS):** paid TO Dennis — they are 1099 income to **Solar Review Corp**, not payouts. Track as receivables: override sold (owed to SRC) vs received.
- **Travel reimbursements:** Top Tier only, manual entries, owed to the rep.
- Data model: `job_costs` (per-job pending/paid cost line items) + `commissions` (kind: rep_commission | override | travel_reimbursement; status: sold | paid) tables — migration `20260707_accounting.sql`.

## Sign & Pay Flow (sign.html + sign-init.js + sign-complete.js)
This is the combined customer agreement + payment page. **DO NOT rebuild — it is already built.**

### How it works:
1. Admin/Tech confirms diagnostic fee in portal → `sign_token` generated + stored in `customers.sign_token`
2. Portal shows shareable link popup with Copy + "Text It" buttons
3. Customer opens `fixmy.energy/sign?t=TOKEN` on their phone
4. `sign-init.js` validates token, creates Stripe PaymentIntent server-side (amount locked — client cannot tamper)
5. `sign.html` shows: agreement text → typed signature field → Stripe Card Element → "Sign & Pay $X" button
6. Stripe processes card — card data never touches our servers (PCI compliant, handled by Stripe Elements)
7. On success: `sign-complete.js` verifies PaymentIntent, updates Supabase (`invoice_status=paid`, `agreement_status=signed`, clears token), fires GHL `diag-signed-and-paid` tag
8. Auto-conversion: portal detects `invoice_status=paid` + `agreement_status=signed` → converts lead to `sold_type=diagnostic` job → opens scheduling modal

### Assessor-roll owner names — trusts on the call card (2026-08-08)
Reported after the dedupe pass: the dialer queue was full of `KOKJOHN FAMILY TRUST 05-04-15`, `EDGAR THOMAS R REVOCABLE TR`, `STRICKER MICHAEL&AMIE F`.
- **The merge did not create these.** `_bbMergeFields` only ever **fills blanks**, so it surfaced `title_owner` values that already existed on one copy of a duplicate onto the survivor. More leads now show an owner name, and a large share of San Diego parcels are genuinely held in family/living trusts. To quantify: `select count(*) filter (where title_owner ~* '(TRUST|TR$|LLC|REVOCABLE)') as trusts, count(*) filter (where title_owner is not null) as with_owner from customers where lead_source='orphaned_list';`
- **No parcel API fixes a trust.** Regrid, SANDAG and the county all return the owner *of record*, and for these parcels the owner of record IS the trust. The only source of a human name is **skip-trace** (Tracerfy → `first_name`/`last_name`) — which is why `_bbLeadDisplayName` always prefers a skip-traced human over the roll string, and why the Tracerfy backfill matters more than any parcel source here.
- **`_bbOwnerParse(raw)` → `{display, surname, isEntity}`** is now the one place a roll string becomes sayable: strips the trailing recording date (`05-04-15`) and a dangling `TR`/`TRS`, title-cases while keeping real acronyms upper (`LLC`, `INC`, `JR`), and derives the **surname from the first token** — the assessor convention is surname-first for people (`STRICKER MICHAEL&AMIE`) and family-name-first for trusts (`KOKJOHN FAMILY TRUST`), so one rule covers both.
- `_bbLeadDisplayName(lead)` feeds every card (dialer current call, Up Next, Recent Calls, Black Box quarantined list, admin canvass door, rep Doors door). ⚠️ The Black Box list already had a one-off cleaner the dialer never got — **same "built in one place, not the other" pattern as the AI notes and shift control.** There is now one function; don't add a second.
- **A `TRUST` badge + "👤 Ask for: the Kokjohn household"** render on the call and door cards, and the script's Opener stage shows a red warning **not to read the entity name out loud** when the name came off the roll rather than from skip-trace.
- Verified in Chromium/Playwright (`scratchpad/test-owner-names.js`): the raw string with its recording date never reaches the card, the cleaned name + TRUST badge + ask-for line do, and a lead with real first/last beats the roll string. Gated both ways.
- **Getting an actual TRUSTEE name (2026-08-08).** Ranked by what is real:
  1. **Skip-trace — already own it, already paid for it.** Tracerfy resolves the *address* to the people living there, and for an owner-occupied trust property that person is the trustee in the overwhelming majority of cases. The exports already carry `first_name`/`last_name` (and `owner_1_first_name`/`owner_1_last_name`). **`tracerfy-backfill.js` may already hold trustee names for these exact leads** — it walks queues whose results were downloaded and discarded by the old `lead_id`-only matcher.
  2. **`_bbTrusteeNote(lead)` — free cross-check, built here.** When `title_owner` is an entity and a skip-traced surname exists, compare it to the trust's family name: match ⇒ green "✓ almost certainly the trustee"; mismatch ⇒ amber "could be a renter, or the property changed hands — confirm before using the name". The mismatch case is the valuable one; it is the difference between opening with the right name and the wrong one. Gated both ways (forcing `match = true` reproduces the wrong-name behaviour).
  3. **The recorded grant deed is the only authoritative source.** The deed that moved the property into the trust names the trustee ("JOHN A KOKJOHN AND MARY B KOKJOHN, TRUSTEES OF…"). **AB 1785 restricts what the ASSESSOR publishes, not the Recorder's index** — recorded documents remain public, searchable by APN. This is a per-parcel lookup, not something to bulk-scrape, and the county portal's URL/ToS were **not verifiable from the dev sandbox** (network blocked) — verify before wiring anything.
  4. **The rep at the door.** Still the fastest single answer; `canvassOwnerManual` already captures it.
- **Trust-owned leads are counted separately in Stage 3** (`window._bbTrustNoHuman`) rather than sitting inside "missing owner" looking fixable — no parcel source will ever resolve them, and mixing them in makes owner coverage look worse than it is.

### The route didn't follow the rep — "next door is 5 miles away, opposite direction" (fixed 2026-08-07)
Reported while driving to an appointment. **Root cause:** `_canvassLoadLeads()` fetched the lead set **once**, around wherever the rep tapped "Get My Location", and the `watchPosition` handler only **re-sorted that same array** — it never re-queried. Drive 15 miles and the nearest door in the stale set is still back at the starting point. A `localStorage` `bbLeadsCache` restore could seed an even older set and mark it `ready`. This is *not* another unordered-`LIMIT` bug (the progressive box from 2026-08-04 was working correctly) — it's a set that was correct for a position the rep had left.
- **Fix:** the box query is factored into `_canvassFetchBox()` so the same code can re-run; `_canvassRefetchAround(lat,lng)` re-queries around the rep's current position when they pass `CV_REFETCH_MI` (1.5 mi) from the last fetch point and **merges by id** — never replaces, so knock_status and in-session card state survive and doors just outside the new box don't vanish mid-shift.
- **Heading:** `pos.coords.heading` is used **only** when `pos.coords.speed > CV_MOVING_MPS` (2.2 m/s ≈ 5 mph) — a stationary phone reports whatever the magnetometer feels like, and sorting a route by that noise is worse than not sorting at all. Doors more than `CV_BEHIND_DEG` (100°) off the heading are pushed down, except anything within 0.15 mi (a door 200 ft away is on the way regardless of which side it's on). With no reliable heading the sort is unchanged — pure nearest-first — so walking a street is unaffected.
- **One comparator, `_cvRouteCmp`**, now drives all three re-sorts (initial load, GPS tick, post-knock) so they can't drift: hot → unknocked-before-knocked → ahead-before-behind → SDCP-when-focused → distance.
- The GPS pill states "re-centered 2m ago · heading SW" and there's a manual **📍 Re-center here**, so a genuinely-far next door reads as sparse coverage rather than a bug.
- Verified in Chromium/Playwright (`scratchpad/test-doors.js`) by replaying a 14-mile drive between two lead clusters. **Gated both ways** — reverting the re-fetch reproduces the exact report: nearest door **14.3 mi away and still from the starting cluster**, vs **0.2 mi in the destination cluster** with the fix. ⚠️ The fixture puts **70** doors at the origin on purpose: with fewer than 60 the progressive box widens to 0.25° (~17 mi) on the cold load, reaches the destination cluster by itself, and the "route followed the rep" assertions pass whether or not the re-fetch works at all. Also asserts SDCP prioritise changes the order but not the count, heading disengages at 0.2 m/s, AI Notes is visible without expanding "Add Note", and Add-a-Door refuses a suffix/case-variant duplicate.
- ⚠️ `navigator.geolocation` is a **read-only accessor** in Chromium — a plain `navigator.geolocation = {...}` in a harness is silently dropped, sends `canvassInit` down the no-GPS path, and voids the whole test. Use `Object.defineProperty`.

- **Canvass "Next Door" showing a lead miles away (fixed 2026-08-04):** reported as "this doesn't look like the closest lead" — GPS active, 80 doors in the route, and the first one 6.3 miles out. **Third instance of the same root cause** as the Focus-ZIP and Phase-2 bugs below: a `limit` with **no `ORDER BY`**. Both canvass loaders (`_canvassLoadLeads` for reps, `adminCanvassInit` for admin) queried a single **~7-mile bounding box** and took `limit(80)` / `limit=100` — PostgREST returns an arbitrary slice of however many thousand leads sit in that box, and the client-side haversine sort can then only order *the arbitrary rows it received*. The genuinely-nearest doors were never fetched. Postgres can't `ORDER BY` a computed great-circle distance without PostGIS, so the fix is a **progressive box** (`CANVASS_BOX_STEPS = [0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25]` ≈ 1.4/3.5/7/17 mi) that starts tight and widens only when the near ring is genuinely thin (<60 leads), with `limit=1000` so each query returns the **whole box** rather than a sample of it; the distance sort then runs over complete data and the 80/100-row cap is applied **after** sorting. Also fixed in the same pass: the box used the same delta for latitude and longitude, but a degree of longitude shrinks with latitude (~0.84× at San Diego's 33°N), making the box ~19% too wide east-west and biasing it toward farther east/west leads — `_canvassGeoBox()` now divides the longitude delta by `cos(lat)`. ⚠️ **The widening must ACCUMULATE, not replace** (caught the same day, before the first version had been relied on): a wider box is geographically a superset, but it carries its own `limit=1000`, and a 17-mile box over San Diego holds far more than 1000 leads — so in a *low-density* area (fewer than 60 leads in the tight box) the widened query truncates arbitrarily and would discard the near doors the tight box already found, reintroducing the original bug exactly where it hurts most. ### Tracerfy auto-apply was silently discarding every result (fixed 2026-08-07)
Reported as "why is this not automated — why am I downloading the email file and uploading it?" It **was** automated end to end: Phase 3 of `bb-auto-pipeline-background.js` submits all no-contact leads to Tracerfy, saves `queue_id` to `pipeline_state` (`tracerfy_pending_queue`), and the next nightly run downloads the finished CSV and applies it. The apply step was the problem — it did `const leadId = row['lead_id'] || null; if (!leadId) continue;`, but **Tracerfy's advanced trace does not echo back custom columns**, so `lead_id` is never present and *every row was skipped*. The nightly run therefore spent real skip-trace credits, downloaded the results, and wrote nothing — which is why the manual paste path existed at all. `tracerfy-results.js` already knew this (its comment says so) and matched on address; the pipeline never got the same treatment. Phase 3 now falls back to **street+zip** using the same `normStreet`/`normZip` helpers as the dedup key, builds the lead index only when some row actually lacks an id, **drops street-only keys that collide across zips** rather than guessing, and writes only rows that resolved. ⚠️ Do not reintroduce a `lead_id`-only match on Tracerfy output.

- **Recovering the credits already spent — `netlify/functions/tracerfy-backfill.js` (2026-08-07):** every skip trace submitted before the fix above was paid for and discarded. It indexes leads and **only ever fills blanks** — a lead that already has a phone keeps it — so the run is idempotent and a re-run cannot undo a rep's correction. Ambiguous street-only keys are dropped as everywhere else. Verified in `scratchpad/test-backfill.js`: suffix-folded + float-zip match lands, a lead with an existing phone/owner gains only the missing email, the wrong-city lead is never written, a dry run writes nothing, and a second run after the data has landed produces zero writes.

### "Last pipeline run: 3d ago" while the pipeline was running nightly (fixed 2026-08-08)
Reported as a stalled nightly pipeline. It was not stalled — `pipeline_state` showed **`phase2_offset` updated 2026-08-07 09:45 while `last_run_at` still read 2026-08-05**, i.e. the run was doing real work and dying before the finish line. `bb-auto-pipeline-background` wrote `last_run_at` **once, after Phase 4**, so any run killed mid-flight (Netlify caps a background function at 15 min, and a 09:00 start still writing at 09:45 means more than one invocation is in play) left the portal reporting the last *completed* run.
- **Fix: `checkpoint(phase)` after every phase** — writes `last_run_at`, the new **`last_run_phase`**, and a partial `last_run_summary`. The final call passes `'complete'`. `checkpoint` swallows its own errors on purpose: a failed stamp must never kill a run.

- ⚠️ **This does NOT fix the underlying timeout** — it makes it visible and names the phase. If runs consistently stop at the same phase, that phase is over its time budget.

Gated both ways — stripping the per-phase calls leaves a single `complete` write and reproduces the stale-timestamp report exactly.

### Automatic shift clock (2026-08-07)
Per Dennis: *"automate the clock function so someone doesn't 'forget to click the buttons so my hours are off' type of excuse."* Hours are now derived from logged work rather than button presses, in two halves:
- **Auto-open (client):** `_bbAutoShiftOnActivity()` runs from `bbLogActivity` on any `dialer`/`door_knock` write. If the rep has no open `rep_shifts` row it inserts one (`source:'auto'`) stamped at that activity. If an open shift has been idle beyond `AUTO_SHIFT_IDLE_MIN` (45m) it is closed **at its last seen activity** and a fresh one opened. Deliberately **not awaited** — a disposition must never wait on, or fail because of, the clock, and the whole thing is try/catch'd so a missing `rep_shifts` table degrades silently.
- **Auto-close (server):** `netlify/functions/shift-autoclose.js`, scheduled `*/15 * * * *`. The client half only fires on the rep's *next* activity, which misses the common case — they shut the laptop and the shift stays open until tomorrow. The sweeper finds open shifts, looks up each rep's most recent `lead_activity` since the shift started, and if that is older than 45 minutes closes the shift **stamped at the last real activity, never at the moment the sweep ran** (otherwise idle time bills as work). A shift with no activity at all closes at its own `started_at` and contributes zero hours; anything beyond `MAX_SHIFT_HOURS` (16) is capped.
- The manual Start/End Shift buttons still work for a rep who wants to clock in before their first dial — nothing depends on them being pressed.
- Verified in `scratchpad/test-autoshift.js`: a logged dial opens exactly one `auto` shift, a second dial on a live shift opens none, and the Phase 3 fallback assertions above.

## Proposal builder — manual System Price was being clobbered (fixed 2026-08-06)
Reported on Erik Starkey's proposal: *"after I typed out the price and the squares it wasn't adding appropriately."* Root cause in `_propRecalcRedline()`: it recomputed **`build.price = lineSum + reroofCharge` unconditionally**, so a manually typed System Price was discarded on the very next recalc — and every square/charge/line-item edit calls `_propUpdateTotal` → `_propRecalcRedline`. Typing $28,000 then entering the roof area produced $30,250 (the catalog sum + reroof), silently throwing the $28,000 away. Catalog `default_price` values are **dealer cost**, so the manual System Price *is* the retail number — losing it is losing the whole quote.
- **Fix:** the manual price is stored as `build.basePrice` **excluding** add-on charges, with a `priceManual` flag (mirrors the existing `partPriceManual` pattern). `price = base + _propAddonCharge(build)`, where base is `lineSum` until the rep types one. The field therefore redisplays exactly what was typed, and squares/panels edited afterwards **add on top** instead of replacing it. `_propRestoreExisting` sets `priceManual` + a back-computed base so a reopened proposal shows the price actually sent, not one re-derived from the catalog.
- **⚠️ Anything quantity-driven must go through `_propAddonCharge`/`_propAddonCost`, never straight into `build.price`** — that is the mistake this fix undoes.
- **Remove & Reinstall card (new, same pass):** `RR_COST_PER_PANEL = 225` (Sub Sheet), `build.rr = { on, panels, chargePerPanel }`, handlers `_propToggleRR`/`_propRRPanels`/`_propRRCharge`, persisted as `opt.remove_reinstall` + a customer-visible `id:'remove_reinstall'` line item, and filtered back out on restore so it can't double-count. It is a **builder card, not a `proposal_catalog` row**, because it is quantity-driven per-panel pricing — the same shape as Reroof, and it needs no migration. **Deliberately no default retail** ($225 is dealer cost; inventing a charge would quietly set customer pricing) — the card warns while $/panel is 0.
- Verified in Chromium/Playwright (`scratchpad/test-proposal-pricing.js`) across six scenarios: price-then-squares (the reported order), squares-then-price, bumping squares after pricing, R&R alone, both add-ons plus the resulting 40% pool, and toggling an add-on back off. Gated both ways — restoring the unconditional recompute reproduces the $28,000 → $13,250 loss exactly.

## Photo Upload — job_photos schema fix + decoupled AI sync (2026-07-22)
Two stacked bugs behind reports of uploads being slow or failing outright ("All uploads failed — see error above" with nothing useful shown):

1. **Schema mismatch (the real root cause of outright failures):** `job_photos` only ever had legacy quoted mixed-case columns `"Path"`/`"Label"`, not the lowercase `path`/`label` every upload path (`uploadPhotos`, `handleUpload`, the storage-scan fallback) actually inserts. Confirmed with a direct `INSERT` test — `column "path" of relation "job_photos" does not exist` (42703). Every DB-record save for an uploaded photo was failing at this step: the file lands in Storage, but the row insert silently errors, so the photo never really saves. Fixed by `supabase/migrations/20260722_job_photos_schema_fix.sql` (renames `"Path"`→`path`, `"Label"`→`label`, non-destructive). **⚠️ Not yet applied — MCP writes were denied in the session that built this; run it manually in the SQL Editor, before `20260722_quoya_async_sync.sql` below.**
2. **AI call blocking the upload:** every photo ran a synchronous Claude vision call (`quoyaAutoAssess`/`quoyaAssessPhoto`) before the DB insert, so a multi-photo upload was as slow as N sequential API calls, and one flaky call could stall the batch. Also found and fixed in the same pass: the "all failed" fallback message check (`status.innerHTML.includes('&#9888;')`) never actually matched, because reading `.innerHTML` back after assigning an HTML entity like `&#9888;` returns the rendered character, not the entity text — so the real per-photo error always got silently overwritten by a generic "see error above" with nothing above it. Replaced with a plain tracked `hadError` boolean.

**New architecture — upload now saves instantly, AI categorization happens after:**
- `uploadPhotos()` (admin/tech) and `handleUpload()` (customer portal) insert the photo row immediately with `quoya_status='pending'` — no blocking AI call. The customer-portal upload cards already know their target category (Utility Bill/Inverter/Main Panel) from which card was tapped, so `label` is set to that immediately; only the quality-check pass (blurry/dark/cut-off) waits.
- **Manual "🔄 Sync with Quoya" button** — added to all three Photos panels (admin job detail, admin lead editor, tech/sales editor). Calls `netlify/functions/quoya-sync.js` (regular/sync function, ≤25 photos, scoped to one lead) and refreshes the grid with real results immediately.
- **Nightly sweep** — `netlify/functions/quoya-sync-background.js` (background function, scheduled `0 10 * * *` ≈3am PT in netlify.toml), processes up to 60 pending/failed photos per run across all leads. A large backlog just takes a few nights to fully drain rather than spiking Anthropic usage in one shot; anyone who needs a specific lead done sooner uses the manual button.
- Shared categorization logic lives in `netlify/functions/lib/quoya.js` (fetch pending rows → fetch image bytes from the saved Storage URL → call Anthropic vision directly, server-side → PATCH the row). Failed attempts retry up to 3 times (`quoya_attempts`) before being marked `skipped` so a permanently broken image URL doesn't keep burning tokens forever.
- **Net effect on AI spend:** roughly neutral to lower — same one Claude call per photo as before, just moved off the upload critical path; the retry cap and nightly batch cap actually bound worst-case cost tighter than the old unbounded per-upload retries.
- New `job_photos` columns (migration `20260722_quoya_async_sync.sql`, ✅ applied — verified 2026-08-08): `quoya_status` (pending|done|failed|skipped), `quoya_attempts`, `quoya_synced_at`, `expected_label`.
- `quoyaAutoAssess`/`quoyaAssessPhoto` (the old client-side blocking-call functions) were deleted from portal.html — no longer called from anywhere.
- Pending photos render with a "⏳ Pending"/"Analyzing…" badge in all three grids until synced.

- **Phase 1c rotation cursor (fixed 2026-08-01):** Phase 1c has no server-side GROUP BY, so it queries city-by-city per installer within a single 5-minute-per-run budget. The nested loop originally always started fresh at SunPower (6 name variants) × San Diego (highest-volume city) every night, so cities late in the list — Carlsbad (#9 of 16), Encinitas, National City, Vista, San Marcos, Lemon Grove — could go unqueried indefinitely even with the key set, since nothing ever advanced past the first installer/city. Root-caused from a "why does 92008 (Carlsbad) show 0 Black Box leads" report. Fixed by flattening `installer × city` into one ordered array and persisting a rotation cursor in `pipeline_state` (key `phase1c_cursor`, same pattern as `expansion_index`) — each run starts where the previous run left off and wraps around, so every combination gets queried within a few weeks instead of the same handful monopolizing every night. Check the Netlify function logs' `Phase 1c: ... covered N/TOTAL units (cursor X→Y)` line to confirm rotation is progressing.
